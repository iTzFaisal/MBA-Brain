# Chapter 11: Managing Team Performance

## Core Idea
Team-based PM must assess three levels together: individual task performance, individual contribution to team functioning, and whole-team performance. Measurement and rewards must fit team type, task complexity, membership configuration, process, outcomes, development, accountability, and stakeholder needs.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Team classification by task complexity and membership configuration**:
  - **Work/service teams**: Stable membership and routine work; use observable quality, quantity, cost, time, and peer measures.
  - **Project teams**: Temporary, cross-functional, often nonroutine; use milestones and process measures before completion.
  - **Network teams**: Dynamic, dispersed, boundary-spanning, and nonroutine; emphasize innovation, adaptability, flexibility, problem solving, and virtual coordination.
  - When to use: Classify the team before selecting measures or review timing.
- **Six-stage team PM process**: Prerequisites -> planning -> execution -> assessment -> review -> renewal/recontracting.
  - How: Add team mission, team and individual KSAs, team results, individual contribution, peer feedback, collective review, and revised team/individual plans.
- **Six principles for team measurement**: Ensure it is a real team; invest to measure; define goals; use multiple methods; measure process and outcomes; measure long-term change.
- **Me/We model**: Measure individual task performance, individual contextual contribution, and team performance as a whole.
  - When to use: Prevent both free riding and individual optimization at the expense of the team.
- **Four team dimensions**: Effectiveness, efficiency, learning and growth, and member satisfaction. Keep the overall measure set to about 15 or fewer.
- **Three team learning modes**: Adaptive, generative, and transformative learning.
- **Multi-stakeholder perspective for expatriate teams**: Consider local client, home company, and other on-site teams, plus home-country expectations.

## Key Concepts
- **Team**: Interdependent people sharing a common and valued goal or mission.
- **Routine task**: Well-defined work with readily assessed outcomes.
- **Nonroutine task**: Poorly specified work with uncertain or delayed outcomes.
- **Team accountability**: Individual responsibility for a meaningful stake in collective performance.
- **Peer evaluation**: Team members' assessment of one another and the team.
- **Free rider/slacker**: Member benefiting from team results without a commensurate contribution.
- **Self-funded reward**: Bonus funded by team savings or efficiency gains.
- **Multi-stakeholder perspective**: Evaluation from the relevant internal and external parties.

## Mental Models
- First ask **Is this really a team?** Shared goals and interdependence are prerequisites, not labels.
- Use **Me/We balance**: team-only measures hide free riders; individual-only measures undermine collaboration.
- Match measurement timing to task: frequent feedback for routine work, milestones for projects, and capability/process indicators for network work.
- Include **process plus outcome**; a final result alone cannot show how a team learned or correct itself.

## Anti-patterns
- **Individual-only measurement**: Encourages personal optimization over collective results.
- **Team-only measurement**: Conceals unequal contribution.
- **One-size-fits-all scorecard**: Ignores work, project, and network differences.
- **Final-only project evaluation**: Provides no opportunity for correction.
- **Outcome-only network evaluation**: Penalizes uncertain work and ignores capability.
- **Measurement overload**: Monitoring consumes execution time.
- **Uncontrollable feedback**: Teams cannot act on it and lose trust.
- **Rewarding cooperation for a speed objective**: Reinforces the wrong behavior.

## Worked Example
**Bose type-specific design:** Stable audio-equipment teams use daily peer observation and routine quality/cost/time measures. Temporary product-development teams use milestone, decision-making, collaboration, and learning measures. A global network team designing a Ferrari audio system uses virtual coordination, innovation, adaptability, and input from the local client, Bose, and other on-site teams. One governance framework can support all three, but measures and timing must differ.

## Key Takeaways
1. Verify genuine interdependence before adding team measures.
2. Measure individual task, individual team contribution, and whole-team performance.
3. Match measures and timing to team type and controllability.
4. Combine results, process, learning, and satisfaction.
5. Review team results collectively and individual contributions separately.
6. Make team rewards visible, contingent, reversible, broadly eligible, and behaviorally aligned.
