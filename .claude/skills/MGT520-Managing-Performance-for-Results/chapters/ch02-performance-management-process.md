# Chapter 2: Performance Management Process

## Core Idea
Performance management is a recurring, interdependent cycle: **prerequisites -> performance planning -> performance execution -> performance assessment -> performance review -> performance renewal and recontracting**. A strong appraisal cannot compensate for a broken link elsewhere in the cycle.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Performance Management Process**: Six stages that repeat as goals, jobs, and conditions change.
  - When to use: Design, diagnose, or repair an operating PM system.
  - How: Start with strategy and job requirements; define expectations; support execution; gather evidence; review; then reset the contract for the next cycle.
- **Job analysis and KSAs**: Identify tasks, outputs, processes, and the knowledge, skills, and abilities required.
  - When to use: Before defining criteria or evaluating a role.
  - How: Combine interviews, observation, questionnaires, incumbent input, and supervisor input. Rank tasks using frequency x criticality when prioritization is needed.
- **Results, behaviors, and development plan**: The three elements of performance planning.
  - When to use: Set complete expectations rather than only quotas.
  - How: For each key accountability, state the desired result, relevant behavior or competency, and development target.
- **Six-step productive performance review**: Identify specific good and poor behavior; solicit the employee's view; discuss consequences; connect strengths to problems; agree on an action plan; schedule follow-up and define evaluation criteria.
  - When to use: Conduct a review that leads to renewal rather than a retrospective judgment.

## Key Concepts
- **Prerequisites**: Knowledge of organizational/unit strategy and the job.
- **Job analysis**: Systematic identification of work activities, outputs, processes, and requirements.
- **KSAs**: Knowledge, skills, and abilities required for effective performance.
- **Key accountability**: A broad area of responsibility for producing results.
- **Objective**: A desired outcome; a **performance standard** specifies its acceptable level.
- **Competency**: A measurable cluster of KSAs influencing how results are achieved.
- **Performance execution**: Employee effort and manager support, observation, documentation, feedback, and resource provision.
- **Renewal and recontracting**: Revising goals, standards, accountabilities, and development in light of evidence and changing conditions.

## Mental Models
- Treat the system as a **chain**, not a form. Inspect the transition between stages when results are poor.
- Use **frequency x criticality** rather than frequency alone to prioritize work that matters.
- Separate **what** is achieved, **how** it is achieved, and **what capability** must be developed.

## Anti-patterns
- **No strategic or job prerequisite**: Criteria become generic and invalid.
- **Results-only planning**: Outcomes may reflect territory, resources, or market conditions beyond employee control.
- **Quota repetition instead of feedback**: Repeating a target does not explain progress or corrective action.
- **Past-only reviews**: Omitting present consequences and future goals disconnects review from renewal.
- **Disconnected stages**: Planning without execution, assessment without review, or review without renewal makes PM ceremonial.

## Worked Example
**Omega, Inc.:** A franchise introduced sales quotas and training, but customer service and order accuracy declined. Employees could not monitor progress, feedback merely repeated quotas, and there was no documented assessment or renewal. The repair is to add service behaviors and standards, provide progress data and coaching, conduct employee and manager assessments, hold a real review, and reset goals for the next cycle.

## Key Takeaways
1. Build from strategy and job analysis, not from an appraisal form.
2. Define both outcomes and the behaviors used to achieve them.
3. Make employees active owners of goals, evidence, feedback, and self-assessment.
4. Require managers to observe, document, support, and address problems early.
5. Use every review to renew the plan as evidence and conditions change.
