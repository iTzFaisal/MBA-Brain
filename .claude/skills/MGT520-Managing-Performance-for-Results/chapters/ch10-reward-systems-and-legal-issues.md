# Chapter 10: Reward Systems and Legal Issues

## Core Idea
Rewards shape motivation, behavior, retention, and culture only when they are meaningful, timely, contingent on valid performance, and aligned with strategy. Pay structures must also be internally defensible and legally sound through job-related standards, consistent procedures, documentation, employee voice, appeals, and disparity monitoring.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Traditional pay versus contingent pay (CP)**: Traditional pay follows position, title, and seniority; CP ties increases or variable pay wholly or partly to performance.
  - When to use: Select CP only when performance is clearly defined, measurable, and controllable.
- **Sorting effect**: CP attracts and retains high performers while encouraging lower performers to leave.
- **Expectancy-Instrumentality-Valence model**: `Motivation = Expectancy x Instrumentality x Valence`.
  - When to use: Audit whether effort can produce performance, performance can produce reward, and the reward is valued. If one link is absent, CP is unlikely to motivate.
- **Folly of rewarding A while hoping for B**: Employees optimize rewarded metrics even when they conflict with stated goals.
  - When to use: Stress-test incentives for gaming, short-termism, unsafe shortcuts, and teamwork damage.
- **Culture-strategy fit**: Traditional cultures may fit specific individual measures; involvement cultures may fit shared, skill-based, profit-sharing, or gain-sharing plans.
- **Job evaluation**: Ranking, classification, and point methods establish relative job worth and pay structure.
- **Broad banding**: Collapse many job classes into a small number of broad pay bands to allow flexible progression.
- **Legal discrimination distinction**: Performance-based differentiation can be legitimate; disparate treatment or adverse impact based on protected status is not.

## Key Concepts
- **Reward**: Anything increasing the likelihood of desirable behavior or results recurring.
- **Variable pay**: Reward not permanently added to base salary.
- **Gain sharing**: Group or organizational reward linked to profitability or savings.
- **Compensable factor**: Job characteristic the organization values and pays for.
- **Point method**: Weighted scoring of job factors to calculate job worth.
- **Adverse impact**: Unintentional system disadvantage to a protected group.
- **Disparate treatment**: Intentional differential treatment based on a protected or nonperformance factor.
- **Broad banding**: Flexible pay structure with few wide salary bands.

## Mental Models
- CP is a **motivation intervention**, not a substitute for knowledge, skill, ability, resources, or sound supervision.
- Choose incentives by the chain **strategy -> desired behavior/result -> controllable measure -> reward**.
- Use legal defensibility as a **process test**: job-related criteria, uniform application, notice, feedback, voice, appeal, documentation, and monitoring.

## Anti-patterns
- **Rewarding an easy metric**: It can undermine quality, teamwork, innovation, or long-term performance.
- **Insignificant reward differences**: Small distinctions are treated as ordinary pay.
- **Manager-driven easy goals or inflated ratings**: Rewards begin to drive evaluations rather than follow them.
- **Extrinsic rewards crowding out intrinsic motivation**: Money can reduce autonomy, challenge, and commitment when overused.
- **Seniority-only rewards**: Tenure becomes a stronger pay predictor than performance.
- **Late, undocumented, non-job-related, or biased reviews**: They increase negligence and discrimination risk.

## Worked Example
**Tractors, Inc.:** Reviews were late, standards were unspecified, employees had no input or copies, and attendance feedback referenced childcare. Women were also passed over for promotion despite stronger experience and job knowledge. A defensible redesign requires job-related standards, supervisor training, on-time reviews, employee comments and copies, multiple unbiased raters, formal appeals, specific evidence, and monitoring by protected group.

## Key Takeaways
1. Reward what the organization genuinely wants, not what is easiest to count.
2. Verify expectancy, instrumentality, and valence before increasing incentives.
3. Use financial and nonfinancial rewards together.
4. Establish internal equity through job evaluation and use broad bands only when flexibility is needed.
5. Build legal safeguards into design and process, not as a later review.
