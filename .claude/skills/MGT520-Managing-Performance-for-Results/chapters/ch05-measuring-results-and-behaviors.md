# Chapter 5: Measuring Results and Behaviors

## Core Idea
Translate strategy and job analysis into **accountabilities -> objectives -> performance standards**, then measure how results are achieved through competencies and observable behavioral indicators. Choose comparative or absolute systems based on whether the primary need is administrative sorting or developmental feedback.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Accountability-objective-standard sequence**: Accountabilities identify responsibility; objectives state desired outcomes; standards define the acceptable level.
  - When to use: Build results measures from a job description and strategic priorities.
  - How: Weight accountabilities by time, mission impact, and consequences of error. State quality, quantity, cost, and time where relevant.
- **SMART goals**: Specific, measurable, achievable, results-based, and time-specific.
  - When to use: Check the minimum quality of an objective.
  - How: Also test challenge, agreement, significance, priority, communication, flexibility, and limited number. Five to ten objectives per review period is usually manageable.
- **Competency-indicator model**: A competency is a measurable cluster of KSAs; indicators are observable behaviors that evidence it.
  - When to use: Measure how results are achieved.
- **Differentiating and threshold competencies**: Differentiating competencies separate superior from average performers; threshold competencies define minimum adequacy.
- **Comparative versus absolute systems**: Comparative systems rank employees against peers; absolute systems compare performance with a preset standard.
  - When to use: Prefer comparative systems for simple administrative sorting and absolute systems for specific developmental feedback.
- **Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales (BARS)**: Define dimensions, generate critical incidents, match incidents to dimensions, and anchor scale levels with concrete behavior.
  - When to use: Invest in behavioral specificity when feedback quality and defensibility matter.

## Key Concepts
- **Accountability**: Broad job responsibility for producing results.
- **Objective**: Important, measurable outcome supporting an accountability.
- **Performance standard**: Yardstick for judging achievement.
- **Critical incident**: Concrete example of especially effective or ineffective behavior.
- **Behavioral indicator**: Observable evidence of a competency.
- **Comparative system**: Employee-to-employee comparison.
- **Absolute system**: Comparison with a predefined standard.
- **Forced distribution**: Predetermined proportions placed into performance categories.

## Mental Models
- Build measures in the order **responsibility -> outcome -> threshold**; do not start with a favorite metric.
- Use **standards before ratings**: define satisfactory performance first, then minimum and outstanding levels if useful.
- Treat every metric as a behavioral intervention. Ask what employees will optimize, game, neglect, or sacrifice to improve the score.

## Anti-patterns
- **Narrow target fixation**: Numerical targets can encourage gaming and reduce service quality.
- **Vague or overloaded objectives**: Effort becomes diffuse and achievement becomes disputable.
- **Trait-based standards**: Personality labels are less concrete and defensible than job-related behavior.
- **Ranking as coaching**: Order does not reveal the behavioral gap or how to improve.
- **Uncritical forced distribution**: Fixed categories can damage teamwork and misclassify whole units.
- **Competency abstraction**: Rating a label without indicators turns measurement into impression.

## Worked Example
**Training Specialist:** The accountability is process leadership, representing about 40% of the role. The objective is to develop leadership programs within budget and schedule while improving executive readiness. The standard is a 20% increase in readiness scores by December 31 with costs below $70,000. The example shows why the accountability, outcome, deadline, and quality/cost constraint must be distinct.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start with job responsibilities and strategy.
2. Use the accountability-objective-standard chain for results.
3. Keep objectives few, meaningful, agreed, measurable, and time-bound.
4. Measure competencies through observable indicators.
5. Select ranking, absolute, BARS, or forced distribution only after checking purpose, culture, validity, and side effects.
