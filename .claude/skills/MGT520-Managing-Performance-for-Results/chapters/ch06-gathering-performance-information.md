# Chapter 6: Gathering Performance Information

## Core Idea
Good performance information requires purpose-fit forms, appropriate rating sources, regular conversations, transparent scoring, and motivated, trained raters. Form design alone cannot create accurate ratings if incentives favor distortion or sources lack firsthand knowledge.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Appraisal-form component audit**: Check employee information; accountabilities, objectives, and standards; competencies and indicators; achievements; developmental achievements and needs; stakeholder input; comments; and signatures.
  - When to use: Design or review a paper or electronic form.
- **Eight desirable form features**: Simplicity, relevancy, descriptiveness, adaptability, comprehensiveness, definitional clarity, communication, and time orientation.
  - When to use: Test practicality and clarity before deployment.
- **Mechanical versus judgmental overall scoring**: Mechanical scoring multiplies ratings by stated weights and combines them; judgmental scoring uses holistic discretion.
  - When to use: Prefer mechanical scoring for administrative decisions requiring transparency.
- **Six formal meetings**: System inauguration, self-appraisal, classical performance review, merit/salary review, development plan, and objective setting.
  - When to use: Separate conflicting topics where feasible so compensation does not eclipse feedback.
- **Source-fit model**: Supervisors, peers, subordinates, self, and customers should rate dimensions they can actually observe.
  - When to use: Build multi-source input without forcing all sources to agree.
- **Rater motivation model**: Accurate ratings compete with the expected benefits of leniency, severity, or central tendency.
  - When to use: Change incentives, accountability, and support when distortion is intentional.

## Key Concepts
- **Appraisal period**: Time span represented in the evaluation.
- **Stakeholder**: Person with firsthand knowledge of and impact from performance.
- **Mechanical score**: Transparent weighted combination of ratings.
- **Rating distortion**: Intentional or unintentional inflation or deflation.
- **Source-specific feedback**: Feedback preserved by supervisor, peer, subordinate, self, or customer source.
- **Developmental rating**: Information used to improve performance, generally less politically constrained than administrative ratings.
- **Context effect**: Rating difference caused by the situation recalled by the rater.
- **CATA**: Computer-aided text analysis that supplements ratings by counting dimension-related terms in comments.

## Mental Models
- Use a **source-assignment matrix**: rate only what the source can observe firsthand.
- Separate **developmental evidence** from **administrative scoring**; the same conversation can contain both, but their purposes should be explicit.
- Use **mechanical scoring for defensibility**, then use the review conversation for interpretation and action.

## Anti-patterns
- **One-size-fits-all form**: It overemphasizes results, competencies, or development for the wrong purpose.
- **Unclear definitions**: Different raters evaluate different constructs.
- **Annual-only cycle**: It delays feedback and produces surprises.
- **Unweighted judgmental score**: Personal priorities override published criteria.
- **Single-source dependence**: Every source has distinct blind spots.
- **Training without accountability**: Skills training cannot defeat a political reward for distorted ratings.

## Worked Example
**Anthony Carmello weighted score:** Competencies weighted .70 and .30 produced half-year subtotals of 3.7 and 2.7. Key results weighted .60 and .40 produced subtotals of 1.6 and 3.4. The transparent overall calculation is `(3.7 + 2.7 + 1.6 + 3.4) / 4 = 2.85`, placing the employee above average under the chapter's scale. Published weights prevent a supervisor from silently changing priorities.

## Key Takeaways
1. Design the form around purpose, not a generic template.
2. Define dimensions and require evidence before rating.
3. Use quarterly or semiannual formal reviews with ongoing informal discussion.
4. Match sources to observable dimensions and preserve source-specific feedback.
5. Make accurate ratings more defensible and rewarding than distortion.
