# Performance Management Cheatsheet

## Decision Rules

| If you see... | Do this | Because... |
|---|---|---|
| Annual form, no ongoing feedback or development | Call it appraisal, then add the PM cycle | PM is continuous and strategically aligned. |
| Employee cannot explain how work supports strategy | Cascade goals through unit plans and the job description | Strategy is not operational until work is traceable. |
| Poor performance | Diagnose knowledge, capability, motivation, HR practices, and context first | Training, incentives, and discipline solve different causes. |
| Outcome is delayed, noisy, or outside employee control | Emphasize observable behaviors and context | Results are evidence, not proof of individual performance. |
| You need results measures | Build accountability -> objective -> standard | Responsibility, outcome, and threshold must be distinct. |
| You need developmental feedback | Prefer absolute standards and behavioral indicators | Rankings show order, not how to improve. |
| Rating affects pay or promotion | Publish weights and use mechanical scoring | Transparency is more defensible than hidden discretion. |
| Different people observe different work | Use source-fit ratings; preserve source-specific feedback | Agreement across sources is not required. |
| Ratings are inflated or deflated intentionally | Change incentives and accountability | Awareness training does not fix motivational distortion. |
| Raters disagree about what a level means | Use FOR calibration and concrete anchors | Shared standards reduce cognitive error. |
| System is about to launch | Communicate, provide appeals, train, and pilot the full system | Completion is not evidence of acceptance or effectiveness. |
| Feedback identifies a development gap | Define activity + resource + deadline + standard + evidence | Course completion alone does not prove transfer. |
| Giving feedback | Describe facts before evaluation; be timely, specific, private, and actionable | Behavior can change; identity labels cannot guide action. |
| Considering contingent pay | Check expectancy x instrumentality x valence and side effects | A weak link or bad metric destroys motivation and fairness. |
| Group is called a team | Verify interdependence and a shared valued goal | Team measures are inappropriate for independent work. |
| Team measures are used | Balance Me/We and include process plus outcomes | Team-only scores hide free riders; individual-only scores harm collaboration. |

## Measurement Choice

| Condition | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Skilled employee, clear causal link, controllable output | Results |
| Delayed or ambiguous outcome, multiple external constraints | Behavior and context |
| Routine stable team | Frequent peer observation and quality/quantity/cost/time |
| Temporary project team | Milestones, process, collaboration, and learning |
| Dynamic network team | Innovation, adaptability, problem solving, and stakeholder evidence |
| Administrative decision | Standardized criteria, weighted score, documentation, and appeal |
| Developmental decision | Specific behavioral examples, employee voice, and follow-up |

## Smells
- Measure is easy to count but strategically irrelevant.
- A goal rewards individual output while the work requires cooperation.
- A review contains adjectives but no observable examples.
- Raters remember one recent or dramatic incident.
- A 360-degree system is tied to pay before trust and anonymity exist.
- A development plan has training dates but no transfer evidence.
- A team has one score and no individual accountability.
- Reward differences are too small to be meaningful.
- A process has no employee voice, copy of the review, or appeal path.
