# Leadership Patterns

## Process Test
**When to use** Decide whether an activity is leadership rather than authority, management, or coercion.
**How** Find an influencer, group, reciprocal influence, and common goal; then audit the power base and whether the purpose is shared.
**Trade-offs** Prevents title inflation, but does not establish effectiveness or ethical quality.

## Trait-Role Fit
**When to use** Select, coach, or build self-awareness for a particular role.
**How** Define situational demands; compare intelligence, confidence, determination, integrity, sociability, and relevant Big Five traits with self and observer ratings. Attach behavioral development actions.
**Trade-offs** Traits suggest fit or emergence, but no universal list predicts performance and visibility can hide integrity or context.

## Three-Skill Development
**When to use** A leader enters a new level or faces an ill-defined problem.
**How** Match technical skill to specialized work, human skill to cooperation, and conceptual skill to systems. Define the problem, gather perspectives, generate options, test constraints, and learn through assignments or mentoring.
**Trade-offs** Skills are developable, but the model is broad and outcomes also depend on experience and environment.

## Task/Relationship Audit
**When to use** Performance, morale, trust, or coordination is off track.
**How** Ask what structure the work needs and what relationship climate the group needs. Use follower feedback or a behavior instrument; add task, relationship, or both, then recheck outcomes.
**Trade-offs** High task plus high relationship is a hypothesis, not a universal 9,9 prescription; self-report can miss impact.

## SLII Matching
**When to use** A follower needs different help across goals or project phases.
**How** Diagnose competence and commitment: D1 -> S1 directing, D2 -> S2 coaching, D3 -> S3 supporting, D4 -> S4 delegating. Reassess after setbacks or new tasks.
**Trade-offs** Memorable and practical, but evidence is limited and group averages can underserve individuals.

## Path-Goal Obstacle Removal
**When to use** Effort is not becoming performance, rewards are not credible, or work is blocked.
**How** Check expectancy, instrumentality, and valence. Use directive behavior for missing structure, supportive for frustration, participative for control or input, and achievement-oriented for credible challenge; remove obstacles and clarify payoffs.
**Trade-offs** Flexible but complex and partly supported; removing every obstacle can create dependency.

## Leadership Making
**When to use** A team has unequal access to trust, information, opportunity, or influence.
**How** Offer every follower a genuine, voluntary chance to expand roles. Move from stranger to acquaintance to mature partnership through reciprocity, trust, respect, obligation, support, and recognition; compare both sides' ratings.
**Trade-offs** High-quality LMX has positive associations, but a permanent clique creates unfairness and unrewarded extra-role labor.

## Four Is
**When to use** Compliance is insufficient and people need meaning, growth, innovation, or collective commitment.
**How** Model values, frame shared purpose, challenge assumptions, and coach individuals: idealized influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualized consideration. Add contingent reward for clarity.
**Trade-offs** Charisma can become pseudotransformational when dissent is punished or the vision serves the leader.

## Authentic Four Components
**When to use** Distrust, inconsistency, or a leader's blind spot is limiting relationships.
**How** Build self-awareness; act from internal morals; seek and weigh dissent; disclose motives and limits appropriately. Use life-story reflection, feedback, and the ALQ as prompts.
**Trade-offs** Authenticity is not unfiltered disclosure or competence; links to outcomes remain incomplete.

## Servant Behaviors
**When to use** Follower growth, autonomy, well-being, and community impact are central.
**How** Apply serve-first, then conceptualize, heal, put followers first, grow them, act ethically, empower, and create community value. Check receptivity, least-privileged effects, boundaries, goals, and accountability.
**Trade-offs** Caring can become paternalistic intrusion; service must not become passive or utopian.

## Adaptive Six Behaviors
**When to use** Values, identity, roles, habits, or behavior must change rather than receive a known technical fix.
**How** Get on the balcony; identify the challenge; regulate distress; maintain disciplined attention; give work back to people; protect voices from below. Hold productive tension.
**Trade-offs** Too much heat overwhelms and too much safety preserves avoidance; the model is abstract and lightly supported.

## Psychodynamic Detective Scan
**When to use** Repeated irrationality, anxiety, scapegoating, idealization, or collusion resists ordinary explanations.
**How** Ask what wish, fear, anxiety, or relationship history makes the pattern rational. Separate expected from actual reactions; scan Bion's assumptions and defenses; use 360 feedback and coaching while checking structure.
**Trade-offs** Reveals hidden dynamics, but hypotheses are not diagnoses and can obscure systems or excuse behavior.

## Ethical Five-Principle Audit
**When to use** A high-power decision affects rights, burdens, safety, or trust.
**How** Test respect, service, justice, honesty, and community. Add consequence, duty, and virtue lenses; inspect the toxic triangle; explain criteria and trade-offs.
**Trade-offs** Exposes blind spots but cannot resolve every conflict; good outcomes do not excuse rights violations.

## Hill Team Loop
**When to use** An interdependent team is missing performance, development, or both.
**How** Monitor and build a mental model; choose monitor or act, task or relational, internal or external; intervene, then re-monitor. Use shared leadership for needed expertise.
**Trade-offs** Supplies variety rather than a script; timing, diagnosis, life stage, and overcontrol matter.

## Labyrinth Audit
**When to use** Leadership advancement or selection shows a gender gap.
**How** Map barriers at entry, development, promotion, and elite selection: assignments, mentoring, visibility, work-home structure, negotiation, sponsorship, and criteria. Use structured evidence, diverse reviewers, and multi-level remedies.
**Trade-offs** Improves on the glass-ceiling story, but research is Western-centered and incomplete on intersectionality; do not shift the burden to women.

## GLOBE Cultural Calibration
**When to use** Leading across national, organizational, or virtual cultural boundaries.
**How** Treat nine GLOBE dimensions and cluster profiles as hypotheses. Ask followers about participation, autonomy, status, directness, humane support, and face; adapt expression while retaining trust, integrity, fairness, and competence.
**Trade-offs** Reduces ethnocentrism, but GLOBE is perception-centered and clusters do not describe every individual.
