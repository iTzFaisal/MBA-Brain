# Chapter 10: Servant Leadership
## Core Idea
Servant leadership reverses the usual power sequence: a person begins with a desire to serve, then makes a conscious choice to lead. Greenleaf's test is whether people served grow healthier, wiser, freer, more autonomous, and more likely to serve others, especially whether the least privileged benefit rather than become further deprived. The approach is behavioral and follower-centered: leaders listen, empathize, empower, develop people, behave ethically, and connect organizational work to community welfare. Its distinctive strength is making altruism and shared power central. Its cautions are the paradoxical label, disagreement about core dimensions, a sometimes utopian or moralistic prescription, and unclear evidence for why conceptualizing belongs as a defining servant behavior.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Greenleaf's servant-as-leader framework (Greenleaf, 1970, 1972, 1977):** Use the "serve first" test before claiming servant leadership. Identify followers' highest-priority needs, place their growth above the leader's self-interest, shift authority rather than dominate, and check effects on the least privileged and the wider community. Community, consensus, interdependence, and social responsibility are part of the framework.
- **Spears's ten characteristics (Spears, 2002):** Use as a practical behavior checklist: listening, empathy, healing, awareness, persuasion rather than coercion, conceptualization, foresight, stewardship, commitment to the growth of people, and building community. The characteristics translate Greenleaf's philosophy into observable development goals.
- **Liden, Wayne, Zhao, and Henderson model, extended by Liden et al. (2014):** Diagnose the chain of antecedents, behaviors, and outcomes. Context and culture, leader attributes, and follower receptivity shape the process; the seven core behaviors are conceptualizing, emotional healing, putting followers first, helping followers grow and succeed, behaving ethically, empowering, and creating value for the community. Expected outcomes are follower performance and growth, organizational performance, and societal impact. Receptivity moderates results: followers who want guidance and empowerment respond more positively than those who experience it as intrusion or micromanagement.
- **Servant Leadership Questionnaire (SLQ):** The 28-item Liden et al. (2008) measure assesses the seven behaviors. Use multi-source feedback to identify development priorities, not as evidence that every context will reward servant leadership.

## Key Concepts
- Serve first: service is the starting motive; leadership follows conscious choice.
- Follower receptivity: followers' desire for support, development, and empowerment affects outcomes.
- Putting followers first: prioritize followers' concerns and success over the leader's interests.
- Emotional healing: attend to personal pain and well-being and provide supportive presence.
- Empowering: share power so followers can decide, act independently, and become self-sufficient.
- Stewardship: hold the organization and leadership role in trust for society's greater good.
- Conceptualizing: understand mission, complexity, and long-term direction while serving daily needs.
- Ripple effect: cared-for and empowered followers may themselves become servant leaders.
- Organizational citizenship behavior: discretionary contributions beyond formal job duties that support the organization.

## Mental Models
- Use "followers first" as a decision test: whose growth, agency, and well-being improve, and what happens to the least privileged?
- Think service is not passivity; preserve vision and accountability while replacing coercive control with persuasion, trust, and shared authority.
- Use the antecedent-behavior-outcome chain before acting: culture, leader disposition, and follower receptivity determine how a caring behavior will land.
- Think in ripple effects: develop people so they can serve customers, peers, communities, and future followers without dependence on the original leader.

## Anti-patterns
- Paternalistic helping: intrusive "care" that ignores follower autonomy is experienced as micromanagement, not service.
- Coercive altruism: forcing people to accept the leader's preferred help contradicts persuasion and shared power.
- Moralistic self-sacrifice: serving others while neglecting goals, production, boundaries, or accountability makes the approach utopian and unsustainable.
- Performative community service: volunteering or a caring slogan does not compensate for unethical decisions or failure to develop followers.
- One-size-fits-all servant leadership: no consensus defines every dimension, and conceptualizing's status as a behavior remains contested.

## Worked Example
- **Southwest Airlines:** A delayed flight left a mother worried that her toddler would go hungry. A flight attendant volunteered to leave the plane, obtained food for the child, and brought meals for four other children, indirectly serving the whole cabin. The behavior reflected Southwest's Golden Rule and its "others first" culture. Cofounder Herb Kelleher resisted hierarchy, hired for substance and willingness to speak honestly, and used collaborative management. Colleen Barrett, who rose from his legal secretary to president, formalized an employee-first model: care for employees, then employees care for passengers, while shareholders benefit. Humor, equality, empowerment, and follower receptivity produced a serving culture associated in the case with 35 consecutive profitable years.

## Key Takeaways
1. Servant leadership is a paradoxical influence process built on service before status.
2. Greenleaf supplies the moral test; Spears makes the philosophy observable; Liden et al. organize it into a measurable model.
3. The seven behaviors should develop follower capability, autonomy, ethics, and community value.
4. Context, leader attributes, and follower receptivity condition whether servant behavior helps or frustrates.
5. Use the SLQ and feedback for development while recognizing unresolved theoretical and causal questions.
