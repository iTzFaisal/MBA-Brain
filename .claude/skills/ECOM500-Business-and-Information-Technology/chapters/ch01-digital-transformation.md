# Chapter 1: Digital Transformation Disrupts Companies, Competition, and Careers Locally and Globally

## Core Idea
Digital transformation is a business-model and process redesign, not merely the installation of newer technology. Use IT to deliver speed, convenience, personalization, reliable data, and continuous access while improving performance, growth, sustainability, and the customer experience.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **On-demand economy vs. sharing economy**: Use the on-demand model when customers value immediate provisioning of a product or service. Use the sharing model when private individuals share goods or services, usually through an online intermediary, either free or for a fee. Both depend on a platform-based business model: hardware, software, networks, authentication, tracking, ordering, and payment.
- **Disruptive Digital Business Models**: Choose the model that matches how customers receive value and how the firm captures revenue. The named forms are **Subscription**, **Freemium**, **Free**, **Access-over-Ownership**, **Experience**, **On-Demand**, and **Ecosystem**. For example, a subscription sells continuing access, access-over-ownership sells temporary use, on-demand fulfills work for customers who lack time, and an ecosystem makes a suite of interdependent products more valuable together.
- **IT-Business Objectives**: Use these six objectives to test whether an IT investment has business value: **product development**, **stakeholder integration**, **process improvement**, **cost efficiencies**, **competitive advantage**, and **globalization**. Link each project to at least one objective and a measurable outcome.
- **Business Process Reengineering (BPR)**: Use BPR when incremental improvement cannot meet customer expectations or when an obsolete process is full of handoffs, documents, and delays. Its slogan is **"Don't automate, obliterate!"**. The eight phases are: (1) develop vision and objectives, (2) understand existing processes, (3) identify process for redesign, (4) identify change levers, (5) implement new process, (6) make new process operational, (7) evaluate new process, and (8) perform continuous improvement.
- **Social-Mobile-Analytics-Cloud (SMAC) Model**: Use SMAC to design an integrated digital experience. Social connects people and creates influence, mobile follows users everywhere, analytics supplies relevant information, and cloud provides continuous access to storage, applications, and services. Together they connect the cloud core, social channels, and mobile or wearable edge.
- **Technology Mega Trends**: Scan **connectivity**, **big data and data analytics**, **artificial intelligence and robotics**, **digitization**, and sensor-enabled **machine-to-machine (M2M) technology/Internet of Things (IoT)** when evaluating a threat or opportunity. These trends create new products, services, processes, and careers.

## Key Concepts
- **Business model**: The core strategy for generating revenue and profit, including products, target market, costs, and plans.
- **Digital business model**: A business model that uses websites, social media, mobile devices, and related digital technology to meet goals.
- **Customer experience**: The cumulative impact of a customer's interactions with an organization.
- **Technology platform/technology stack**: The hardware, software, networks, and data capabilities that support connected transactions.
- **Business process**: A series of steps that converts inputs into outputs; inputs, activities, and deliverables are its three basic components.
- **Business process management (BPM)**: Methods, tools, and technology for mapping, supporting, and continuously improving processes.
- **Competitive advantage**: Differentiating by charging less and creating and delivering better-quality products or services than competitors.
- **Digitization**: Transforming activity or information into a digital form that can be collected, stored, searched, and analyzed efficiently.
- **Informed user**: A person knowledgeable about information systems and IT who can recommend and use technology productively and responsibly.

## Mental Models
- Use the **customer expectation loop**: time, convenience, and "my way" raise the minimum acceptable digital experience; loyalty follows the firm that anticipates needs.
- Think of a process as **inputs -> activities -> deliverables**. Improve the whole flow, not one department's local metric.
- Treat software and data as strategic capabilities, not only as an IT cost center. Speed of learning and adaptation can be the advantage.

## Anti-patterns
- **Automating an obsolete process**: It preserves unnecessary steps, documents, and handoffs instead of removing them.
- **Local optimization**: A faster call may create repeat calls and lower quality, as in the Dell support example.
- **Ignoring platform requirements**: A useful website cannot compensate when customers expect mobile apps, secure payment, location awareness, and real-time service.
- **Treating digital disruption as only technical**: Regulation, culture, worker status, customer trust, and business-model economics determine whether the technology creates value.

## Worked Example
Uber and Airbnb illustrate two sides of platform disruption. Uber connects passengers with drivers through location-aware apps, social authentication, and cashless payment: it is primarily on-demand. Airbnb gives hosts a platform to monetize spare rooms or homes: it is access-over-ownership and sharing. Global expansion required localization, foreign currencies, translations, local regulations, and local sign-up methods. When COVID-19 reduced rides and travel, Uber extended the model through Uber Eats while Airbnb broadened accommodation offerings. The lesson is to use a digital platform as a resilient coordination mechanism, then reconfigure the value proposition when demand changes.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start digital transformation with the customer and business model, not with a technology purchase.
2. Use BPR to eliminate non-value-added work before automating what remains.
3. Evaluate IT against product development, stakeholder integration, process improvement, cost, advantage, and globalization.
4. SMAC works through convergence; social, mobile, analytics, and cloud should reinforce one another.
5. Digitization creates value only when people can interpret data and act on it responsibly.
