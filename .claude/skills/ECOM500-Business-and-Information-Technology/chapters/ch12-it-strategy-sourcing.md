# Chapter 12: IT Strategy, Sourcing, and Strategic Technology Trends

## Core Idea
IT strategy is a set of deliberate bets about capabilities, investments, services, sourcing, and technology change. Align it with business objectives at strategic, tactical, and operational levels, then use competitive analysis, balanced measures, vendor governance, and structured technology scanning to turn uncertainty into better odds of success.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Five activities for Business-IT alignment**: Use to connect IT investment to business value.
  - Secure senior-management commitment to IT planning; make the CIO a member of senior management; understand IT and corporate planning; build a shared culture with effective communication; and link business and IT plans at strategic, tactical, and operational levels.
- **The Open Group Architecture Framework (TOGAF)**: Use to organize enterprise architecture and requirements before major development or integration.
  - How: Move through Preliminary Framework and Principles, A Architecture Vision, B Business Architecture, C Information System Architectures, D Technology Architecture, E Opportunities and Solutions, F Migration Planning, G Implementation Governance, and H Architecture Change Management.
- **Porter's Competitive Forces Model**: Use to assess industry pressure before choosing an IT-enabled competitive strategy.
  - How: Evaluate threat of new entrants, supplier power, buyer power, threat of substitute products or services, and competitive rivalry; then select cost leadership, differentiation, or focus, supported by technology.
- **Porter's Value Chain Model**: Use to find where IT can create or increase value.
  - How: Examine five primary activities: inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, sales and marketing, and servicing. Examine four support activities: accounting/legal/finance, HR management, product and technology development, and procurement.
- **The Five Components of an IT Strategy**: Use as a completeness check: applications, integration, infrastructure, services, and sourcing strategy.
- **Balanced Scorecard (BSC)**: Use after planning to connect strategy to targets and review performance.
  - How: Measure financial, customer, internal business process, and innovation/learning/growth perspectives. Set SMART objectives, metrics, targets, actions, and feedback loops rather than relying only on lagging financial indicators.
- **ITIL 4 Service Value System (SVS)**: Use to manage IT services as co-created business value.
  - How: Apply its four dimensions - organizations and people; information and technology; partners and suppliers; value streams and processes - with the seven guiding principles and six value-chain activities: plan, improve, engage, design and transition, obtain/build, deliver and support.
- **Five-Phase Outsourcing Life Cycle**: Use for any major outsourced service.
  - How: Assess and document the opportunity; evaluate and select a service provider; develop and negotiate the contract; transition the service; and manage the service and SLA continuously.
- **Five Steps to Technology Scanning**: Use before investing in an emerging technology.
  - How: Conduct a general technology search, map technology to business needs, build systems models, analyze customer requirements, and analyze specific technologies with potential.

## Key Concepts
- **Business-IT alignment**: Applying IT appropriately and in a timely way consistent with business strategy, goals, and needs.
- **Value driver**: A business activity or factor that enhances customer value and future revenue or profit.
- **IT strategic plan**: A formal document guiding IT support, investment, operation, maintenance, and disposal.
- **Long-range IT plan**: Three-to-five-year direction, requirements, sourcing, budgets, and time frames.
- **Medium-term IT plan**: Multi-year project and applications portfolio aligned with the long-range plan.
- **Tactical IT plan**: Current-year budgets, schedules, and daily operational resources.
- **IT service management (ITSM)**: Managing IT services, change, incidents, assets, and relationships to co-create business value.
- **Service-level agreement (SLA)**: Contract defining services, service standards, responsibilities, and consequences for nonperformance.
- **Proof of concept (POC)**: A measurable vendor demonstration or limited trial used to test whether a product works as promised.
- **Tech scanning**: A structured search and evaluation of emerging technologies against business and customer needs.

## Mental Models
- **Strategy is a portfolio of bets**: Use a clear vision and hard choices to increase the odds of success; do not pretend uncertainty can be eliminated.
- **Balance leading and lagging indicators**: Financial results confirm what happened; customer, process, and learning measures help manage what will happen.
- **Source capabilities, not just labor**: Compare in-house, onshore, offshore, and cloud options by control, skills, privacy, integration, resilience, and value.
- **A contract is an operating control**: A vague SLA or RFP transfers practical control to the supplier. Specify scope, architecture, governance, risk, pricing, assets, transition, and measures before signing.

## Anti-patterns
- **Reactive IT investment**: Patches and one-off purchases create redundant, incompatible systems that fail to support the business strategy.
- **Tactical cloud adoption**: Incrementally stitching together cloud apps without a coordinated architecture recreates ERP and legacy-integration problems.
- **Choosing the lowest TCO only**: Capability, reliability, data control, vendor stability, and service quality may matter more than price.
- **Unrealistic SLAs**: Overpromised response times create conflict and hidden cost; define priorities and achievable service levels.
- **Skipping POC, transition, or governance**: A successful vendor demo is not evidence of a successful production relationship.

## Worked Example
**San Diego County's outsourcing journey.** The County outsourced its entire IT function to replace fragmented infrastructure across 50-plus departments. The first contract had an overly ambitious scope, unrealistic service levels, costly administrative friction, and a late ERP rollout. The next contract used fewer, more realistic SLAs, separated priority-one and priority-two incidents, offered role-appropriate desktop resources, and addressed disaster recovery and continuity. Over time, 17 help desks became one, five e-mail systems became one, 800-plus distributed servers fell to 520 in one data center, and IT silos disappeared. The lesson is to treat outsourcing as a long-term, governed transformation rather than a single procurement event.

## Key Takeaways
1. Align business and IT plans through leadership, communication, shared culture, and multilevel links.
2. Use Porter's forces and value chain to connect technology choices to competitive pressure and value creation.
3. Maintain all five IT strategy components and all three planning horizons.
4. Use SWOT early, BSC late, and recurring reviews throughout the planning cycle.
5. Govern vendors with an explicit RFP, POC, contract, SLA, transition plan, and ongoing relationship management.
6. Scan broadly, map technology to needs, model impact, test customer value, and invest only when the case is credible.
