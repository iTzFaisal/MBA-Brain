# Chapter 13: Systems Development, IT Service Management and Project, Program and Portfolio Management

## Core Idea
Successful systems and projects begin with a real business need, explicit feasibility and requirements, and a method suited to uncertainty. Use SDLC and PPPM discipline to define deliverables, balance scope-time-cost-quality, expose bad news early, control change, and close with reusable lessons. Technology skill, or PMTQ, is now part of project competence.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Five-stage Systems Development Life Cycle (SDLC)**: Use to move from a business need to a supported system.
  - **Systems planning** tests technical, economic, legal/organizational, and behavioral feasibility and produces a Project Plan. Economic feasibility uses time value of money, ROI, NPV, and breakeven analysis: ROI is net income attributable to a project divided by the average cost of resources invested; NPV is project benefits less costs after allowing for cost of capital and time value; breakeven is where cumulative cash flow equals the investment. **Systems analysis** gathers requirements and produces a Systems Proposal. **Systems design** creates logical and physical specifications. **Implementation and testing** deploys, tests, and trains users to produce a working system. **Support and maintenance** audits, updates, and supports the system under an SLA.
- **Four "Ps" of systems conversion**: Use when replacing a live system.
  - **Plunge** is cheapest and riskiest; **parallel** runs old and new together and is safest but most expensive; **pilot** introduces the system to one location or group; **phased** introduces modules in stages.
- **Systems development methodologies**: Use the method that fits requirements volatility, risk, users, and organizational culture.
  - **Waterfall Model** is sequential and predictive; use for small, short projects with stable requirements. **Object-Oriented Analysis and Design** is iterative and emphasizes modularity, reusability, UML, and use cases. **Agile Systems Development Methodology** delivers small increments early through collaboration, iterations, and feedback. **DevOps** integrates development, testing, operations, and support to minimize disruption and improve release reliability.
- **Project, Program and Portfolio Management (PPPM)**: Use the hierarchy to allocate resources at the right level.
  - Project management governs one temporary endeavor at the operational level. Program management coordinates related projects at the tactical level. Portfolio management centrally selects and sequences proposed, current, and future projects at the strategic level.
- **Project Triple Constraint**: Use to negotiate changes and protect feasibility.
  - Manage **scope**, **time**, and **cost** together while protecting quality. A change to one constraint normally changes the other two.
- **Five Phases of the Project Management Life Cycle**: Use for any project: **Initiating**, **Planning**, **Execution**, **Monitoring/Controlling**, and **Closing**.
- **Ten Knowledge Areas of Project Management**: Use as a control checklist: cost, scope, time, quality, integration, communication, human resources, procurement, risk, and stakeholders.
- **Integrated change control**: Use whenever scope, schedule, cost, quality, or corrective action changes. Document approved and rejected changes, updated plans and scope, and validated repairs.

## Key Concepts
- **Business need**: A gap between the current state and business goals.
- **Feasibility study**: Preliminary assessment of technical, economic, legal/organizational, and behavioral feasibility.
- **Deliverable**: A measurable, tangible, verifiable result produced by a project or stage.
- **Use-case model**: Use-case diagram plus description showing actors, events, system responses, flows, requirements, and assumptions.
- **Scrum**: Agile framework using self-organizing teams, 7- to 30-day sprints, a sprint backlog, daily Scrum, and transparency, inspection, and adaptation.
- **Work breakdown structure (WBS)**: Ordered list of all project work and task dependencies.
- **Risk register**: Known risks, sources, estimates, responses, and responsible people.
- **Baseline**: Formally reviewed and accepted project plan used to measure variance.
- **Critical path**: Shortest path through all required tasks; delay to a critical task delays the project unless compensated.
- **Project management technology quotient (PMTQ)**: Ability to adapt, manage, and integrate technology for project or organizational needs.

## Mental Models
- **Project, program, portfolio**: Manage tasks inside a project, dependencies across related projects in a program, and strategic value across the portfolio.
- **Triangle plus quality**: Scope, time, and cost constrain one another; quality is the outcome that exposes whether the trade-off was acceptable.
- **Baseline plus feedback**: Plan a reference point, measure actual performance, and correct variances before they become expensive.
- **Bad news early is a control**: Candid feedback protects the project; silence converts small variances into runaway failure.

## Anti-patterns
- **Skipping feasibility and requirements**: Denver's baggage project ignored experts, underestimated complexity, excluded stakeholders, and accepted an impossible schedule.
- **Using Waterfall for volatile requirements**: A sequential method makes late discovery of user needs expensive.
- **Separating development, testing, and operations**: Handoffs create bottlenecks and systems that work technically but fail user needs; DevOps addresses this gap.
- **Starting without a baseline, milestones, WBS, or risk register**: The team cannot distinguish progress from activity or control variance.
- **Allowing scope creep without compensating time, cost, or resources**: Small changes accumulate into a runaway project.
- **Using sunk cost to justify continuation**: Decide from the future value and future cost of continuing, not money already spent.
- **Cutting training, testing, or change management first**: Lower short-term cost produces poor quality and low user acceptance.

## Worked Example
**VELCO East Avenue Loop Project.** VELCO needed a redundant electrical feed but faced public opposition, environmental commitments, a $35.7 million estimate, and limited internal project expertise. PM Solutions began with public and regulator engagement, then established a realistic estimate, demanding schedule, PMO governance, and risk mitigation. When an island access problem arose, the team used an ice bridge rather than a standard bridge and saved $460,000. The project finished on time and $6 million under budget, increased customer satisfaction by 20%, and gained approval six months earlier than comparable projects. Early stakeholder work and disciplined controls reduced both political and execution risk.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start with a business need and use feasibility analysis to make a defensible go/no-go decision.
2. Use the SDLC as an iterative governance frame, then choose Waterfall, O-O, Agile, or DevOps from project conditions.
3. Use PPPM to align individual delivery, program coordination, and portfolio investment.
4. Protect the triple constraint with a WBS, milestones, baseline, risk register, and integrated change control.
5. Monitor execution continuously, publish candid status, and protect the critical path.
6. Close formally with acceptance, archived documents, released resources, and a lessons-learned postmortem.
7. Build PMTQ through curiosity, inclusive leadership, future-proof talent, and appropriate project technology.
