# Economics Reasoning Cheatsheet

## Fast Sequence
1. Name the scarce resource, decision maker, alternatives, and time horizon.
2. State what is held fixed; separate positive prediction from normative judgment.
3. Compare marginal benefit with marginal cost and identify changed incentives.
4. Test distribution, external effects, information, market power, and implementation lags.

## Default Decision Rules

| Question | Default rule |
|---|---|
| Do one more? | Continue while `MB >= MC`; stop at `MB = MC`. |
| Cost something fully? | Include the best forgone use of money, time, labor, and owned assets. |
| Who should specialize? | Lower opportunity cost, not necessarily absolute advantage. |
| Curve changed? | Own price = movement; any other determinant = shift. |
| Is a market efficient? | Check no external benefits/costs, perfect information, and perfect competition. |
| Consumer's next dollar? | Move it to the larger `MU/P`. |
| Competitive firm's output? | `P = MC`; operate short-run if `P >= AVC`; long-run survival requires `P >= ATC`. |
| Monopoly output? | `MR = MC`, then read `P` from demand. |
| Pollution level? | Abate until `MB of abatement = MC of abatement`, not automatically to zero. |
| Search longer? | Continue only while expected savings from another search exceed its opportunity cost. |

## Core Formulas
- `Y = C + I + G + NX`; GDP counts current final production once.
- `GDP deflator = nominal GDP / real GDP * 100`; `CPI = basket cost / base basket cost * 100`.
- `unemployment rate = unemployed / labor force * 100`; `labor force = employed + unemployed`.
- `PV = K / (1+i)^t`; `real interest ~= nominal interest - expected inflation`.
- `MPC + MPS = 1`; spending multiplier `= 1/(1-MPC)`; tax multiplier `= -MPC/(1-MPC)`.
- `M * V = P * y`; growth rates: money growth + velocity growth = inflation + real-output growth.
- `MRP = marginal product of labor * output price`; hire while `MRP >= wage`.
- `economic profit = revenue - explicit cost - implicit cost`.
- `total surplus = consumer surplus + producer surplus`.
- `HHI = sum of squared market shares`; `years to double ~= 70 / growth rate (%)`.

## Trade-Off Tables

| Intervention or structure | Immediate result | Main cost or caution |
|---|---|---|
| Price ceiling below equilibrium | Shortage, lower quantity supplied | Excluded high-value trades, queues, nonprice allocation |
| Price floor above equilibrium | Surplus, lower quantity demanded | Unsold output, unemployment, or purchase obligations |
| Tax | Price wedge, lower quantity, revenue | Deadweight loss; burden follows relative elasticity |
| Tariff or quota | Higher domestic price, protected output | Consumer loss, lost trade; quota creates rents |
| Spending stimulus below potential | AD and output rise in short run | Multiplier leakage, lags, later inflation or crowding out |
| Monetary expansion | Rates fall, AD rises in short run | Expectations, exchange-rate effects, long-run price increase |
| Pollution tax | Flexible, least-cost abatement incentives | Emissions quantity is uncertain |
| Tradable permits | Emissions cap, trading finds abatement allocation | Permit price is uncertain; allocation can create rents |
| Natural-monopoly regulation | `P = ATC` lowers price and raises output | Weak cost-control incentives; do not duplicate infrastructure blindly |

## Tells and Smells
- Price and quantity move together: suspect a demand shift. They move oppositely: suspect a supply shift, unless both curves moved.
- A falling unemployment rate with falling participation: do not call it unambiguous labor-market improvement.
- Nominal wage or GDP rises: check inflation before calling it a real gain.
- Output falls while prices rise: test for an adverse supply shock, not only weak demand.
- A policy is called "free": find the tax, debt, time, opportunity cost, or displaced use.
- A market price is an "average": ask whether low-quality sellers or high-risk buyers are selectively entering.
- A firm's price is above MC: identify market power, differentiation, or an external cost before calling it inefficient.
- A firm is losing money: compare `P` with `AVC` today, not `ATC`; fixed costs may be sunk.
- More firms appear but price barely falls: examine substitutability, entry squeeze, and strategic interaction.
- A fixed peg is defended by reserve loss or foreign-currency debt: test for currency-crisis vulnerability.
