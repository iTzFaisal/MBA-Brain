# Glossary

Significant terms only; chapter references point to the on-demand chapter files.

**Aggregate demand** - Total planned demand for currently produced real GDP: `C + I + G + NX` (Ch 9, 15)

**Adverse selection** - Hidden information before a transaction changes who enters the market, often attracting higher-risk or lower-quality participants (Ch 29)

**Comparative advantage** - Ability to produce a good at lower opportunity cost than another producer (Ch 3, 18)

**Consumer surplus** - Willingness to pay minus the price actually paid (Ch 21, 22)

**CPI** - Cost of a representative fixed consumer basket relative to a base year, used to measure price change and purchasing power (Ch 6)

**Crowding out** - Reduction in private spending, especially investment, caused by increased government demand or borrowing (Ch 7, 10, 15, 17)

**Deadweight loss** - Surplus destroyed when a policy or market power prevents mutually beneficial transactions (Ch 21, 25, 31)

**Demand** - Quantities consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices, holding other determinants fixed (Ch 4)

**Diminishing returns** - Output rises at a decreasing rate when one input increases while another input remains fixed (Ch 2, 7, 23)

**Economic profit** - Revenue minus explicit and implicit opportunity costs (Ch 23, 24)

**Elasticity** - Percentage responsiveness of one variable to a percentage change in another (Ch 20)

**Equilibrium** - Outcome where opposing market forces are balanced; in a product market, quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (Ch 4, 11)

**External cost** - Cost imposed by an activity on people outside the transaction; social cost equals private plus external cost (Ch 31)

**Fiscal policy** - Government changes in purchases, taxes, and transfers intended to influence GDP, employment, or inflation (Ch 10, 17)

**GDP** - Market value of all final goods and services produced within an economy in a given period (Ch 5)

**Inflation** - Percentage increase in a general price index, not merely a high price level (Ch 6, 16)

**Marginal benefit** - Additional benefit from a small increase in an activity (Ch 1, 2)

**Marginal cost** - Additional cost from a small increase in an activity (Ch 1, 2, 23)

**Marginal revenue** - Change in total revenue from selling one more unit (Ch 24, 25)

**Market failure** - Market outcome that does not maximize social surplus because of externalities, information gaps, market power, or related problems (Ch 3, 21, 29, 30, 31)

**Moral hazard** - Hidden action after a contract changes incentives and increases risk or cost (Ch 29)

**Natural monopoly** - Market in which economies of scale make one supplier cheaper than duplicate facilities (Ch 25, 28)

**Natural rate of unemployment** - Frictional plus structural unemployment when cyclical unemployment is absent (Ch 6, 7, 16)

**Nominal value** - Face-dollar amount; real value adjusts it for purchasing power or prices (Ch 2, 5, 6)

**Opportunity cost** - Best alternative sacrificed to obtain something (Ch 1, 2, 3)

**Perfect competition** - Benchmark with many buyers and sellers, homogeneous output, free entry, and price-taking firms (Ch 24)

**Present value** - Maximum amount worth paying today for a future payment, discounted at the relevant interest rate (Ch 12, 14)

**Producer surplus** - Price received minus the producer's marginal cost or willingness to accept (Ch 21)

**Public good** - Good that is nonrival in consumption and nonexcludable in use (Ch 30)

**Purchasing power** - Quantity of goods and services that money or income can buy (Ch 2, 6)

**Quantity equation** - `M * V = P * y`, linking money, velocity, prices, and real output (Ch 16)

**Real interest rate** - Nominal interest rate minus actual or expected inflation, depending on the decision (Ch 12, 16)

**Supply** - Quantities firms are willing and able to sell at different prices, holding other determinants fixed (Ch 4)

**Tariff** - Tax on imported goods that raises domestic price and creates government revenue (Ch 18, 21)

**Unemployment rate** - Unemployed people as a percentage of the labor force; active job search is required for official counting (Ch 6)

**Utility** - Satisfaction generated by consuming a bundle of goods and services (Ch 22)

**Voluntary exchange** - Freely chosen trade expected to make both parties better off (Ch 2, 3)
