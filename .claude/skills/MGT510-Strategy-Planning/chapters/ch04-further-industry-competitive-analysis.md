# Chapter 4: Further Topics in Industry and Competitive Analysis

## Core Idea
Five Forces is a baseline, not a complete strategy. Extend it when innovation changes industry structure, when complements and platforms co-create value, when rivals are interdependent, or when broad industries hide different segments and strategic groups.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Hypercompetition**: Use when innovation and competitive moves rapidly create and erode advantage. Track how rivals build, attack, and replace positions rather than assuming stable structure.
- **Winner-take-all analysis**: Test for positive feedback and network externalities. Identify whether users, complementors, talent, or capital gravitate toward the leader and create critical mass.
- **Six Forces / complements analysis**: Add complementors to the five forces. Map how complements increase customer value, who controls bottlenecks, and who captures the resulting value.
- **Business ecosystem**: Map users, suppliers, complementors, platforms, technologies, institutions, and regulators. Locate dependencies and bottlenecks before choosing a position.
- **Value creation and value capture in ecosystems**: Estimate the value lost if the firm exits and the value it could contribute elsewhere; then improve both value created and bargaining position.
- **Business Model Canvas**: Map infrastructure (resources, activities, partners), value proposition, customer segments, channels, relationships, revenue, costs, and profit logic; use it to test alternative configurations.
- **Game theory and competitor analysis**: Identify players, options, payoffs, sequence, information, and likely equilibria. Use the model to change incentives, timing, commitments, or information; do not pretend it predicts complex markets precisely.
- **Porter's four-part competitor framework**: Predict a rival from its current strategy, objectives, assumptions, and resources/capabilities.
- **Segmentation analysis**: Choose strategically important dimensions, map segments, assess each segment's forces and KSFs, then choose specialist or broad coverage.
- **Strategic group analysis**: Plot firms on important strategic dimensions and analyze rivalry within and between groups, including barriers to mobility.

## Key Concepts
- **Complementor**: A participant whose product or service increases the value of the focal offering.
- **Business ecosystem**: A network of organizations and technologies that jointly create and capture value.
- **Critical mass**: Participation level at which positive feedback sustains adoption.
- **Lock-in**: Switching or coordination costs that keep participants with a system.
- **Bottleneck**: A scarce or weak component that constrains ecosystem performance.
- **Coopetition**: Simultaneous cooperation and competition among interdependent firms.
- **Nash equilibrium**: A set of choices where no player benefits from changing alone.
- **Barrier to mobility**: An obstacle preventing a firm from moving between strategic groups or segments.

## Mental Models
- **Industry as ecosystem**: Value depends on adjacent participants, not only direct rivals.
- **Bottleneck leverage**: Control of a constraining component can determine value capture.
- **Change the game**: Alter payoffs, timing, information, or commitments instead of only changing a move.
- **Conditional competitor**: Rival behavior is a function of strategy, objectives, assumptions, and capabilities.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating Five Forces as a static verdict or ignoring creative destruction.
- Applying ordinary industry analysis to a platform market without testing network effects.
- Confusing ecosystem value creation with the firm's share of value.
- Using threats as deterrence without credibility or commitment.
- Applying game theory with unrealistic assumptions and false precision.
- Collecting competitor information illegally or segmenting with excessive, correlated variables.

## Worked Example
A meal-delivery platform may look unattractive under ordinary rivalry analysis, but a two-sided network lens changes the decision. The platform maps diners, restaurants, couriers, payment providers, mapping technology, and regulators; identifies courier density as a bottleneck; launches in one district to build critical mass; and uses a Business Model Canvas to connect convenience, restaurant access, commissions, subscriptions, and delivery costs. Competitor analysis then predicts incumbent discounting from its objectives and financial resources.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use Five Forces as a starting point, then test dynamics and adjacent participants.
2. Analyze both ecosystem value creation and value capture.
3. Use game theory to structure interdependence and find ways to reshape rivalry.
4. Use competitor analysis when behavioral evidence is more useful than formal modeling.
5. Segment markets and map strategic groups to identify distinct positions and KSFs.
