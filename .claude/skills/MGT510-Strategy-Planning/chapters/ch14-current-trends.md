# Chapter 14: Current Trends in Strategic Management

## Core Idea
Digitization, global interconnectedness, social legitimacy pressures, and rapid competitive change are reshaping strategy. Grant presents the chapter's ideas as emerging concepts and experimental approaches, not proven replacements for industry analysis, resource analysis, or economies-of-scope logic.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Digital industry characteristics**: Map complementary products, ecosystems, network externalities, fast-cycle innovation, easy imitation, and fungible IT resources when digitization changes boundaries and value chains.
- **Systemic risk: black swans and gray rhinos**: Identify dependencies and amplification channels. Separate rare, difficult-to-predict shocks from foreseeable but neglected threats.
- **Legitimacy, license to operate, and shared value**: Treat social expectations, inequality, environmental harm, human rights, and corruption as strategic inputs; connect responsibility to value creation.
- **Real options**: Use growth, abandonment, and flexibility options under uncertainty, irreversible investment, and unstable industry structure.
- **Transient advantage and multidexterity**: Build several reinforcing advantages rather than rely on one core capability; integrate efficiency, differentiation, innovation, responsiveness, and adaptability.
- **Informal organization**: Use modular, team-based, project-based structures, standardized interfaces, mutual adjustment, and horizontal collaboration for rapid learning.
- **Self-tuning enterprise**: Combine organizational identity, broad information access, and relationships so local units can respond without waiting for hierarchy.
- **Changing managerial roles**: Treat leaders as social architects, constitution writers, and entrepreneurs of meaning. Emphasize purpose, ethics, emotional intelligence, humility, and resolve as algorithms automate operational decisions.

## Key Concepts
- **Fourth Industrial Revolution**: Sensors, big data, AI, and 5G extending digitization.
- **Ecosystem strategy**: Value co-created across linked suppliers of complements and services.
- **Fungibility**: Digital resources can be redeployed across applications and industries.
- **Transient advantage**: Advantage must be continually recreated because imitation and change erode it.
- **Strategic integration**: Multiple goals and capabilities create value only when they reinforce one another.
- **Legitimacy**: Organizational survival depends on alignment with society's values and expectations.
- **Distributed intelligence**: Information and relationships throughout the organization coordinate action.

## Mental Models
- **Opportunity portfolio**: Strategy should create future choices, not only commit to one forecast.
- **Core coherence, peripheral flexibility**: Preserve identity and purpose while changing products, structures, and practices.
- **Integration over accumulation**: More capabilities do not create advantage without reinforcing fit.
- **Human judgment at the boundary**: As algorithms handle operations, managers contribute meaning, ethics, creativity, and social integration.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating emerging approaches as validated replacements for established tools.
- Building the entire strategy around one capability, business model, or dominant logic.
- Measuring attractiveness only by current profit and ignoring future options.
- Adding goals and capabilities without integrating them around customer experience.
- Responding to complexity with more hierarchy and compliance.
- Assuming self-organization works without identity, information, and relationships.
- Treating predictable systemic threats as surprises.

## Worked Example
A regional appliance maker facing smart-home platforms, global entrants, climate regulation, and supply-chain shocks maps an ecosystem rather than an isolated appliance industry. It runs small connected-product pilots, retains abandonment and flexibility options, and builds several transient advantages: reliable hardware, software updates, service, repairability, and circularity. Cross-functional teams coordinate through interfaces, while managers preserve a trusted identity and set ethical boundaries for data use.

## Key Takeaways
1. Digital competition favors ecosystems, platforms, speed, timing, and fungible capabilities.
2. Interconnected systems make local events strategically consequential.
3. Legitimacy and shared value broaden the inputs and purpose of strategy.
4. Under uncertainty, create options before making irreversible commitments.
5. Emerging approaches should augment, not discard, rigorous conventional analysis.
