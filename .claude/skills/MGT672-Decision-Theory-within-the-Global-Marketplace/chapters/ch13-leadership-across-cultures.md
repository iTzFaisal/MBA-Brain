# Chapter 13: Leadership Across Cultures

## Core Idea

Leadership is the process of influencing people to direct effort toward goals. International leaders need both managerial execution and earned legitimacy, but the behaviors that signal competence, care, authority, and trust vary across cultures. Use universal principles such as integrity, vision, encouragement, and team integration, then adapt expression, participation, rewards, communication, and decision rights to the context.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Manager-Leader Paradigm:** Managers maintain stability, follow objectives, oversee complexity, and do things right; leaders create vision, motivate, handle ambiguity, earn respect, and do the right things. Use the contrast to diagnose a role's current versus future demands, while developing the combined manager-leader rather than assuming the titles are interchangeable.
- **Theories X, Y, and Z:** **Theory X** assumes people avoid work, lack ambition, prefer direction, and require control and punishment. **Theory Y** assumes work can be natural, people can self-direct, seek responsibility, and possess widely distributed creativity. **Theory Z** combines intrinsic motivation with teamwork, shared responsibility, broad business learning, and long-term employment security. Use these as assumptions to test, not labels to stereotype a country.
- **Leadership Behaviors and Styles:** **Authoritarian leadership** is work-centered, one-way, and decision-centered at the top; use it for crisis or low readiness with caution. **Paternalistic leadership** combines demanding work with protection, security, and loyalty. **Participative leadership** combines task and people concerns through consultation, joint decisions, and delegation. The **Managerial Grid** locates styles from 1,1 (low task, low people) through 9,1 authoritarian, 1,9 people-centered, 9,5 paternalistic, and 9,9 participative.
- **Bass's Full-Range Model and the 4 I's:** **Transformational leaders** use **idealized influence**, **inspirational motivation**, **intellectual stimulation**, and **individualized consideration**. Compare them with transactional leaders, including contingent reward, active management-by-exception (MBE-A), passive management-by-exception (MBE-P), and laissez-faire (LF). Use the model to choose the right blend of vision, exchange, correction, and nonintervention; transformational leadership has the strongest cross-cultural potential but still needs local expression.
- **GLOBE Culturally Endorsed Implicit Leadership (CLT) Dimensions:** Diagnose expectations through **charismatic/value-based**, **team-oriented**, **participative**, **humane-oriented**, **autonomous**, and **self-protective** leadership. GLOBE also finds broadly desirable attributes such as integrity, foresight, positivity, motivation, communication, and team integration, and broadly undesirable attributes such as irritability, malevolence, and ruthlessness.
- **Positive and Responsible Leadership:** **Positive organizational scholarship (POS)** links enablers, altruistic motivations, and outcomes such as vitality, meaningfulness, exhilaration, and high-quality relationships. **Responsible global leadership** combines values-based leadership, ethical decision making, and quality stakeholder relationships. Servant leadership adds listening, empathy, healing, awareness, persuasion, conceptualization, foresight, stewardship, growth of others, and community building.

## Key Concepts (5-10)

- European patterns vary: British and Scandinavian managers tend toward participation; French and German managers often emphasize work-centered authority, though codetermination increases worker voice.
- Japanese leadership is paternalistic yet can be highly participative; ambiguous goals encourage subordinates to anticipate contingencies and preserve harmony.
- Japanese **variety amplification** expands alternatives and uncertainty; U.S. **variety reduction** narrows alternatives and focuses action.
- China's New Generation leaders score higher on individualism and lower on collectivism and Confucianism than older generations.
- Middle Eastern patterns have traditionally been centralized and personal, but education, technology, and Gulf business ties are increasing participation among younger managers.
- Indian managers often combine high people and high task orientation; Latin American styles range from authoritarian to participative and shift with globalization.
- Effective leadership prototypes differ by cultural cluster, industry, gender expectations, and communication norms.
- Global teams develop through expectations, self-awareness, trust, and collaboration.

## Mental Models

- **Universal core, local expression:** Integrity, purpose, confidence, and team integration travel better than tone, hierarchy, reward, or meeting behavior.
- **Style x follower x situation:** High achievers may respond to a creative, supportive 9,9 style; low achievers may initially need clear task direction. Do not infer effectiveness from style alone.
- **Authority is granted twice:** The organization appoints a manager; followers confer leadership through respect and credible action.
- **Responsible influence:** Vision without ethics is manipulation; leadership is durable when it improves stakeholder relationships and follower capability.

## Anti-patterns

- Exporting a home-country leadership style and treating local adaptation as weakness.
- Confusing title, hierarchy, or charisma with earned leadership.
- Assuming participative leadership is always best, even when followers need clarity, capability, or crisis direction.
- Reading emotional restraint as indifference or vocal intensity as aggression without checking cultural meaning.
- Using transformational language while rewarding short-term self-interest and ignoring stakeholders.

## Worked Example

Roche's Business Perspectives Program develops global leaders through two years and four six-month rotations across sectors and countries. Participants arrange rotations with executive mentors, work outside cultural comfort zones, gain exposure to developing economies and local, regional, and global levels, and receive simulations, lectures, feedback, coaching, and senior-leader networking. The design builds both management capability and leadership judgment: participants learn to adapt behavior, interpret culturally different situations, and carry a common purpose without assuming one operating style fits every location.

## Key Takeaways

1. International leadership requires a manager-leader capable of execution, vision, adaptation, and earned trust.
2. Theories X, Y, and Z reveal assumptions about people; styles should follow evidence and context.
3. Bass's 4 I's and GLOBE's CLT dimensions provide useful universal and culturally endorsed lenses.
4. Culture shapes the form of effective leadership, especially participation, emotion, communication, and rewards.
5. Responsible global leadership makes ethics and stakeholder relationships part of performance, not an optional add-on.
