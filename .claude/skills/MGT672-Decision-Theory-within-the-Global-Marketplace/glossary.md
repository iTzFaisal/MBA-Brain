# Glossary

**Act of state doctrine** - Courts may not question a foreign government's public acts within its own territory (Ch 2).

**Alliance** - Cooperative arrangement in which firms share selected resources, risks, knowledge, or market access (Ch 9, Ch 10).

**Anticipatory adjustment** - Expatriate adjustment before departure through selection, prior experience, and cross-cultural preparation (Ch 14).

**Bargaining power** - The ability to influence a host government's or partner's choices through distinctive value and alternatives (Ch 10).

**Base of the pyramid (BOP) strategy** - A business model redesigned for affordable, accessible offerings to low-income consumers (Ch 8).

**BATNA** - Best alternative to a negotiated agreement; the walk-away reference point (Ch 7).

**Civil or code law** - Legal system built primarily on comprehensive written statutes and codes (Ch 2).

**Collectivism** - Priority given to group or society goals over individual preference (Ch 2, Ch 4).

**Common law** - Legal system developed through judicial precedent as well as statutes (Ch 2).

**Corporate social responsibility (CSR)** - Voluntary action benefiting society beyond legal duties and direct firm interests (Ch 3).

**Country-specific advantage (CSA)** - Location advantage from a country's resources, labor, institutions, or skills (Ch 8).

**Cultural assimilator** - Validated culture-pair incidents with alternative interpretations used for intercultural learning (Ch 14).

**Cultural relativism** - View that ethical standards arise from the culture that created them (Ch 3).

**Decentralization** - Delegation of decision authority to lower or local levels with capability and accountability (Ch 9, Ch 11).

**Distributive negotiation** - Bargaining that allocates a fixed value in a primarily win-lose exchange (Ch 7).

**Economic system** - Arrangement governing ownership, prices, resource allocation, and state-market roles (Ch 1).

**Ethnocentric predisposition** - Parent-country values and interests guide cross-border decisions (Ch 5).

**Expectancy theory** - Motivation depends on effort-performance belief, performance-outcome belief, and outcome value (Ch 12).

**Foreign direct investment (FDI)** - Investment in property, plant, or equipment in another country (Ch 1).

**Four strategic imperatives** - Economic, political, quality, and administrative-coordination lenses for international strategy (Ch 8).

**Geocentric predisposition** - Integrated global-systems view using the best ideas and people across countries (Ch 5).

**Global integration** - Centralization and coordination used to gain scale, experience, and worldwide consistency (Ch 5, Ch 8).

**Globalization** - Social, political, economic, cultural, and technological integration among countries (Ch 1).

**GLOBE** - Cross-cultural research framework linking nine cultural dimensions to organizational practices and leadership (Ch 4, Ch 13).

**High-context communication** - Meaning is carried substantially by relationships, status, timing, shared knowledge, and implicit signals (Ch 5, Ch 7).

**Hofstede's cultural dimensions** - Power distance, uncertainty avoidance, individualism, masculinity, time orientation, and indulgence (Ch 4).

**Human rights** - Basic claims including life, liberty, expression, and freedom from slavery, torture, and discrimination (Ch 3).

**Hyper norms** - Universal or near-universal moral boundaries used by ISCT to limit local variation (Ch 3).

**Individualism** - Priority given to individual freedom and self-directed economic or political activity (Ch 2, Ch 4).

**Integrative negotiation** - Cooperation that combines interests to create value and support durable mutual gain (Ch 7).

**Integrative Social Contracts Theory (ISCT)** - Framework allowing legitimate local norms within limits set by hyper norms (Ch 3).

**Internationalization** - A firm's process of crossing national and cultural borders through sales, operations, ownership, or management (Ch 1).

**Joint venture (JV)** - Venture in which partners invest, share control, and combine complementary capabilities (Ch 9, Ch 10).

**Kantian ethics** - Tests whether a decision's guiding maxim could be applied as a universal law (Ch 3).

**Key success factor (KSF)** - Capability or condition necessary to compete effectively in a market niche (Ch 8).

**Local responsiveness** - Adaptation to local customers, institutions, regulations, and cultural expectations (Ch 5, Ch 8).

**Macro and micro political risk** - Broad risks affecting all firms versus sector- or firm-specific policy constraints (Ch 10).

**Market economy** - System in which private ownership and demand guide resource allocation while the state supports competition (Ch 1).

**Mixed economy** - System combining private markets with government planning, regulation, or strategic ownership (Ch 1).

**Multinational corporation (MNC)** - Firm with operations in multiple countries, international sales, and mixed nationalities among managers or owners (Ch 1).

**National treatment** - Principle that foreign firms receive treatment no less favorable than domestic firms under relevant rules (Ch 2).

**Offshoring** - Performing an activity in an offshore location, whether internally or through a supplier (Ch 1).

**Political risk** - Unanticipated likelihood that host or other government policy will constrain foreign-investment value (Ch 10).

**Polycentric predisposition** - Host-country culture shapes decisions separately in each country (Ch 5).

**Power distance** - Extent to which less powerful members accept unequal power distribution (Ch 4).

**Principled negotiation** - Separates people from problems, focuses on interests, creates options, and uses objective criteria (Ch 7).

**Regionalization** - Persistence of distinct regional economic groupings within growing global linkages (Ch 1).

**Repatriation** - Return and reintegration of an international assignee into the home organization (Ch 14).

**Return on investment (ROI)** - Profit divided by assets, interpreted in light of currency, tax, and transfer-pricing effects (Ch 11).

**Schein's organizational culture model** - Diagnostic model moving from observable artifacts to values and deep shared assumptions (Ch 6).

**Sovereignty** - Government's right to rule itself and control public authority in its territory (Ch 2).

**Total quality management (TQM)** - Organization-wide quality system using empowerment, training, supplier involvement, measurement, and continuous improvement (Ch 11).

**Transfer, operational, and ownership-control risks** - Political-risk categories covering movement constraints, operating rules, and ownership or asset control (Ch 10).

**Triple bottom line** - Simultaneous evaluation of people, planet, and profits (Ch 3).

**Utilitarianism** - Chooses the action producing the greatest net good for everyone affected (Ch 3).

**Wholly owned subsidiary** - Fully controlled foreign operation, built greenfield or acquired, with concentrated investment exposure (Ch 9).
