# International Management Cheatsheet

## If/Then Rules

| If... | Then prefer... | Because / watch... |
|---|---|---|
| Scale and coordination pressure are high, local adaptation low | Global strategy | Standardize the homogeneous core; do not ignore regulation. |
| Local tastes, institutions, or legitimacy dominate | Multi-domestic strategy | Adapt product, message, authority, and HR. |
| Both pressures are high | Transnational strategy | Integrate production and differentiate locally; coordination is the cost. |
| Both pressures are low | International strategy | Transfer core competencies with limited adaptation. |
| Market uncertainty is high and commitment should stay low | Export or license | Learn first; accept less control and possible IP leakage. |
| Control and proprietary coordination dominate | Wholly owned subsidiary | Price capital concentration, political exposure, and exit difficulty. |
| Local knowledge, legitimacy, or risk sharing dominates | Alliance or JV | Define authority, IP, targets, and exit before trust is tested. |
| Speed and assets matter | Acquisition | Speed does not remove cultural integration work. |
| Scale, interdependence, technology, or brand protection dominates | Centralize | Give local units clear execution rights. |
| Distance, differentiation, local speed, and capable managers dominate | Decentralize | Keep boundaries, information, and accountability explicit. |

## Fast Screens

- **Risk**: Ask macro or micro? Then transfer, operational, or ownership-control? Risk rises when host dependence is high and the firm's contribution is replaceable.
- **Culture**: Use country models to form questions, not labels. Check values versus practices and confirm with the individual.
- **Communication**: If a nod, silence, or written yes is ambiguous, paraphrase, ask for the next action, and verify behavior.
- **Negotiation**: If interests can be combined, use integrative options; never concede without reciprocity and never accept less than the BATNA.
- **Ethics**: If legal but unsafe, degrading, corrupt, or environmentally damaging, apply the moral screen and hyper norms; include the supply chain.
- **Motivation**: If dissatisfaction is visible, repair hygiene factors first. If stability exists but effort is low, add recognition, responsibility, growth, meaningful work, and feedback.
- **Leadership**: Keep integrity, vision, encouragement, and team integration; adapt participation, emotion, hierarchy, rewards, and communication.
- **Global HR**: If a technical star lacks adaptability, language, health, leadership, or family readiness, do not assign yet. Make whole before using incentives.

## Defaults, Tells, and Smells

- Use monthly indirect control plus periodic direct contact; numbers are signals, not verdicts.
- Treat a fast-growing market as unproven until institutions, infrastructure, currency, and political exposure pass review.
- A single home-country template, country score, or short briefing is a smell of parochialism or simplification.
- "Local custom" used to justify a payment is an anticorruption red flag.
- A signed contract, WTO membership, or formal code is not a control against policy reversal or misconduct.
- Too many reporting lines and no clear owner signal a failing matrix; clarify authority before adding coordination.
- A multicultural team earns its cost when innovation, customer insight, or complex judgment matters; routine work may not justify it.
- Cultural-assimilator programs commonly narrow 150-200 incidents to 75-100 validated incidents.
- An expatriate assignment can approach US$1 million over three years; screen family and adjustment risk before selection.
