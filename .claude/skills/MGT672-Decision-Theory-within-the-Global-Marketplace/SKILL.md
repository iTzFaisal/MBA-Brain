## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

- **Globalization, internationalization, and regionalization**: Separate global flows from a firm's cross-border activity. Use a global-regional-local lens: global integration creates possibilities, regional blocs set access conditions, and local institutions determine execution. Screen growth separately from investability. Use market exchange rates for current conversion and purchasing power parity (PPP) for local purchasing power.
- **Economic systems**: In market, command, and mixed economies, predict who owns resources, sets prices, allocates supply, and constrains managerial discretion. A fast-growing market is not automatically an investable market.
- **Environment and ethics**: Before entry, scan the political-system dimensions, four legal foundations, jurisdiction, national treatment, most-favored-nation treatment, regulation, technology, and resilience. Apply a compliance floor plus a moral screen: use Kantian, Aristotelian virtue, utilitarian, and Eastern lenses; use Integrative Social Contracts Theory (ISCT) to respect legitimate local norms without violating hyper norms. Test the **triple bottom line** of people, planet, and profits through the supply chain.
- **Culture as a decision aid**: Diagnose artifacts, norms/values, and basic assumptions with the three-layer model. Use Hofstede's six dimensions, Trompenaars's relationship orientations, and GLOBE's nine dimensions to form questions about authority, uncertainty, rewards, time, status, and emotion. Distinguish GLOBE values from practices. Validate the person and situation; never substitute a country score for observation.
- **Global core, local execution**: Balance the globalization imperative with national responsiveness. Choose among ethnocentric, polycentric, regiocentric, and geocentric predispositions deliberately. Standardize where scale, a homogeneous niche, or proprietary coordination creates value; adapt where local meaning, regulation, relationships, or subsidiary capability creates value.
- **Communication and negotiation**: In high-context settings, trust, status, timing, and relationships carry meaning; in low-context settings, explicit words and written agreements carry more of it. Check interpretation through feedback and nonverbal signals. For durable relationships, use the five negotiation stages, principled negotiation, interest-based options, objective criteria, reciprocity, and a protected BATNA.
- **Strategy fit**: Scan the external environment, assess resources and key success factors (KSFs), compare country-specific advantages (CSAs) with firm-specific advantages (FSAs), set goals, implement, and rescan. Use the four strategic imperatives (economic, political, quality, and administrative coordination) and the Global Integration vs. National Responsiveness Matrix. A transnational strategy fits high pressure on both dimensions, but only if global synergy can fund local variation.
- **Entry and structure**: Use the control-investment-local knowledge triangle. Export or license to limit commitment; use a wholly owned subsidiary for control; use an alliance or joint venture for knowledge, risk sharing, and legitimacy; use acquisition for speed only with an integration plan. Let strategy determine global product, area, functional, mixed, or transnational network structure. In a JV, define authority, scope, knowledge protection, performance, and exit before launch.
- **Political risk and government relations**: Separate macro from micro risk, then score transfer, operational, and ownership-control exposure against the investment's general and special nature. Protect bargaining power with distinctive, renewing capabilities. Become local through sourcing, employment, R&D, and relationships without becoming captive; preserve portable knowledge and alternatives. Treat alliances as systems that create value, claim value, and eventually may need an orderly exit.
- **Decision and control**: Run a decision-control loop: choose, act, measure, compare, correct. Centralize scale, proprietary technology, strategic interdependence, and brand protection; decentralize local adaptation when distance, differentiation, and capable managers make local knowledge decisive. Pair scalable indirect controls with periodic direct contact. Use ROI as a signal, adjusted for currency, tax, transfer pricing, and imposed costs. Build quality through TQM, empowerment, supplier involvement, training, statistical control, QCCs, and kaizen.
- **Motivation, leadership, and global HR**: Treat motivation as universal needs with culturally different fuel: diagnose needs -> drives -> valued outcomes, fix hygiene factors, then add motivators. Compare equity with the relevant group, use participation and feedback appropriate to the culture, and match individual/group rewards to job and work-centrality patterns. Use a manager-leader mindset, Theory X/Y/Z, Bass's 4 I's, GLOBE CLT dimensions, and responsible leadership; keep a universal core of integrity and purpose with local expression. Run global HR as a lifecycle: select home-, host-, or third-country talent; assess adaptability and family readiness; prepare and support adjustment; make compensation whole; use validated cultural assimilators; develop local capability; and plan repatriation and knowledge transfer.

Country conditions, laws, sanctions, exchange rates, and cultural practices change; verify current facts and local requirements before acting. Use project-specific tools and current primary sources for implementation.
