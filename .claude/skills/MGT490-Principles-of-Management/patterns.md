# Patterns and Techniques

## POLC Diagnostic Loop
**When to use**: A team has a goal but execution is unclear or off track.
**How**: Define the result; arrange work and resources; motivate and influence people; compare evidence with the goal; correct the plan or execution.
**Trade-offs**: More control can improve coordination but can reduce autonomy; more delegation increases capacity but requires clear accountability.

## Stakeholder and Ethics Screen
**When to use**: A decision has legal, social, environmental, or reputational consequences.
**How**: Map internal, task-environment, and general-environment stakeholders. Test consequences, self-interest, rights, and justice. Check governance, incentives, reporting channels, and social-audit measures.
**Trade-offs**: A broader analysis takes time, but narrow analysis hides costs and rights violations.

## Shared-Value Scan
**When to use**: A social or environmental problem intersects with the firm's business.
**How**: Examine societal benefits/harms in products, the value chain, and local clusters. Search for new markets, value-chain transformation, and supportive-cluster development. Define paired social and economic outcomes.
**Trade-offs**: CSV requires strategic focus and long-term investment; it is more durable than disconnected charity but less immediately visible.

## Planning and Goal Cascade
**When to use**: An aspiration must become coordinated work.
**How**: Start with mission, vision, and values. Set strategic, tactical, and operational goals. Make goals SMART, convert them into action plans, assign owners and dates, then review through the planning/control cycle.
**Trade-offs**: Specific goals improve accountability but can narrow attention; balance measures with values and learning goals.

## Strategic Assessment
**When to use**: Choosing a position, investment, or competitive response.
**How**: Use SWOT for internal/external diagnosis; use VRIO for resource advantage; use forecasting and benchmarking for reality checks; use the five forces for industry pressure; select a generic strategy and align functions.
**Trade-offs**: Frameworks structure judgment but do not replace market evidence or scenario thinking.

## Rational Decision Process
**When to use**: The decision is consequential and enough information can be gathered.
**How**: Diagnose actual versus desired conditions, generate alternatives, evaluate cost/quality/ethics/feasibility/effectiveness, implement, and evaluate. Add an ethical decision tree, evidence, dissent, and a post-mortem.
**Trade-offs**: More analysis improves quality up to a point; use satisficing for time-sensitive, low-consequence choices.

## Culture Change
**When to use**: Stated values and actual behavior do not match, or strategy requires new behavior.
**How**: Diagnose artifacts, espoused values, and assumptions. Unfreeze the current state, use multiple culture levers, train and model the new behavior, then refreeze through rewards, systems, goals, and structure.
**Trade-offs**: Multiple levers improve durability but increase coordination and political cost.

## Strategic HR System
**When to use**: Capability, performance, retention, or fit is a strategic bottleneck.
**How**: Align recruitment, validated selection, onboarding, learning/development, compensation, performance management, employee relations, and legal safeguards with strategy and culture.
**Trade-offs**: Standardization supports fairness and validity; excessive standardization can reduce flexibility and candidate experience.

## Motivation Diagnosis
**When to use**: Performance or engagement is lower than expected.
**How**: Check needs and meaning, equity, expectancy/instrumentality/valence, job characteristics, reinforcement, resources, and stress. Select a targeted intervention and measure the response.
**Trade-offs**: Incentives can focus effort but can crowd out intrinsic motivation or encourage gaming; combine monetary and nonmonetary rewards.

## Team Construction and Conflict
**When to use**: Work depends on collaboration or disagreement is damaging results.
**How**: Define purpose and accountability; set composition, roles, norms, trust, goals, feedback, and processes. Use appropriate conflict styles, devil's advocacy, or dialectic debate.
**Trade-offs**: Integration produces high-quality solutions but requires time; domination is faster but risks commitment and information loss.

## Situational Leadership
**When to use**: A leader must choose how to influence different people in different contexts.
**How**: Identify outcomes, leader behaviors, follower characteristics, and environmental conditions. Match task/relationship behavior and combine transactional clarity with transformational purpose.
**Trade-offs**: Adaptation improves fit but can appear inconsistent; explain the rationale and keep values stable.

## Communication Design
**When to use**: Information, change, conflict, or credibility is at stake.
**How**: Choose media richness for ambiguity, encode plainly, invite feedback, paraphrase, listen actively, and address grapevine information quickly. Use social media with transparent policy and privacy safeguards.
**Trade-offs**: Rich media costs more time; lean media scales better but increases misunderstanding risk.

## Control and Quality Improvement
**When to use**: Performance must stay within standards or improve continuously.
**How**: Set a standard, measure, compare, correct, and learn. Combine feedforward, concurrent, and feedback controls; use the Balanced Scorecard and PDCA/TQM.
**Trade-offs**: More measurement can improve visibility but also increase gaming, cost, and surveillance risk.

## Project Planning Toolkit
**When to use**: A project needs sequence, timing, and economic feasibility.
**How**: Flowchart decisions and sequence; Gantt-chart tasks and dates; break-even-test price, volume, and costs.
**Trade-offs**: Simple tools are accessible but become misleading for highly concurrent projects or uncertain assumptions.
