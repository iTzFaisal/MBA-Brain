# Chapter 11: Managing Individual Differences and Behavior: Supervising People as People

## Core Idea
Managers improve performance by understanding individual differences, the way perceptions shape behavior, work-related attitudes, diversity, and stress. People are not interchangeable inputs; supervision must combine evidence, self-awareness, psychological safety, and meaningful work.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Big Five personality dimensions**: Extroversion, agreeableness, conscientiousness, emotional stability, and openness to experience.
- **Core Self-Evaluations**: Self-efficacy, self-esteem, locus of control, and emotional stability.
- **Emotional intelligence**: Monitor one's own and others' feelings and use the information to guide thinking and action. Goleman's components are self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, and relationship management.
- **Values-attitudes-behavior model**: Values are broad ideals; attitudes are learned predispositions toward specific objects; behavior expresses action and judgment.
- **Four-step perceptual process**: Selective attention; interpretation/evaluation; storing in memory; retrieval from memory.
- **Five perceptual distortions**: Stereotyping, implicit bias, halo effect, recency effect, and causal attribution.
- **Pygmalion effect**: Expectations of oneself or others can produce behavior that makes those expectations come true.
- **Diversity Wheel**: Personality; internal dimensions; external dimensions; organizational dimensions.
- **Stress-performance relationship**: Moderate positive stress can support performance; very low or very high stress can reduce it.

## Key Concepts
- **Organizational behavior**: Understanding and managing people at work.
- **Prosocial behavior**: Voluntary behavior intended to benefit another.
- **Organizational citizenship behavior**: Discretionary behavior beyond formal job requirements.
- **Counterproductive work behavior**: Behavior that harms employees or the organization.
- **Employee engagement**: Involvement, satisfaction, and enthusiasm for work.
- **Organizational commitment**: Identification with and commitment to the organization and its goals.
- **Burnout**: Emotional, mental, and physical exhaustion.
- **Stressors**: Demands, constraints, or opportunities that create tension.

## Mental Models
- Separate observation from interpretation: ask what happened, what you inferred, and what evidence could disconfirm it.
- Use expectations as a leadership responsibility; what managers notice, praise, and assume can become self-fulfilling.
- Diagnose stress at individual, task, role, group, organizational, and nonwork levels before prescribing resilience.

## Anti-patterns
- **Single-trait judgment**: Letting one attractive trait, recent event, stereotype, or assumed cause dominate evaluation.
- **Bias as objectivity**: Treating implicit or causal assumptions as facts.
- **Forced citizenship**: Pressuring employees to perform discretionary prosocial behavior.
- **Toxic positivity**: Demanding a good attitude while leaving excessive workload, poor treatment, or insecurity unchanged.
- **Token diversity**: Counting representation without voice, inclusion, psychological safety, or fair opportunity.

## Worked Example
Away's rapid growth and strong brand were paired with reports of public Slack criticism, excessive hours, restricted time off, surveillance concerns, and burnout. Its stated values included being thoughtful, customer-obsessed, empowered, accessible, and "in it together," yet employee experiences suggested a gap between espoused and enacted values. The case can be diagnosed through EI, perception, engagement, commitment, diversity climate, and the six sources of job stress.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use validated, job-relevant evidence instead of shortcuts in judging people.
2. Build self-efficacy through challenging work, feedback, small successes, mentoring, and role models.
3. Treat engagement, commitment, prosocial behavior, and psychological safety as management outcomes.
4. Audit diversity across internal, external, personality, and organizational dimensions.
5. Reduce unhealthy stressors through staffing, autonomy, support, interesting work, assistance programs, and counseling.
