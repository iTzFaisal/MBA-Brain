# Chapter 14: Power, Influence, and Leadership: From Becoming a Manager to Becoming a Leader

## Core Idea
Leadership is influencing employees to voluntarily pursue organizational goals; power is the ability to marshal human, informational, and other resources. Effective leadership combines appropriate power, influence, behavior, situational fit, ethical purpose, and follower development.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Six sources of power**: Legitimate, reward, coercive, expert, referent, and informational.
- **Influence tactics**: Rational persuasion, inspirational appeals, consultation, ingratiation, personal appeals, exchange, coalition, pressure, and legitimating tactics.
- **Trait approach**: Positive task traits include intelligence, conscientiousness, openness, emotional stability, and positive affect; dark traits include narcissism, Machiavellianism, and psychopathy.
- **Behavioral approach**: Task-oriented behavior ensures efficient accomplishment; relationship-oriented behavior focuses on interactions and employee concerns.
- **Fiedler contingency model**: Match task-oriented or relationship-oriented leadership to leader-member relations, task structure, and position power.
- **House path-goal model**: Leaders clarify paths and provide support; effective behavior depends on employee characteristics and environmental factors.
- **Full-range model**: Passive/avoidant leadership, transactional leadership, and transformational leadership. Strong leaders combine transactional clarity with transformational purpose.
- **Transformational leadership**: Inspire motivation, inspire trust/idealized influence, encourage excellence/individualized consideration, and stimulate employees intellectually.
- **Contemporary perspectives**: Leader-member exchange, servant leadership, humility, empowering leadership, and ethical leadership.

## Key Concepts
- **Socialized power**: Power directed toward helping others.
- **Personalized power**: Power used for selfish ends.
- **LMX in-group/out-group**: High-trust relationships versus limited-trust relationships with different followers.
- **Servant leadership**: Serving followers and organizational purpose rather than the leader's self-interest.
- **Psychological empowerment**: Employees' belief that they have control over their work.
- **Abusive supervision**: Sustained verbal and nonverbal hostility toward subordinates.
- **Dunning-Kruger effect**: Inability to recognize one's incompetence while feeling confident.

## Mental Models
- Prefer power that builds commitment and capability over power that produces short-term compliance.
- Match style to the situation; do not ask whether a leader is universally task- or relationship-oriented.
- Combine transactional clarity about goals and rewards with transformational meaning and development.
- Humility means shifting from "me" to "we," sharing credit, seeking feedback, and tolerating learning errors.

## Anti-patterns
- **Coercion as default**: Pressure and punishment generate resentment and conceal information.
- **Charisma without ethics**: Vision and attraction can mobilize people toward self-serving ends.
- **One-style leadership**: Ignoring the task, followers, environment, and leader-member relationship.
- **Favoritism**: Creating neglected out-groups with unequal trust, information, and opportunity.
- **Abusive supervision**: Humiliation, insults, shouting, deliberate ignoring, or intimidation.

## Worked Example
Adam Neumann's charisma, risk-taking, and grand vision helped WeWork reach more than 520 locations and an almost $50 billion valuation. The same influence was paired with self-dealing: he trademarked "We," charged his own company $5.9 million for its use, borrowed from WeWork, and rented buildings back to it. Investors rejected the IPO, valuation fell sharply, and he was removed. The case separates entrepreneurial influence from ethical, accountable leadership.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diagnose what source of power is being used and who benefits.
2. Use rational persuasion, consultation, collaboration, and inspiration to build commitment.
3. Match leadership behavior to situational control and follower needs.
4. Pair transactional accountability with transformational purpose.
5. Treat humility, ethical modeling, empowerment, and safe reporting as performance requirements.
