# Learning Module 1: Shared Value and Sustainable Development

## Core Idea
Creating shared value (CSV) improves and reorients isolated corporate social responsibility through a strategic approach: implement policies and operating practices that improve a company's competitiveness while advancing economic and social conditions in its communities. The goal is to enlarge the total value created, not divide a fixed pie between profit and society.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **CSR versus CSV**: CSR often uses profits to address a narrow issue; CSV uses the core business to create profit while solving a meaningful social problem.
  - When to use: When a social issue is material to products, customers, operations, supply chains, or local economic conditions.
  - How: Move the issue into strategy and operations, define both stakeholder and economic outcomes, and measure whether the initiative can sustain itself.
- **Model of shared value creation**: Three dynamic inputs feed three key processes and produce shared and economic value.
  - Inputs: Consider societal benefits, harms, and needs in products/services; consider them in the value chain; identify local constraints to productivity and growth.
  - Processes: Discover new products, markets, and opportunities; transform the value chain; develop supportive local clusters.
- **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**: The UN's 17 goals provide a shared global roadmap for governments, business, civil society, and schools.
- **CSV recommendations**: Set priorities for strategic alignment, encourage a long-term mindset, and break out of old routines by thinking bigger and collaborating across the ecosystem.

## Key Concepts
- **Shared value**: Economic value for shareholders plus lasting benefits for broader stakeholders.
- **Value chain**: All activities through which a firm adds value to products or services, including production, marketing, and support.
- **Supportive cluster**: A geographic concentration of related firms, suppliers, universities, governments, and other organizations whose interaction improves the local business environment.
- **Fixed-pie perspective**: The assumption that social spending necessarily reduces shareholder value.
- **Strategic alignment**: Connecting social initiatives to a firm's core capabilities, markets, and objectives.
- **Stakeholder collaboration**: Businesses, entrepreneurs, governments, schools, nonprofits, and communities each contribute capabilities to system-level solutions.
- **Inertia**: Resistance to change caused by established routines and expectations.

## Mental Models
- If a social initiative sits in an isolated CSR department, ask how to move it into the value proposition, value chain, or cluster.
- Look for the constraint that harms both society and business; relieving it often creates the strongest CSV opportunity.
- Treat societal needs as dynamic inputs requiring continuous reassessment.

## Anti-patterns
- **Unaligned charity**: Funding a cause unrelated to the business may create goodwill but rarely changes the underlying system.
- **Cosmetic CSV**: Renaming an old program without changing incentives, operations, or measurable outcomes.
- **Ignoring harm**: Searching only for benefits while refusing to examine negative value-chain effects destroys credibility.

## Worked Example
Novartis's Arogya Parivar program used local health educators and free health camps in rural Indian villages, then sold inexpensive medicines for common conditions. Villagers gained health knowledge and access to doctors; Novartis found a profitable market. The example follows the model: identify an underserved need, redesign market access and the value chain, involve local actors, and measure both social and economic results.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start with a real societal need that intersects the firm's capabilities.
2. Evaluate products, value-chain activities, and local clusters separately.
3. Involve businesses, entrepreneurs, governments, schools, nonprofits, and communities.
4. Set priorities, adopt a long-term mindset, and break old routines to make CSV real.
