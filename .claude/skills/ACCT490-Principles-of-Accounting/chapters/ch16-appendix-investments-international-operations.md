# Appendix C: Investments and International Operations

## Core Idea
Investment accounting depends on security type, management's intent and ability to hold or sell, and the investor's ownership influence. The measurement basis determines whether changes reach net income, other comprehensive income/equity, the investment account, or consolidated statements; foreign-currency transactions require the same discipline about dates and exchange rates.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Investment-classification decision**: First distinguish debt securities (creditor relationship) from equity securities (owner relationship). A short-term investment is intended to convert to cash within one year or the operating cycle, whichever is longer, and is readily convertible to cash. Otherwise it is long term. Then classify by intent and ownership: trading, held-to-maturity, available-for-sale, significant influence, or controlling influence.
- **Noninfluential securities matrix**: Trading debt/equity is actively managed for short-term profit, reported at fair value, and sends unrealized gains/losses to income. Held-to-maturity applies only to debt the investor intends and is able to hold until maturity; report at cost/amortized cost and accrue interest. Available-for-sale includes debt/equity not in the other two categories; report at fair value, with unrealized gains/losses in other comprehensive income/equity and realized sale gains/losses in income.
- **Influence procedure**: At 20% to 50% voting ownership, significant influence is presumed and the equity method normally applies. Record cost, add the investor's share of investee income, and subtract the investor's share of investee dividends; dividends are not dividend revenue under this method. Above 50% generally indicates control: the investor is the parent, the investee is the subsidiary, and consolidated statements combine assets, liabilities, revenues, expenses, and cash flows.
- **Fair-value adjustment procedure**: Compare the portfolio's total cost and fair value, determine the required ending Fair Value Adjustment balance, compare it with the existing balance, and record only the difference. Portfolio adjustment is separate from the gain/loss on an individual security sale, which compares sale proceeds with that security's cost.
- **Foreign-currency procedure**: Translate a sale, purchase, receivable, or payable at the transaction-date spot rate. Remeasure an outstanding monetary balance at each reporting date; recognize the exchange gain/loss in income. On settlement, compare the cash translated at the settlement rate with the carrying amount. Translate a controlled foreign subsidiary into the parent's reporting currency for consolidation; translation changes are reported in other comprehensive income under the chapter's framework.
- **ROA component analysis**: Use `return on total assets = profit margin x total asset turnover`, where `profit margin = net income/net sales` and `total asset turnover = net sales/average total assets`. If one component declines, the other must improve enough to maintain ROA.

## Key Concepts
- **Trading securities**: Actively managed debt/equity investments reported at fair value through income.
- **Held-to-maturity (HTM)**: Debt investments intended and able to be held to maturity.
- **Available-for-sale (AFS)**: Debt/equity investments not classified as trading or HTM.
- **Unrealized gain/loss**: Fair-value change before a security is sold.
- **Equity method**: Method recognizing the investor's share of investee income and dividends in the investment account.
- **Significant influence**: Usually 20%-50% voting ownership or persuasive evidence of influence.
- **Controlling influence**: Usually more than 50% voting ownership.
- **Parent/subsidiary**: Controlling investor and controlled investee in consolidated reporting.
- **Foreign exchange rate**: Price of one currency stated in another currency.
- **Other comprehensive income**: Equity changes such as AFS and foreign-currency translation adjustments.

## Mental Models
- **Use the classification triad**: Ask what the security is, how long management intends/needs to hold it, and how much influence the investor has; do not classify from market value alone.
- **Think measurement destination before journalizing**: Trading unrealized changes affect income, AFS unrealized changes affect OCI/equity, HTM changes are not fair-valued, and equity-method earnings change both income and the investment balance.
- **Treat dividends according to the method**: Dividend revenue is appropriate for noninfluential holdings; an equity-method dividend is recovery of part of the investment balance.
- **Use the exchange-rate date rule**: Transaction date establishes the initial dollar amount, reporting date remeasures an open balance, and settlement date recognizes the final currency difference.

## Anti-patterns
- **Using ownership percentage as the only test**: Significant influence can be overridden by evidence, and control can exist below 50% in unusual cases.
- **Reporting AFS unrealized gains in net income**: Under this chapter's framework, they belong in OCI/equity until realized.
- **Recording HTM fair-value adjustments**: HTM is carried at cost/amortized cost, with interest recognized as earned.
- **Crediting dividend revenue under the equity method**: The investor already recognized its share of investee earnings; the dividend reduces the investment account.
- **Using the transaction-date rate at settlement**: Remeasure open foreign-currency receivables/payables and recognize the intervening gain or loss.
- **Leaving the parent's investment account in consolidated statements**: Consolidation presents the parent and subsidiaries as one entity and combines their underlying balances.

## Worked Example
Micron buys 30% of Star's common stock for $70,650. When Star reports $20,000 net income, Micron records its 30% share, `$6,000`, as income and an increase in the investment. When Star pays $10,000 dividends, Micron receives `$3,000` but debits the investment rather than dividend revenue:

```text
Investment balance = $70,650 + $6,000 - $3,000 = $73,650
If sold for $80,000: gain = $80,000 - $73,650 = $6,350
```

For contrast, a $10,000 foreign-currency receivable recorded at $1.80 per pound is initially $18,000; remeasured at $1.84 it produces a $400 gain, and settlement at $1.78 for $17,800 produces a $600 loss against the $18,400 carrying amount.

## Key Takeaways
1. Classify by security type, short/long-term intent, and ownership influence before selecting the accounting method.
2. Keep fair-value changes in the correct destination: income for trading, OCI/equity for AFS, and no fair-value adjustment for HTM.
3. Under the equity method, record investee income when earned and dividends as reductions of the investment.
4. Consolidate controlled subsidiaries and eliminate the parent's investment account from the consolidated presentation.
5. Remeasure foreign-currency monetary balances at reporting and settlement dates, recording exchange gains/losses as they arise.
6. Decompose ROA into margin and turnover to identify whether pricing/cost control or asset use drives performance.
