# Appendix A: Financial Statement Information

## Core Idea
This appendix is a reference set of annual-report evidence rather than a new accounting cycle. It supplies financial statements and selected notes for Apple, Google, and Samsung so the procedures in Chapters 1-13 can be applied to real companies, different fiscal years, currencies, capital structures, and reporting conventions.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Annual-report reading sequence**: Use the letter to shareholders, financial history/highlights, MD&A, management's internal-control report, independent auditor's report, financial statements, notes, and officer/director information as a connected evidence package. The financial statements are the core, but notes explain policies, estimates, risks, and classifications.
- **Cross-statement linkage**: Start with the balance-sheet equation; trace net income and dividends through retained earnings/stockholders' equity; trace operating, investing, and financing flows through the cash-flow statement; then use notes to explain the measurement and timing behind each number.
- **Comparative-company reference**: Apple appears on pages A-2 through A-9, Google on A-10 through A-13, and Samsung on A-14 through A-17. Normalize units, dates, currencies, share counts, and accounting definitions before using horizontal, vertical, or ratio analysis.
- **Note-evidence procedure**: Check fiscal-year definition, revenue recognition, cash-equivalent policy, investment classification, fair-value hierarchy, warranties, debt terms, stock splits, and comprehensive-income components before interpreting a reported trend.

## Key Concepts
- **Annual report**: Summary of a company's results, financial condition, and plans for external and internal users.
- **Form 10-K/EDGAR**: U.S. annual filing and SEC electronic database used for company disclosures.
- **MD&A**: Management's discussion of operating results, liquidity, capital resources, cash flows, risks, and critical estimates.
- **Consolidated statements**: Statements presenting a parent and controlled subsidiaries as one reporting entity.
- **Comprehensive income**: Net income plus other comprehensive income.
- **Accumulated other comprehensive income**: Cumulative equity balance from items such as AFS gains/losses and foreign-currency translation.
- **Fiscal year**: A company's reporting period, which may not match the calendar year.
- **Cash equivalents**: Highly liquid investments with original maturities generally of three months or less.
- **Selected notes**: Explanations of recognition, measurement, estimates, commitments, and risks.

## Mental Models
- **Use statements as a linked system**: Never interpret income, equity, or cash in isolation; each should explain a movement in another statement or note.
- **Think in units before ratios**: Verify whether amounts are millions, thousands, Korean won, dollars, or per-share figures before comparing companies.
- **Use notes as measurement metadata**: A number without its fiscal date, policy, estimate, or fair-value basis is incomplete evidence.
- **Treat the appendix as a reproducible dataset**: Recompute a ratio from the displayed statements, then reconcile it to the chapter's example before drawing a conclusion.

## Anti-patterns
- **Comparing raw dollar totals across Apple, Google, and Samsung**: Scale, currency, fiscal dates, and business mix make unadjusted totals misleading.
- **Ignoring notes and reading only headline earnings**: Revenue deferrals, warranties, fair value, debt, and foreign operations can change the interpretation.
- **Confusing net income, comprehensive income, and equity changes**: Other comprehensive income and owner transactions follow different reporting paths.
- **Using unadjusted per-share data after Apple's seven-for-one split**: The appendix states that share and per-share information was adjusted retroactively.
- **Treating a PDF-extracted column as authoritative without reconciliation**: The source extraction interleaves columns; verify totals and formulas against the nearby statement labels.

## Worked Example
Apple's 2014 statements provide a compact cross-statement check, in millions of dollars. The balance sheet satisfies `assets $231,839 = liabilities $120,292 + shareholders' equity $111,547`. The income statement reports net income of $39,510, basic EPS of $6.49, and cash dividends declared per common share of $1.82. The cash-flow statement reports operating cash of $59,713, investing cash of $(22,579), and financing cash of $(37,549), giving a net decrease of $(415). Beginning cash of $14,259 less $415 equals ending cash of $13,844, the balance-sheet cash amount.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use the annual-report structure to locate statements, auditor evidence, management explanations, and policy notes.
2. Reconcile totals across the balance sheet, income statement, equity statement, and cash-flow statement before analysis.
3. Normalize units, dates, currencies, share counts, and fiscal-year conventions before comparing companies.
4. Use notes to explain recognition, fair value, debt, revenue, warranties, stock splits, and other measurement choices.
5. Recompute chapter ratios from the appendix data and investigate any mismatch caused by rounding or extraction order.
