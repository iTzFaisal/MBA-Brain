# Chapter 1: Introducing Financial Statements

## Core Idea
Accounting is an information and measurement system that **identifies, records, and communicates** relevant, reliable, and comparable information about business activities. Transaction analysis keeps the accounting equation in balance and turns operating events into four linked financial statements.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Accounting Functions (Identifying, Recording, Communicating)**: Use when tracing any business activity into a report. Select the relevant transaction or event; measure and log it in dollars; then prepare, analyze, and interpret reports such as financial statements.
- **Ethical Decision Making**: Use when more than one accounting treatment or reporting choice is possible. (1) Identify ethical concerns using personal ethics, (2) analyze options and all good and bad consequences, and (3) make the best ethical decision after weighing those consequences.
- **Fraud Triangle**: Use to assess fraud risk. Fraud requires **opportunity** (a low-risk way to commit it), **pressure** or incentive, and **rationalization** or attitude. Prevention through internal controls is cheaper and more effective than recovery after detection.
- **Conceptual Framework and Convergence**: Use to judge whether accounting information is decision-useful. The framework connects objectives of financial accounting to qualitative characteristics (relevant, reliable, comparable), statement elements, and recognition and measurement criteria. U.S. GAAP (FASB/SEC) and IFRS (IASB) are similar but not identical; convergence reduces inconsistencies.
- **Building Blocks for GAAP**: Apply the measurement (cost), revenue recognition, expense recognition (matching), and full disclosure principles with the going-concern, monetary-unit, time-period, and business-entity assumptions. Apply the materiality and benefit-exceeds-cost constraints when deciding what to disclose.
- **Expanded Accounting Equation**: Use for every transaction or event:

```text
Assets = Liabilities + Common Stock - Dividends + Revenues - Expenses
```

## Key Concepts
- **External users**: Lenders, shareholders, regulators, auditors, suppliers, customers, and others who rely on general-purpose financial statements but do not run the organization.
- **Internal users**: Managers, officers, controllers, auditors, and operating staff who need reports designed for internal decisions; this is managerial accounting.
- **Assets**: Resources owned or controlled that are expected to provide future benefits.
- **Liabilities**: Creditor claims and obligations to transfer assets, products, or services.
- **Equity**: The owner's residual claim, equal to assets minus liabilities; also called net assets.
- **Revenue recognition principle**: Recognize revenue when goods or services are provided, not merely when cash is collected.
- **Expense recognition principle**: Record costs in the period in which they help generate recognized revenue.
- **Retained earnings**: Accumulated revenues less expenses and dividends since the business began.
- **Financing, investing, operating activities**: Financing supplies owner/creditor resources; investing acquires or disposes of operating assets; operating uses resources to provide products or services.
- **Return on assets (ROA)**: `Net income / Average total assets`; compare with prior periods, competitors, industry norms, or alternative investments.

## Mental Models
- **Use the equation before the account title**: Identify what the business owns and owes, then classify the change as an asset, liability, owner, revenue, expense, or dividend effect.
- **Think timing, not cash**: Cash receipt can be a liability, and cash collection can be neither new revenue nor new income when the earning event occurred earlier.
- **Use statement links as a dependency chain**: Income statement net income feeds retained earnings; ending retained earnings feeds the balance sheet; cash from the balance sheet reconciles the statement of cash flows.
- **Use risk-return as a comparison rule**: A higher return may justify a higher risk, but an investment with a lower return than the existing business needs a clear diversification or strategic benefit.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating bookkeeping as all of accounting**: A chronological log alone cannot replace analysis and interpretation.
- **Recording cash as revenue automatically**: Advance cash creates a liability until the company performs.
- **Calling dividends an expense**: Dividends are distributions to owners, reduce equity, and do not enter net income.
- **Confusing legal form with accounting separation**: A proprietorship is a separate accounting entity even though it is not legally separate from its owner.
- **Ignoring opportunity, pressure, or rationalization**: Fraud prevention fails when internal controls address only detection or only one side of the Fraud Triangle.

## Worked Example
FastForward begins operations with 11 December transactions. The accounting-equation analysis is:

| Event | Effect |
|---|---|
| Owner invests $30,000 for stock | Cash +30,000; Common Stock +30,000 |
| Buy supplies for cash and equipment for cash | Cash -28,500; Supplies +2,500; Equipment +26,000 |
| Buy $7,100 supplies on credit | Supplies +7,100; Accounts Payable +7,100 |
| Earn $4,200 cash consulting revenue | Cash and Revenues +4,200 |
| Pay $1,000 rent and $700 salary | Cash and Expenses -1,700 |
| Earn $1,600 consulting plus $300 rental on credit | Accounts Receivable and Revenues +1,900 |
| Collect the $1,900 receivable | Cash +1,900; Accounts Receivable -1,900; no new revenue |
| Pay $900 on the payable and a $200 dividend | Cash -1,100; Accounts Payable -900; Dividends -200 |

Ending balances are assets $40,400 ($4,800 cash + $9,600 supplies + $26,000 equipment), liabilities $6,200, common stock $30,000, retained earnings $4,200, revenues $6,100, and expenses $1,700. Thus `net income = 6,100 - 1,700 = 4,400`; operating, investing, and financing cash flows are respectively $1,000, $(26,000), and $29,800, producing the $4,800 ending cash balance.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start with the expanded accounting equation and preserve equality after every transaction.
2. Recognize revenue when performance occurs and expenses when their related revenue is recognized.
3. Separate internal decision needs from external general-purpose reporting needs.
4. Prepare the income statement, retained earnings statement, balance sheet, and cash-flow statement in their linked order.
5. Evaluate ROA against a meaningful benchmark and consider the risk that produces it.
