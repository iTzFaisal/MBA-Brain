# Chapter 9: Reporting and Analyzing Current Liabilities

## Core Idea
A liability is a probable future sacrifice of assets or services arising from a past transaction that creates a present obligation. Current-liability accounting records known obligations, estimates obligations whose amounts are uncertain but measurable, and distinguishes contingent obligations that should be recorded, disclosed, or ignored.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Liability recognition test**: Record a liability only when a past event has occurred, the company presently owes a creditor or service, and a future payment or performance is probable. Expected future wages do not qualify until employees work and earn them.
- **Current-versus-long-term classification**: Classify an obligation as current when due within one year or the operating cycle, whichever is longer, and expected to be settled with current assets or another current liability. Classify the remainder as long term. Report the current portion of long-term debt as current; the source treats this as a balance-sheet classification, not a required adjusting entry.
- **Known-liability procedure**: Record accounts payable when goods or services are bought on credit; credit Sales Taxes Payable for taxes collected as an agent for government; credit Unearned Revenue for cash received before performance and debit it as revenue is earned.
- **Short-term note timeline**: For an interest-bearing note, record `Dr Cash / Cr Notes Payable` at issuance, accrue interest earned by the creditor at each reporting date, and at maturity remove principal and Interest Payable while recording only the remaining current-period interest. Interest uses `principal x annual rate x time fraction`, commonly days over 360 under the banker's rule.
- **Payroll liability procedure**: Gross pay is compensation before deductions; net pay is gross pay less deductions. Debit salaries expense for gross pay, credit employee withholding liabilities and Salaries Payable for the net amount, then debit Salaries Payable when cash is paid. Separately record employer FICA, FUTA, and SUTA expense and payable.
- **Estimated-liability matching**: Recognize probable, reasonably estimable employee benefits, bonuses, and warranty costs in the period employees earn the benefit or the related sale occurs. Later payment or warranty repair debits the liability, not a second expense.
- **Contingency decision tree**: A potential obligation from a past event is recorded when the future loss is probable and reasonably estimable; disclosed in notes when reasonably possible, or when probable but not estimable; and neither recorded nor disclosed when remote. A future business risk with no past transaction is not a contingent liability.
- **Times interest earned analysis**: Use `income before interest expense and income taxes / interest expense` to assess the cushion for fixed interest payments. A ratio persistently below roughly 1.5-2.0 signals sharply higher default risk; interpret it with income volatility and trends.

## Key Concepts
- **Liability**: Present obligation from a past event requiring future assets, services, or performance.
- **Current liability**: Obligation due within the longer of one year or the operating cycle.
- **Accounts payable**: Amount owed to suppliers for credit purchases.
- **Sales Taxes Payable**: Tax collected from customers and owed to a government agency.
- **Unearned revenue**: Customer cash received before the company delivers the promised product or service.
- **Short-term note payable**: Negotiable written promise for a specified amount on a definite near-term date.
- **Payroll deductions**: Required or voluntary amounts withheld from gross pay and temporarily owed to others.
- **Estimated liability**: Known obligation with an uncertain amount that can be reasonably estimated.
- **Contingent liability**: Potential obligation dependent on an uncertain future event arising from a past event.
- **Times interest earned**: Coverage ratio comparing income available for interest with interest expense.

## Mental Models
- **Ask whom, when, and how much**: Liability uncertainty may concern the payee, settlement date, or amount; each answer determines measurement and classification.
- **Separate collection from earning**: Cash collected for sales tax or advance tickets belongs to another party or future performance until remitted or earned.
- **Treat a note as a calendar**: Allocate principal and interest to the periods that use the borrowed funds; do not wait for the cash payment to recognize expense.
- **Use probability plus measurability**: For a possible loss, combine likelihood and estimateability before choosing record, disclose, or no action.

## Anti-patterns
- **Classifying by account name alone**: A multi-year note or warranty can have both current and long-term portions.
- **Recording sales tax as revenue or expense**: The seller is collecting an amount owed to the government.
- **Recognizing unearned revenue on receipt**: The cash creates a performance obligation until goods or services are delivered.
- **Charging all note interest at maturity**: Period-end accruals are required by matching and accrual accounting.
- **Treating employee withholdings as employer expense**: Withholdings are liabilities; employer payroll taxes are the additional expense.
- **Recording every lawsuit or business risk**: Contingencies require a past event and the source's probability/estimateability thresholds.
- **Using times interest earned without checking volatility**: A high current ratio can coexist with unstable earnings and fixed payment risk.

## Worked Example
Kern's illustrative note is issued November 25 for $8,000, 90 days, at 5%. Total interest is `$8,000 x 5% x 90/360 = $100`. At December 31, 36 days have elapsed, so accrue `$8,000 x 5% x 36/360 = $40`:

```text
Dec. 31  Dr Interest Expense                 $40
             Cr Interest Payable                         $40
```

At maturity on February 23, the remaining 54 days create `$60` of interest. Assuming no reversing entry, settle both the accrued and remaining interest and the principal:

```text
Dr Interest Expense                         $60
Dr Interest Payable                         $40
Dr Notes Payable                          8,000
    Cr Cash                                         $8,100
```

The $40 belongs to the earlier reporting period, the $60 to the later period, and the total cash equals principal plus the $100 interest.

## Key Takeaways
1. Establish the past event, present obligation, due horizon, and settlement resources before classifying a liability.
2. Record sales taxes and advance receipts as liabilities until remitted or earned.
3. Compute note interest by principal, annual rate, and elapsed time; accrue it at reporting dates.
4. Separate employee deductions from employer payroll-tax expense and monitor the remittance liabilities.
5. Estimate warranty, vacation, pension, and bonus obligations when probable and measurable, then settle against the liability.
6. Record only probable and estimable contingencies; disclose reasonably possible or probable-but-unmeasurable losses.
7. Compare times interest earned with risk, volatility, prior periods, and industry norms.
