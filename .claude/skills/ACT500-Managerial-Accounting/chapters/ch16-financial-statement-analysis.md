# Chapter 16: Financial Statement Analysis

## Core Idea
Financial statement analysis converts general-purpose statements into decision information about liquidity, solvency, profitability, and market prospects. The analyst combines horizontal, vertical, and common-size analysis with ratios, trends, industry comparisons, cash flows, annual-report disclosures, internal controls, audit opinions, and unusual-item normalization.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Decision-to-ratio chain**: Identify the stakeholder's decision, select the relevant analytical block, calculate, compare over time or with peers, investigate causes, and decide.
- **Horizontal analysis**: Compare periods; `percentage change = (current - base) / base`.
- **Vertical/common-size analysis**: Express balance-sheet items as a percentage of total assets or liabilities/equity and income-statement items as a percentage of sales.
- **Liquidity analysis**: Use working capital, current ratio, quick ratio, receivable turnover, and inventory turnover.
- **Solvency analysis**: Use fixed-assets support, liabilities-to-equity, and times-interest-earned together.
- **Profitability/market analysis**: Use asset turnover, ROA, ROE, EPS, P/E, dividends per share, and dividend yield.
- **Annual-report interpretation**: Read notes, MD&A, cash flows, internal-control reports, audit opinions, and non-GAAP reconciliations alongside statements.
- **Unusual-item normalization**: Separate continuing operations from discontinued operations, prior-period errors, accounting changes, fair-value effects, and OCI.

## Key Concepts
- **Liquidity**: Ability to pay short-term obligations.
- **Solvency**: Ability to pay long-term debt and interest.
- **Profitability**: Ability to generate earnings and returns.
- **Working capital**: Current assets less current liabilities.
- **Quick assets**: Cash, temporary investments, and receivables normally convertible quickly.
- **Turnover**: Activity generated per average asset or balance.
- **Leverage**: Use of debt that can magnify both returns and losses.
- **Unmodified audit opinion**: Clean opinion that statements fairly present the required information.
- **Other comprehensive income (OCI)**: Specified gains/losses outside net income.

## Mental Models
- Liquidity is a conversion pipeline: cash -> receivables -> inventory; prepaid assets do not directly provide cash.
- Solvency is a safety stack: asset support, debt/equity mix, and interest coverage.
- Profitability is a ladder: turnover -> ROA -> ROE -> common-shareholder returns.
- A ratio is a relationship, not a verdict; compare it with time, industry, and causes.
- Normalize unusual and non-GAAP items before forecasting recurring performance.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating one ratio as a complete assessment.
- Comparing absolute working capital across companies of different size.
- Treating the current ratio as immediate liquidity without examining asset mix.
- Assuming high inventory turnover is always favorable.
- Using ending rather than average balances when turnover requires averages.
- Treating higher ROE as superior operations without checking leverage.
- Ignoring MD&A, notes, controls, audit opinions, cash flow, and unusual items.
- Treating non-GAAP adjusted income as equivalent to GAAP income.

## Worked Example
Rainbow Paint has current assets of `$1,500,000`, current liabilities of `$750,000`, sales of `$5,000,000`, COGS of `$3,400,000`, average receivables of `$375,000`, and average inventory of `$600,000`.

- Working capital = `$750,000`; current ratio = `2.0`; quick ratio = `1.0` when quick assets are `$750,000`.
- Receivables turnover = `$5,000,000 / $375,000 = 13.3`; days' sales in receivables = about `27.4` days.
- Inventory turnover = `$3,400,000 / $600,000 = 5.7`; days' sales in inventory = about `64.4` days.
- If net income is `$245,000`, interest is `$105,000`, average assets are `$3,508,000`, and average equity is `$1,803,000`, ROA is about `10.0%` and ROE about `13.6%`.

Interpret these ratios with credit terms, industry, seasonality, leverage, and asset quality rather than in isolation.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start with the stakeholder's decision, not a memorized ratio.
2. Use horizontal, vertical, and common-size analysis before explaining change.
3. Examine current-asset quality, not only the current ratio.
4. Use averages and context for turnover and return ratios.
5. Read annual-report narrative, controls, audit opinion, cash flow, and unusual items with the numbers.
