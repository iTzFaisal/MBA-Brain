# Glossary

**Alternative hypothesis (`H1` or `Ha`)** - The claim that contradicts the null and determines a left-, right-, or two-tailed test (Ch 8).

**ANOVA** - Analysis of variance; compares between-group variation with within-group variation to test whether several means are all equal (Ch 12).

**Bayes' theorem** - A rule that updates a prior probability using the likelihood of observed evidence and the total evidence probability (Ch 4).

**Bias** - A systematic tendency in selection, measurement, response, or analysis that makes data misrepresent a target population (Ch 1).

**Binomial distribution** - A model for successes in a fixed number of independent binary trials with constant success probability (Ch 5).

**Central limit theorem** - For suitable random samples, the distribution of sample means becomes approximately normal as sample size grows, with standard error `sigma/sqrt(n)` (Ch 6).

**Chi-square distribution** - A nonnegative, right-skewed reference distribution used for variance procedures and categorical count comparisons (Ch 7, 8, 11).

**Coefficient of determination (`r^2`)** - The fraction of sample response variation explained by a fitted linear relationship; it is not a causal percentage (Ch 10).

**Confidence interval** - An interval-producing procedure intended to capture a fixed population parameter at a stated long-run confidence level (Ch 7).

**Confounding** - A design condition in which effects of different factors cannot be separated (Ch 1).

**Control chart** - A time-ordered display with a centerline and control limits used to detect process instability (Ch 14).

**Correlation** - Association between variables; correlation alone does not establish causation (Ch 1, 10).

**CVDOT** - Center, Variation, Distribution, Outliers, Time; a checklist for exploring a data display (Ch 2, 15).

**Degrees of freedom** - The number of independent values available after constraints such as a fixed mean or margins are accounted for (Ch 3, 7-13).

**Expected value** - The probability-weighted mean of a random variable, `E(X) = sum[xP(x)]` (Ch 5).

**F distribution** - A nonnegative ratio distribution used for comparing variances and for ANOVA (Ch 9, 12).

**Hypothesis test** - A procedure that evaluates how unusual sample evidence would be if a null model were true (Ch 8).

**Independence** - A relationship in which knowing one event or variable does not change the probability distribution of the other (Ch 4, 11).

**Interquartile range (`IQR`)** - The middle-half spread, `Q3 - Q1`, relatively resistant to extreme values (Ch 3).

**Level of measurement** - Nominal, ordinal, interval, or ratio structure that limits meaningful operations and method choices (Ch 1, 15).

**Margin of error (`E`)** - The critical value multiplied by the standard error in a confidence interval (Ch 7).

**Median** - The middle ordered value, or the mean of the two middle values; it is resistant to extremes (Ch 3).

**Null hypothesis (`H0`)** - The equality-based reference claim used to construct a sampling distribution for a test (Ch 8).

**Normal distribution** - A continuous symmetric bell-shaped model determined by its mean and standard deviation (Ch 6).

**Nonparametric method** - A procedure that avoids a particular population-distribution requirement, but still has design and method-specific assumptions (Ch 13).

**P-value** - The probability, assuming `H0`, of a result at least as extreme as the observed result in the direction specified by `H1` (Ch 8).

**Parameter** - A numerical feature of a population, such as `p`, `mu`, `sigma`, or `sigma^2` (Ch 1, 7).

**Poisson distribution** - A count model for independent random occurrences at a stable rate over a defined time, distance, area, or volume interval (Ch 5).

**Population** - The complete collection of individuals or measurements targeted by a study (Ch 1).

**Power** - The probability of rejecting a false null for a specified alternative, `1 - beta` (Ch 8).

**Random assignment** - Using chance to place subjects into treatment groups, supporting causal comparisons (Ch 1, 12).

**Random sample** - A sample selected by a probability mechanism intended to support population representation (Ch 1, 7).

**Regression residual** - The observed response minus its fitted response, `e = y - y_hat` (Ch 10).

**Relative frequency** - A class or category count divided by the total count, `f/n` (Ch 2).

**Sample** - A selected subcollection of a population (Ch 1).

**Sampling distribution** - The probability distribution of a statistic across repeated samples of the same size and design (Ch 6).

**Significance level (`alpha`)** - The probability of a Type I error, rejecting a true null hypothesis (Ch 8).

**Standard deviation** - A same-unit measure of typical distance from the mean; sample `s` uses `n - 1` in the variance denominator (Ch 3).

**Standard error** - The standard deviation of a sampling distribution, such as `sigma/sqrt(n)` for a sample mean (Ch 6, 7).

**Statistic** - A numerical feature calculated from a sample, such as `phat`, `xbar`, `s`, or `s^2` (Ch 1, 7).

**Type I error** - Rejecting a true `H0`; its probability is `alpha` (Ch 8).

**Type II error** - Failing to reject a false `H0`; its probability is `beta` for a specified alternative (Ch 8).

**z score** - A standardized distance from a mean in standard-deviation units, such as `z = (x - mu)/sigma` (Ch 3, 6).
