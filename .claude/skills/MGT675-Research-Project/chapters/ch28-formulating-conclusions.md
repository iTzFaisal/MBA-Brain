# Chapter 28: Formulating Conclusions

## Core Idea
A conclusion closes the logic of the literature review by returning to the topic or question, its importance, the main findings of the critique, the conclusions those findings support, and what the results mean for the next research, design, grant, change project, or practical decision. It should synthesize the review rather than introduce unsupported evidence.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Five-part conclusion recap**: Restate the **topic or question**, the **reason it is important**, the **main points revealed by the critique**, the **conclusions**, and the **so what or next step**. Use the five parts as a completeness check, not as rigid headings.
- **Research-design impact conclusion**: Use when the review supports a proposed study. State what the literature suggests, identify limitations or unavailable data, and show how those constraints change the population, observations, measures, or methods the study can use.
- **Future-research bridge**: Use when the literature exposes unanswered questions or unexplored factors. Name the gap and explain why the next investigation would clarify the issue rather than merely listing future topics.
- **Evidence-to-action conclusion**: Use for an applied review or change project. Connect the supported conclusion to the decision or design that follows, while preserving the limits of the literature.

## Key Concepts
- **Conclusion**: The final synthesis of what the review established and what it means.
- **Restatement**: A concise return to the question or topic without copying the introduction.
- **Critique result**: The main strengths, weaknesses, patterns, and limitations found in the literature.
- **Research-design impact**: The way literature findings and constraints alter how a study can be conducted.
- **Future research**: A justified next investigation arising from an unresolved question or gap.
- **Limitation**: A boundary on what the reviewed evidence can support.
- **"So what?"**: The practical or scholarly significance of the conclusion.
- **Citation-free closing synthesis**: The chapter's examples place no citations in the final summary passage because the evidence has already been discussed and the ending draws together the review's own points.

## Mental Models
- **Think of the conclusion as a return trip.** Follow the path from the opening question through the evidence and back to the decision that question was meant to inform.
- **Use limitations as design inputs.** A gap is useful when it changes what should be measured, observed, sampled, or tested next.
- **End with consequence, not repetition.** Restate the core finding briefly, then answer what the finding permits someone to do or investigate.

## Anti-patterns
- Adding a new major source or argument that the body never examined.
- Restating the introduction without stating what the critique revealed.
- Making causal or universal claims that exceed the studies, samples, and methods reviewed.
- Listing future research topics without linking them to a documented gap or limitation.
- Ending without answering why the review matters or how it affects the next step.

## Worked Example
For a review of technology-supported distance learning, a conclusion can state that the traditional model treats technology as a teacher while the newer model treats it as a resource and facilitator. It can summarize benefits such as flexibility, cost, and accelerated learning, then acknowledge that long-term outcomes and cooperative learning remain underexamined. The "so what" is conditional: a cyber program may offer both educational flexibility and social interaction, but the next study should test long-term adjustment and collaboration rather than assume those benefits. The conclusion thus informs research design and preserves the literature's limitations.

## Key Takeaways
1. Close the loop on topic, importance, critique findings, conclusions, and next step.
2. Use limitations to shape future research or project design.
3. Keep the conclusion faithful to the evidence and avoid introducing new unsupported claims.
4. State the "so what" explicitly so the reader knows why the review matters.
5. Use the body of the review for citations and the conclusion for a clear, integrated closing synthesis when the evidence is already traceable.
