# Appendix 3A: Free Cash Flows

## Core Idea
Free cash flow (FCF) is the cash generated by operations after taxes and after the firm funds its operating working capital and long-term asset investments. Positive FCF can be distributed to creditors and shareholders; negative FCF requires investors to provide additional financing.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three-step free-cash-flow procedure**
  - When to use: Measure cash available to all capital providers after maintaining and growing the operating business.
  - How: (1) Compute after-tax cash flows from operations: `EBIT + depreciation - income taxes`. (2) Compute the change in net operating working capital (NOWC): `change in current assets - change in non-interest-bearing current liabilities`. (3) Compute investment in long-term assets using increases in gross fixed assets and other long-term assets, including intangibles.
- **Free cash flow equation**
  - When to use: Combine the three pieces into a decision-ready cash measure.
  - How: `FCF = after-tax cash flows from operations - increase in NOWC - increase in long-term assets`. A decrease in either investment is added. Include a non-operating cash loss when the chapter's presentation identifies one.
- **Operating working-capital boundary**
  - When to use: Decide which current liabilities are operating financing versus investor financing.
  - How: Include accounts payable and accrued operating expenses as non-interest-bearing current liabilities in NOWC. Exclude interest-bearing short-term debt; it belongs in financing cash flows.
- **Financing-cash-flow reconciliation**
  - When to use: Explain how FCF was distributed or how a deficit was funded.
  - How: Track interest paid, dividends, changes in interest-bearing debt, and common-stock issuance or repurchase. Under the source/use sign convention, positive FCF is matched by financing cash outflows of the same magnitude; negative FCF is funded by investors.

## Key Concepts
- **Free cash flows**: Cash available after operating expenses, taxes, operating working-capital investment, and long-term asset investment.
- **After-tax cash flows from operations**: `EBIT + depreciation - income tax expense`; depreciation is added back because it is noncash.
- **Net operating working capital (NOWC)**: Operating current assets less non-interest-bearing current liabilities.
- **Non-interest-bearing current liabilities**: Operating financing such as accounts payable and accrued wages or expenses.
- **Long-term asset investment**: Cash invested in gross fixed assets, goodwill, trademarks, and other long-term assets.
- **Financing cash flows**: Cash paid to or received from lenders and shareholders.
- **Positive FCF**: Operations fund investment and leave cash for distribution to investors.
- **Negative FCF**: Operations do not cover investment needs, so debt or equity investors must supply cash.

## Mental Models
- **Cash waterfall**: First generate after-tax operating cash, then fund NOWC, then fund long-term assets; only the remainder is free.
- **Operating liabilities as spontaneous financing**: Supplier credit and accrued operating expenses provide cash support without being treated as interest-bearing investor capital.
- **Match the source to the use**: An increase in an operating asset uses cash; an increase in an operating liability supplies cash. Keep signs explicit.
- **FCF is capital-neutral operating cash**: Exclude interest from after-tax operating cash because FCF is measured before distributing cash between debt and equity providers.

## Anti-patterns
- **Use accounting net income as FCF**: Net income includes interest, depreciation, and working-capital timing that do not represent cash available to all investors.
- **Use statement-of-cash-flows operating cash without checking the definition**: The appendix's after-tax operating cash flow is EBIT-based and is not the same measure as the statement-of-cash-flows total.
- **Subtract all current liabilities from current assets**: Exclude interest-bearing short-term debt from NOWC; classify it as financing.
- **Use net rather than gross fixed assets to measure investment**: Depreciation is noncash and should not obscure the cash spent on gross assets.
- **Forget non-operating cash losses**: A loss outside regular operations may still be a cash outflow and must be included when computing the final FCF.
- **Treat positive FCF as idle cash by definition**: It represents cash available for distributions, debt repayment, repurchase, or other owner-approved uses.

## Worked Example
For Walmart, the appendix computes the following amounts in millions:

```text
After-tax operating cash = EBIT + depreciation - taxes
                         = 20,437 + 10,529 - 5,261 = 25,705

Change in current assets = 59,664 - 57,689 = 1,975 use
Change in non-interest-bearing current liabilities
                         = 70,541 - 63,008 = 7,533 source
Decrease in NOWC         = 7,533 - 1,975 = 5,558 source

Long-term asset investment = 11,169 + 3,082 = 14,251 use
Non-operating loss          = 3,136 use
FCF = 25,705 - 3,136 + 5,558 - 14,251 = 13,876
```

Walmart therefore generated $13.876 billion of positive FCF. Its financing cash flows were an equal $13.876 billion net distribution: interest and dividends, debt repayment net of new short-term borrowing, and equity changes absorbed the surplus.

## Key Takeaways
1. FCF measures cash left after both operating and long-term investment, not merely cash from operations.
2. Start with EBIT, add depreciation, and subtract taxes; do not subtract interest at this stage.
3. Measure NOWC with operating current assets and non-interest-bearing operating liabilities.
4. Use gross fixed-asset increases plus other long-term asset increases to capture investment cash outflows.
5. Positive FCF is available to creditors and shareholders; negative FCF identifies a financing requirement.
6. Reconcile FCF to financing cash flows to verify that every surplus or shortfall has an investor-side destination.
