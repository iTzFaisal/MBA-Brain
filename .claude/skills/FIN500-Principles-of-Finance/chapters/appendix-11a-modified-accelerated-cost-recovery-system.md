# Appendix 11A: The Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System

## Core Idea
The **Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System (MACRS)** is a tax-depreciation system that accelerates deductions into earlier years. Depreciation itself is not a cash flow, but earlier deductions reduce taxes sooner, increasing the present value of project cash flows. MACRS changes the timing of the tax shield, not the asset's total depreciable amount.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **MACRS recovery-class method**
  - **When to use:** Use for tax cash-flow analysis when bonus depreciation is unavailable, expires, or is not selected.
  - **How:** Identify the asset's property class under the asset-depreciation-range approach, identify the recovery period, and multiply the depreciable basis by the MACRS percentage for each recovery year:
    `Tax depreciation_t = depreciable basis * MACRS percentage_t`
    The basis includes the asset cost plus expenses necessary to place it in operating order.
- **Half-year convention**
  - **When to use:** Use for personal property such as machinery under the standard MACRS convention.
  - **How:** Treat the asset as placed in service or disposed of at the midpoint of the taxable year. Take one-half year of depreciation in the first and final tax years, so a 3-year class asset spans four calendar years and a 5-year class asset normally has a final deduction in year 6.
- **Midmonth convention**
  - **When to use:** Use for real property such as buildings.
  - **How:** Treat property as placed in service in the middle of the month. Allow one-half month of depreciation in the service month and the final month.
- **Tax-book separation**
  - **When to use:** Use when financial-statement depreciation differs from tax depreciation in a capital-budgeting model.
  - **How:** Use the tax books, not shareholder reporting books, to determine tax deductions and project cash flows. A firm may use accelerated depreciation for tax purposes and straight-line depreciation for financial reporting.

## Key Concepts
- **MACRS:** Tax system that assigns accelerated depreciation percentages by property class and recovery year.
- **Asset depreciation range (ADR):** Classification approach that groups assets by type and industry to determine the recovery period.
- **Recovery period:** The tax period over which MACRS deductions are allocated.
- **Depreciable basis:** Asset cost plus necessary shipping, installation, and other costs to put it into operating order.
- **Averaging convention:** Rule limiting the amount of first- and last-year depreciation.
- **Half-year convention:** Personal-property assumption of six months in both the first and final service years.
- **Midmonth convention:** Real-property assumption of half a month in the first and final service months.
- **Depreciation tax shield:** Tax reduction generated by a deductible depreciation expense; economically valuable because it increases after-tax cash flow.
- **Bonus depreciation:** Extreme accelerated depreciation under which qualifying property can be expensed immediately in the applicable tax year.

## Mental Models
- **Depreciation is a tax clock, not a cash payment.** The cash effect is the tax reduction caused by the deduction.
- **Earlier tax savings are more valuable.** With a positive time value of money, accelerating the same total deductions increases present value by deferring taxes to later dates.
- **Same total, different timing.** Bonus depreciation and MACRS can produce the same total depreciation while producing different annual FCFs and NPVs.

## Anti-patterns
- **Subtracting depreciation as if it were a cash outflow:** Depreciation is noncash; add it back after calculating its tax effect.
- **Using book depreciation for tax cash flows:** Capital-budgeting cash flow depends on the tax depreciation schedule.
- **Applying a full first-year deduction automatically:** Personal property may be subject to the half-year convention, and real property to the midmonth convention.
- **Confusing acceleration with extra depreciation:** MACRS changes when deductions occur, not the total amount depreciated.
- **Ignoring basis additions:** Shipping and installation needed to place the asset in service belong in depreciable basis.

## Worked Example
Equipment costs `$12,000` and is assigned to the 5-year property class. Under the MACRS percentages shown in the source, the deductions are:

`Year 1: 20.0% * $12,000 = $2,400`

`Year 2: 32.0% * $12,000 = $3,840`

`Year 3: 19.2% * $12,000 = $2,304`

`Year 4: 11.5% * $12,000 = $1,380`

`Year 5: 11.5% * $12,000 = $1,380`

`Year 6: 5.8% * $12,000 = $696`

The deductions total `$12,000`. The sixth-year deduction is the other half of the half-year convention. For a firm with tax rate `T`, the annual cash-flow benefit is approximately `depreciation_t * T`; the accelerated pattern makes that benefit arrive earlier than under straight-line depreciation.

## Key Takeaways
1. Determine the tax recovery class before calculating MACRS depreciation.
2. Multiply depreciable basis by the applicable recovery-year percentage.
3. Apply the half-year convention to personal property and the midmonth convention to real property.
4. Use tax depreciation, not financial-reporting depreciation, in capital-budgeting FCFs.
5. Value acceleration because it postpones taxes and increases the present value of tax savings.
6. Treat bonus depreciation as an alternative timing rule, not as additional total depreciation.
