# Glossary

**Absolute advantage** - Ability to produce a good with fewer inputs than another producer (Ch 2).
**Absorption** - Domestic spending, `C + I + G`; the trade balance is output minus absorption (Ch 14, 16).
**Appreciation** - Market-driven rise in a currency's value; it tends to make exports costlier in foreign currency (Ch 11, 14, 15).
**Arbitrage** - Near-riskless simultaneous buying where a currency is cheap and selling where it is expensive (Ch 11, 12).
**Autarky** - No-trade state in which domestic production equals domestic consumption (Ch 2).
**Balance of payments** - Double-entry record of residents' economic transactions with the rest of the world (Ch 10).
**Capital and financial account** - Record of international asset purchases, sales, capital transfers, and official transactions (Ch 10).
**Capital controls** - Restrictions on cross-border asset transactions that preserve policy room but distort allocation (Ch 15, 16).
**Capital mobility** - Sensitivity of international investment flows to interest-rate differences (Ch 16).
**Comparative advantage** - Lower relative opportunity cost in producing a good or task (Ch 1, 2, 3, 9).
**Conditionality** - Lending requirements tying IMF support to prescribed corrective policies (Ch 7, 17).
**Currency crisis** - Heavy pressure against a currency, visible in reserve loss, capital flight, or speculative selling (Ch 15).
**Currency risk** - Uncertainty in the domestic-currency value of a foreign-currency cash flow (Ch 11, 17).
**Current account** - Goods, services, income, and unilateral-transfer balance (Ch 10).
**Debt service/export ratio** - Scheduled interest and principal divided by export earnings (Ch 17).
**Depreciation/devaluation** - Depreciation is market-driven currency loss; devaluation is an official fixed-rate reduction (Ch 14, 15).
**Dumping** - Selling abroad below the home-market price or below the applicable production-cost benchmark (Ch 5, 6).
**Economic integration** - Removing restrictions on regional trade, payments, and factor mobility (Ch 8).
**Effective rate of protection** - Percentage change in domestic value added caused by output and input tariffs (Ch 4).
**Exchange rate** - Price of one currency in units of another; quote convention determines its interpretation (Ch 11).
**Exchange-rate pass-through** - Share of an exchange-rate change transmitted to trade prices (Ch 14).
**Export-led growth** - Development strategy using world demand, competition, scale, and comparative advantage (Ch 7).
**Factor-endowments theory** - Countries export goods intensive in their relatively abundant factors (Ch 3).
**Fixed exchange rate** - Official currency value defended with reserves, policy, or capital restrictions (Ch 13, 15).
**Foreign direct investment** - Overseas investment creating or acquiring controlling productive capacity (Ch 7, 9).
**Foreign exchange market** - Network where currencies and currency-denominated claims are traded (Ch 11).
**Forward contract** - Agreement today to exchange currency at a set future rate (Ch 11).
**Free trade area** - Regional bloc removing internal barriers while members retain separate external policies (Ch 8).
**Heckscher-Ohlin theory** - Factor-abundant countries export goods intensive in their abundant factors (Ch 3).
**Impossible trinity** - A country cannot sustainably combine free capital flows, a fixed rate, and monetary independence (Ch 15).
**Increasing-cost model** - Bowed production frontier in which expanding specialization raises marginal opportunity cost (Ch 2).
**International reserves** - Foreign assets used to settle external deficits or intervene in currency markets (Ch 13, 17).
**J-curve effect** - Depreciation may worsen the trade balance before quantities adjust and later improve it (Ch 14).
**Law of one price** - Identical tradable goods tend toward one common-currency price when arbitrage is feasible (Ch 12).
**Marshall-Lerner condition** - Depreciation improves trade in the long run when `|eta_M| + |eta_X| > 1` (Ch 14).
**Monetary approach to the balance of payments** - External imbalance reflects a gap between desired money holdings and money supplied (Ch 13, 14).
**Monetary union** - Integration stage with a common currency and unified monetary policy (Ch 8).
**Multinational enterprise** - Firm with cross-border operations, ownership or management, and substantial foreign activity (Ch 9).
**Nontariff trade barrier** - Non-tariff measure that restricts, redirects, or raises the cost of trade (Ch 5).
**Openness** - ` (Exports + Imports) / GDP `, a compact measure of trade integration (Ch 1).
**Optimum currency area** - Region where one currency's benefits exceed the value of separate adjustment tools (Ch 8).
**Optimum tariff** - Large-country tariff balancing terms-of-trade gain against protective and consumption losses (Ch 4).
**Overshooting** - Short-run exchange-rate movement larger than its eventual long-run adjustment (Ch 12).
**Partial specialization** - Continued production of both goods because costs rise or trade is incomplete (Ch 2).
**Purchasing-power parity** - Long-run exchange-rate benchmark based on relative national price levels (Ch 12).
**Quota rent** - `(Domestic price - world price) x quota quantity`, allocated by licensing or bargaining (Ch 5).
**Real exchange rate** - Nominal rate adjusted for relative price levels; a competitiveness measure (Ch 11).
**Specific-factors theory** - Short-run distributional model where some factors cannot move between industries (Ch 3).
**Stolper-Samuelson theorem** - A higher product price raises the return to its intensive factor and lowers the other factor's return (Ch 3).
**Tariff** - Tax imposed when a product crosses a national boundary (Ch 4).
**Tariff escalation** - Higher duties on processed goods than on their raw-material inputs (Ch 4, 7).
**Tariff-rate quota** - Low tariff within a quantity threshold and high tariff above it (Ch 5).
**Terms of trade** - Relative price at which exports exchange for imports (Ch 2, 4, 8).
**Trade balance** - Export receipts minus import expenditures, `X - M` (Ch 10, 14).
**Trade creation** - Regional integration replacing inefficient domestic production with lower-cost partner imports (Ch 8).
**Trade diversion** - Regional integration replacing cheaper outsider imports with costlier partner imports (Ch 8).
**Transfer pricing** - Internal MNE prices that allocate reported profit among affiliates and jurisdictions (Ch 9).
**World Trade Organization** - Institution administering multilateral rules for goods, services, intellectual property, and disputes (Ch 6).
