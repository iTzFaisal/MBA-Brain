## Core Frameworks and Mental Models

### 1. Define PM Correctly
Use **performance management** for a continuous cycle of identifying, measuring, developing, and aligning individual and team performance. Treat **performance appraisal** as one assessment component. If the system is an annual form with no ongoing goals, feedback, coaching, development, or renewal, it is appraisal rather than PM.

### 2. Run the Six-Stage Cycle
Use the recurring sequence **prerequisites -> performance planning -> performance execution -> performance assessment -> performance review -> performance renewal and recontracting**. Start with organizational strategy and job analysis. Plan results, behaviors, and development. Support execution with resources and feedback. Gather evidence across the period. Review past performance, present consequences, and future goals. Recontract as conditions change. A broken link weakens the whole system.

### 3. Cascade Strategy into Work
Trace **organizational strategic plan -> unit plan -> job description -> individual/team performance**. Use environmental analysis to identify external opportunities/threats and internal strengths/weaknesses. In gap analysis, opportunity + strength is leverage, opportunity + weakness is a constraint, threat + strength is a vulnerability, and threat + weakness is a problem. Keep mission present-oriented and vision future-oriented. Every criterion should be observable and verifiable.

### 4. Diagnose Before Intervening
Use `Performance = Declarative Knowledge x Procedural Knowledge x Motivation`. Check facts and requirements, capability to execute, and choice/level/persistence of effort. Then inspect HR practices and the work environment. Do not prescribe training for a motivation or context problem, and do not expect contingent pay to repair capability.

### 5. Choose Measures by Causality and Control
Use behavior measures when outcomes are delayed, causally ambiguous, or outside employee control. Use results when skilled employees control outputs and the behavior-result link is clear. Use traits mainly for placement or exceptional restructuring, not routine appraisal. Include both task and contextual performance. Treat results as evidence, not proof: `performer + situation -> behavior -> result`.

### 6. Build Defensible Results and Behavior Measures
Use **accountability -> objective -> performance standard**. Make objectives specific, measurable, achievable, results-based, time-specific, challenging, agreed, prioritized, flexible, and limited in number. Define competencies as KSA clusters and rate observable indicators. Prefer absolute systems for developmental feedback; use comparative systems for administrative sorting. Use BARS when concrete critical-incident anchors justify the design effort. Treat forced distribution as conditional, not a default.

### 7. Match Information to Purpose
Design appraisal forms for their purpose and test simplicity, relevance, descriptiveness, adaptability, comprehensiveness, definitional clarity, communication, and time orientation. Match sources to firsthand knowledge: supervisors for strategic/results criteria, peers for teamwork, subordinates for leadership, self for activity/development, and customers for service. Use source-specific feedback. When ratings affect administration, publish weights and use mechanical scoring.

### 8. Implement as Change
Before launch, communicate what PM is, how it fits strategy, what it means for each audience, how it works, and what consequences follow. Provide a two-level appeals process. Separate intentional rating distortion from cognitive error: change incentives for leniency/severity/central tendency; use RET, FOR, BO, diaries, and practice for memory and calibration errors. Pilot the complete system with a representative group and monitor reactions, implementation quality, evidence, follow-up, satisfaction, cost, and results.

### 9. Turn Evaluation into Development and Coaching
Every developmental plan needs an objective, activity, resource, deadline, standard, evidence, and follow-up. Use the Feedforward Interview to discover conditions behind prior success. Use 360-degree feedback primarily for development and only with anonymity, trust, behavioral specificity, interpretation, and follow-up. Coach with the employee, not to the employee. Describe facts before evaluation, document observable patterns, give early and specific feedback, and use the past-present-future review model.

### 10. Align Rewards and Law
Use the Expectancy-Instrumentality-Valence model: `Motivation = Expectancy x Instrumentality x Valence`. Reward what the organization genuinely wants, not what is easiest to count. Check for gaming, short-termism, teamwork damage, intrinsic-motivation crowd-out, and protected-group effects. Build legal defensibility through job-related criteria, standardized application, timely notice, employee voice, appeals, diverse raters, training, documentation, and adverse-impact monitoring.

### 11. Manage the Me and the We
Verify a group is a genuine team before applying team PM. Measure individual task performance, individual contextual contribution, and whole-team performance. Classify work/service, project, and network teams by task complexity and membership configuration, then vary measures and timing. Include effectiveness, efficiency, learning/growth, satisfaction, process, outcomes, and stakeholder perspectives. Keep measures limited and rewards aligned with actual team behavior.
