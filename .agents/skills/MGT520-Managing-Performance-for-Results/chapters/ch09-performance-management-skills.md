# Chapter 9: Performance Management Skills

## Core Idea
Effective PM depends on an ongoing coaching cycle: set developmental goals, identify resources, implement, observe and document behavior, give actionable feedback, and revise goals. Managers must also handle review meetings, defensiveness, discipline, and termination fairly.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four coaching principles**: Build trust and collaboration; treat the employee as the source and director of change; understand the employee as unique and whole; facilitate growth rather than taking control.
- **Four coaching styles**: Driver (direct), persuader (benefit and buy-in), amiable (empathy and relationship), and analyzer (logic, rules, and procedure).
  - When to use: Adapt style to employee and situation rather than relying on one default.
- **Coaching process**: Set developmental goals -> identify resources/strategies -> implement -> observe/document -> give feedback -> revise goals and repeat.
- **Feedforward/actionable feedback**: Use past behavior as evidence while focusing the conversation on future action.
  - When to use: Correct behavior without turning the discussion into an identity judgment.
- **Past-present-future review model**: Review past evidence, present reward implications, and future goals/actions.
- **Fight-or-flight defensiveness model**: Fight appears as blame/aggression; flight appears as withdrawal, subject changing, or unreflective agreement.
- **Decision-making leave**: A possible paid day of contemplation before formal discipline, used only within policy and with senior support.

## Key Concepts
- **Coaching**: Collaborative, ongoing direction, support, feedback, correction, and development.
- **Employee agency**: The employee directs behavioral change; the manager facilitates.
- **Adaptive coaching**: Adjusting style and intervention to the person and context.
- **Behavior-based documentation**: Specific, observable, job-related behavior, outcomes, frequency, and context.
- **Feedback gap**: Mutual avoidance of negative feedback that falsely signals acceptable performance.
- **Core self-evaluation**: Self-esteem, self-efficacy, emotional stability, and locus of control affecting feedback response.
- **Performance review meeting**: Structured conversation linking evidence, development, rewards, appeals, and follow-up.
- **Progressive discipline**: Escalating intervention when performance or conduct does not improve.

## Mental Models
- Coach **with**, not **to**, the employee; ownership improves learning and commitment.
- Use **describe before evaluate**: establish observable facts, then discuss meaning and consequences.
- Make feedback **timely, frequent, specific, verifiable, private, consequential, and pattern-based**.
- Choose transfer, demotion, discipline, or termination based on diagnosed capability, motivation, context, repeated standards, and documented history.

## Anti-patterns
- **One coaching style for everyone**: Creates person-style mismatch.
- **Character labels**: "Lazy" or "poor attitude" cannot be verified or acted upon fairly.
- **Late or rare feedback**: Problems intensify and the annual review becomes punitive.
- **One-sided records**: Recording only positives or negatives distorts the full period.
- **Identity-level criticism**: Makes employees defend their worth rather than change behavior.
- **Threatening review meetings**: Triggers defensiveness and blocks learning.
- **Ignoring poor performance or bypassing HR**: Increases operational, legal, and termination risk.

## Worked Example
**Matt's retail coaching:** Matt completed a sale and helped the customer find the right size, but omitted an add-on offer, contact entry, and promotion notice. The coach first recognized the successful service, described the omissions, checked whether training was understood, heard Matt's concern about rushed customers, and collaborated on a faster process. The next step is to define the exact behaviors Matt will practice and review their recurrence and sales impact.

## Key Takeaways
1. Establish trust and employee ownership before prescribing change.
2. Adapt driver, persuader, amiable, and analyzer behaviors.
3. Treat goals, observation, documentation, and feedback as one loop.
4. Document behavior and evidence, never personality judgments.
5. Give early, private, specific, actionable feedback, including negative feedback.
6. Separate development, rewards, and discipline where feasible, while remaining consistent and respectful.
