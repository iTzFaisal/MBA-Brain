# Chapter 3: Performance Management and Strategic Planning

## Core Idea
Strategic planning creates a resource-allocation blueprint; PM makes it actionable by cascading mission, vision, goals, and strategies through units, jobs, and individual or team performance. Strategy is incomplete until employees can identify the behaviors, results, and development that advance it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Environmental analysis**: Scan external economic, political/legal, social, technological, competitor, customer, and supplier factors, plus internal structure, culture, politics, processes, and size.
  - When to use: Before selecting or revising strategy.
  - How: Classify findings as opportunities, threats, strengths, or weaknesses, then test their implications for capability and performance.
- **Gap analysis**: Opportunity + strength = **leverage**; opportunity + weakness = **constraint**; threat + strength = **vulnerability**; threat + weakness = **problem**.
  - When to use: Prioritize strategic action.
  - How: Exploit leverage, close constraints, use strengths against vulnerabilities, and treat problems as urgent risks.
- **Strategic alignment/cascading model**: Organizational strategic plan -> unit strategic plan -> job description -> individual and team performance.
  - When to use: Audit whether strategy reaches daily work.
- **Mission and vision distinction**: Mission expresses present purpose and scope; vision expresses an envisioned future.
  - When to use: Draft or test direction-setting statements before converting them into goals.
- **Performance-management design-choice matrix**: Choose behavior versus results, participation level, time horizon, individual versus team criteria, developmental versus administrative emphasis, and performance versus tenure/position pay according to strategy.

## Key Concepts
- **Strategic plan**: Blueprint for allocating resources toward organizational goals.
- **Mission**: Why the organization exists and what it currently does.
- **Vision**: What the organization aspires to become.
- **Goal**: Specific target that operationalizes mission and vision.
- **Strategy**: The game plan for achieving goals.
- **Strategic consensus**: Agreement on common strategies across units.
- **Alignment**: Traceable connection from organizational priorities to job tasks, KSAs, and performance criteria.
- **Observable and verifiable criterion**: A criterion that can be seen or evidenced rather than inferred from vague impressions.

## Mental Models
- Use the **cascade test**: if a priority cannot be expressed as a unit contribution, job responsibility, and observable criterion, it is not operational.
- Think of mission as **why now** and vision as **where next**; do not use future aspirations as immediate job criteria without translation.
- Choose measures from **strategy first**, not from a generic form.

## Anti-patterns
- **Plan without execution**: Statements without follow-through create cynicism.
- **Firm/individual disconnect**: Employees know the strategy but cannot identify their contribution.
- **Detached job descriptions**: Generic tasks and KSAs cannot guide aligned performance.
- **Strategically irrelevant criteria**: The system measures what is easy rather than what the strategy needs.
- **Unobservable criteria**: Ratings become subjective and difficult to defend.

## Worked Example
**Key Bank of Utah alignment:** A corporate goal to provide profitable, superior service was narrowed into branch objectives for stronger management and lower losses. The HR manager's job was then defined around staff expertise, effectiveness, motivation, and succession depth. The artifact shows the necessary chain: corporate priority -> unit strategy -> job responsibilities -> individual behaviors, results, and development.

## Key Takeaways
1. Cascade strategy through units and job descriptions before selecting appraisal criteria.
2. Use gap analysis to distinguish exploitable strengths from capability constraints.
3. Draft mission and vision iteratively as environmental information changes.
4. Agree on strategies before setting unit goals when possible.
5. Build support through participation, transparent mechanics, visible sponsorship, and a credible answer to "What is in it for me?"
