# Patterns and Techniques

## Strategy-to-Performance Cascade
**When to use**: A PM system does not clearly support organizational priorities.
**How**: Translate mission and vision into strategies and goals, unit plans, job tasks and KSAs, then individual/team behaviors, results, and development.
**Trade-offs**: Improves strategic congruence but requires rewriting generic job descriptions and communicating the logic.

## Six-Stage PM Cycle
**When to use**: Designing or diagnosing the full operating system.
**How**: Prerequisites -> planning -> execution -> assessment -> review -> renewal/recontracting.
**Trade-offs**: Creates continuity and learning, but requires regular evidence and manager time.

## Accountability-Objective-Standard Chain
**When to use**: Converting a job into measurable results.
**How**: Cluster tasks into accountabilities; define objectives; state quality, quantity, cost, and time standards.
**Trade-offs**: Specific and defensible, but can encourage narrow target fixation if side effects are ignored.

## Competency Indicators
**When to use**: The manner of performance matters.
**How**: Define the competency as a KSA cluster and rate observable positive, negative, or missing behaviors.
**Trade-offs**: Improves feedback and validity, but requires behavior definition and rater calibration.

## Deliberate-Practice Loop
**When to use**: Building expertise or a specific capability.
**How**: Set improvement intent; focus during performance; seek expert feedback; update mental models; repeat.
**Trade-offs**: Builds skill through iteration, but needs access to feedback and practice opportunities.

## Source-Assignment Matrix
**When to use**: Multiple people observe different parts of performance.
**How**: Assign supervisors to strategic/results criteria, peers to teamwork, subordinates to leadership, self to activity/development, and customers to service; preserve source-specific feedback.
**Trade-offs**: Broadens evidence but adds collection cost and source-specific bias.

## Mechanical Weighted Scoring
**When to use**: Ratings affect pay, promotion, or other administrative decisions.
**How**: Publish dimensions and weights; calculate `rating x weight`; combine according to the stated formula.
**Trade-offs**: Transparent and defensible, but a precise formula cannot rescue invalid dimensions or weights.

## Rater Calibration
**When to use**: Raters disagree or common errors are recurring.
**How**: Use RET for error awareness, FOR for shared anchors, BO for observation and recording, and self-leadership/role-play for confidence.
**Trade-offs**: Improves consistency, but training must be paired with accountability and incentives for accuracy.

## Two-Level Appeals
**When to use**: Employees challenge a rating or claim procedure was not followed.
**How**: Let HR mediate first; escalate to an unbiased peer/manager panel with defined timelines, confidentiality, and final authority.
**Trade-offs**: Builds justice and trust, but requires independence and administrative capacity.

## Personal Developmental Plan
**When to use**: Appraisal identifies a gap, strength, or future-role need.
**How**: Define objective, activity, resource, deadline, standard, evidence, and supervisor follow-up.
**Trade-offs**: Converts feedback into action, but unmeasured activities can become symbolic compliance.

## Feedforward Interview
**When to use**: Find repeatable conditions behind successful performance.
**How**: Ask for a specific success story; identify personal and contextual enablers; select future behaviors and conditions to reproduce.
**Trade-offs**: Strength-based and practical, but should complement diagnosis of persistent deficiencies.

## Coaching Feedback Protocol
**When to use**: An observable behavior needs reinforcement or correction.
**How**: Describe the event; establish facts; evaluate; explain consequences; identify pattern; ask for employee-generated improvement; agree on follow-up.
**Trade-offs**: Preserves dignity and agency, but requires timely documentation and willingness to address negative behavior.

## Contingent-Pay Stress Test
**When to use**: Considering a bonus, commission, gain-sharing, or merit plan.
**How**: Check strategy fit, controllability, expectancy, instrumentality, valence, gaming, teamwork, quality, legal effects, and protected-group impact.
**Trade-offs**: Aligns incentives when valid, but can crowd out intrinsic motivation or reward A while hoping for B.

## Team Me/We Scorecard
**When to use**: Work is genuinely interdependent.
**How**: Measure individual task performance, individual contextual contribution, and team effectiveness, efficiency, learning/growth, and satisfaction.
**Trade-offs**: Balances accountability and collaboration, but requires type-specific measures and a limited measure set.
