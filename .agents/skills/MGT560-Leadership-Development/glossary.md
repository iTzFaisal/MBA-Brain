# Glossary

**Adaptive Leadership / Adaptive Leadership Questionnaire** - Heifetz mobilizes learning on adaptive challenges; questionnaire: 30-item 360 development measure (Ch 11).
**Authentic Leadership / Authentic Leadership Questionnaire (ALQ)** - Self-awareness, internal morals, balanced processing, relational transparency; ALQ measures all four (Ch 9).
**Bennis and Nanus four strategies** - Vision, social architecture, trust, creative self-development (Ch 8).
**Big Five** - Neuroticism, extraversion, openness, agreeableness, conscientiousness; no universal recipe (Ch 2).
**Bion's group assumptions** - Dependency, fight-flight, and pairing are unconscious group processes under anxiety (Ch 12).
**Blake and Mouton Leadership Grid** - Rates concern for results and people from 1 to 9; 9,9 is team management (Ch 4).
**Burns's transformational/transactional distinction** - Transactional exchanges outcomes; transformational raises motivation and morality (Ch 8).
**CCRT** - Core Conflictual Relationship Theme: recurring wish, expected response, response pattern (Ch 12).
**Clinical Paradigm** - Psychodynamic lens for unconscious motives, defenses, and relationship patterns (Ch 12).
**Culture in the Workplace questionnaire / Dimensions of Culture Questionnaire** - Hofstede and GLOBE-related cultural reflection measures; not societal research (Ch 16).
**Emotional intelligence / MSCEIT** - Perceive, use, understand, and manage emotion; MSCEIT measures ability (Ch 2).
**Ethical lenses / Five principles of ethical leadership** - Consequences, duties/rights, character; respect, service, justice, honesty, community (Ch 13).
**Expectancy theory** - Motivation depends on expectancy, instrumentality, and valence (effort-performance, performance-outcome, outcome value) (Ch 6).
**Four Is / Full Range model / Multifactor Leadership Questionnaire (MLQ)** - Four transformational factors plus transactional and laissez-faire; MLQ assesses the range (Ch 8).
**French and Raven six bases of power** - Referent, expert, legitimate, reward, coercive, and information power (Ch 1).
**Gender-Leader IAT** - Reaction-time comparison of male-leader/female-supporter pairings with their reverse; awareness tool, not diagnosis (Ch 15).
**George's practical approach** - Purpose, values, relationships, self-discipline, compassion, and a True North (Ch 9).
**GLOBE** - Nine cultural dimensions and six leadership profiles across 62 societies; profiles are hypotheses (Ch 16).
**Great Man theory** - Innate exceptional qualities explain leadership; deterministic and exclusionary (Ch 2).
**Greenleaf's servant-as-leader test / Spears's ten characteristics / Liden servant model / Servant Leadership Questionnaire (SLQ)** - Serve first; seven behaviors develop followers, ethics, empowerment, and community value; SLQ measures them (Ch 10).
**Hackman and Larson-LaFasto team criteria** - Assess team reality, goal, members, norms, support, coaching, commitment, climate, and principled leadership (Ch 14).
**Hill Model for Team Leadership** - Monitor, diagnose, intervene on task/relational needs inside/outside, then re-monitor (Ch 14).
**Implicit Leadership Theory** - Followers recognize leaders partly by matching culturally shaped prototypes (Chs 1, 16).
**Katz's Three-Skill Approach / Skills Inventory** - Technical, human, and conceptual skills; Inventory scores 23-30 high, 14-22 moderate, 6-13 low (Ch 3).
**Kohlberg's stages** - Six moral-reasoning stages: preconventional, conventional, postconventional; reasoning does not guarantee conduct (Ch 13).
**Kotter's management-leadership distinction** - Management creates order; leadership creates direction, alignment, motivation, and change (Ch 1).
**Kouzes and Posner five practices / Leadership Practices Inventory (LPI)** - Five behavioral practices; LPI is a 30-item 360-degree measure (Ch 8).
**Leader-Member Exchange (LMX) / Vertical Dyad Linkage (VDL) / leadership making / LMX 7** - Dyads move from formal roles toward reciprocal partnerships; LMX 7 measures relationship quality (Ch 7).
**Leadership Archetype Questionnaire (LAQ)** - Maps eight complementary archetypes, including strategist, builder, innovator, coach, and communicator (Ch 12).
**Leadership Behavior Questionnaire** - Twenty items profile task and relationship behavior; odd/even sums range 10-50 (Ch 4).
**Leadership labyrinth** - Barriers interact across entry, development, promotion, and elite selection (Ch 15).
**Leadership process definition** - Individual influence over a group toward a common goal; process, influence, group, purpose required (Ch 1).
**Leadership Trait Questionnaire (LTQ)** - Self plus five observer ratings of 14 characteristics; exposes perception gaps (Ch 2).
**McGrath's critical leadership functions** - Diagnose deficiencies, remedy problems, forecast change, prevent harmful change (Ch 14).
**Michigan and Ohio State studies / LBDQ** - Employee/production orientation and initiating-structure/consideration dimensions; LBDQ uses follower ratings (Ch 4).
**Mumford Skills Model** - Problem-solving, social judgment, and knowledge produce performance, shaped by experience and environment (Ch 3).
**Path-Goal theory / Path-Goal Leadership Questionnaire** - Clarify goals, clear obstacles, fit behavior to followers and tasks; questionnaire measures tendencies (Ch 6).
**Perceived Leader Integrity Scale (PLIS)** - Thirty-item observer measure; reverse-score 9 and 30; lower totals mean higher perceived ethics (Ch 13).
**Role congruity theory** - Communal stereotypes of women conflict with agentic leader prototypes, producing doubt and backlash (Ch 15).
**Situational Leadership II / Situational Leadership Questionnaire** - Match directive/supportive S1-S4 to development D1-D4; scenarios assess style and flexibility (Ch 5).
**Stogdill's trait-situation findings** - Traits contribute, but leadership depends on situation; no universal trait set (Ch 2).
**Team Excellence Survey / Collaborative Team Leader Instrument** - Paired ratings diagnose team excellence and collaborative leadership (Ch 14).
**Toxic triangle** - Destructive leaders, susceptible followers, and conducive environments enable destructive leadership (Ch 13).
**Walumbwa four-component model** - Authentic leadership requires self-awareness, internalized morals, balanced processing, relational transparency (Ch 9).
