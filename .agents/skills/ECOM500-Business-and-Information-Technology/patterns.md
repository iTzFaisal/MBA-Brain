# Patterns

## Business-model and process reset
**When to use**: A digital initiative is being framed as a technology purchase, or a process has excessive handoffs and delay.
**How**: Start with customer value and revenue logic; map inputs, activities, and deliverables; apply BPR before automation.
**Trade-offs**: Radical redesign can unlock speed and convenience but creates change, training, and adoption risk. (Ch 1)

## SMAC convergence
**When to use**: A service must connect engagement, mobility, insight, and continuous access.
**How**: Combine social influence, mobile context, analytics, and cloud services around one customer or employee journey.
**Trade-offs**: Integration increases relevance and reach but expands privacy, identity, security, and data-quality obligations. (Ch 1)

## IS decision ladder
**When to use**: Choose or diagnose an information system.
**How**: Trace IPOS and feedback; match repeatable transactions to TPS, structured reports to MIS, what-if decisions to DSS, and strategic trends to EIS.
**Trade-offs**: More flexible systems support ambiguity but require better data, models, skills, and governance. (Ch 2)

## Governed data pipeline
**When to use**: Operational data must support analytics or shared decisions.
**How**: Separate OLTP from OLAP; use ETL, CDC, deduplication, quality checks, MDM, access controls, retention, and disposition.
**Trade-offs**: Preparation improves trust and performance but adds latency, cost, stewardship, and process discipline. (Ch 3)

## Edge-to-cloud IoT
**When to use**: Sensors generate high-volume data or local action is urgent.
**How**: Collect and prepare at the device or edge, transmit relevant results, analyze broadly in the cloud, and govern storage and retention.
**Trade-offs**: Edge lowers latency and bandwidth cost but distributes software, security, and operational complexity. (Ch 4)

## Risk-based defense in depth
**When to use**: Protect critical assets against uncertain cyber threats.
**How**: Estimate probability, vulnerability, and impact; use prevention, detection, containment, recovery, correction, and awareness across layered controls.
**Trade-offs**: Redundancy improves resilience but costs money and can add user friction; compare expected loss and BIA impacts. (Ch 5)

## Decision-first analytics lifecycle
**When to use**: A team wants a dashboard or model without a clear action.
**How**: Define the decision and constraints, then capture, store, model, analyze, communicate, deploy, monitor, and reiterate.
**Trade-offs**: A simple descriptive answer may be faster and more explainable; predictive or prescriptive methods add value only when the decision needs them. (Ch 6)

## Conversation and discovery loop
**When to use**: Build social reach, service, search visibility, or recommendations.
**How**: Listen, talk, energize advocates, support users, embrace ideas; combine SEO/SEM, semantic metadata, and content/collaborative/hybrid recommendations.
**Trade-offs**: Personalization improves discovery but faces cold start, sparsity, overspecialization, privacy, and trust limits. (Ch 7)

## Omnichannel promise
**When to use**: Customers move between store, Web, and mobile before, during, or after purchase.
**How**: Share customer identity, inventory, price, payment, fulfillment, service, and returns; use stores as supply-chain nodes.
**Trade-offs**: Convenience and personalization rise, but integration, reverse logistics, channel conflict, consent, and security become harder. (Ch 8)

## Lean flow and inventory fit
**When to use**: Reduce operational waste while preserving service levels.
**How**: Map value, create flow, establish pull, seek perfection; balance EOQ, JIT, safety stock, carrying, ordering, and shortage costs.
**Trade-offs**: JIT reduces carrying cost but increases stockout and supplier-disruption exposure. (Ch 9)

## Enterprise integration
**When to use**: Multiple functions, partners, or customer stages use conflicting copies of data.
**How**: Select ERP, SCM, CRM, EKM, ECM, or ESP by process need; evaluate fit and TCO, then govern leadership, change, training, and adoption.
**Trade-offs**: A shared repository creates shared reality but increases migration, implementation, dependency, and organizational-change risk. (Ch 10)

## AI maturity and augmentation
**When to use**: A proposed AI use case needs a realistic path from experiment to production.
**How**: Classify the task and learning signal, test a bounded POC, validate data and explainability, then progress from Awareness through Active, Operational, Systematic, and Transformational maturity.
**Trade-offs**: AI scales pattern recognition and physical action, but bias, opacity, privacy, job transition, and monitoring costs favor augmentation before replacement. (Ch 11)

## Strategy-to-service sourcing
**When to use**: Plan IT investment, choose a vendor, or adopt an emerging technology.
**How**: Align strategy, map Porter forces and value chain, define the five IT strategy components, scan technology, run a POC, contract measurable SLAs, transition, and review with BSC/ITIL practices.
**Trade-offs**: Outsourcing and cloud can add capability and scale but reduce control and increase vendor, integration, privacy, and exit risk. (Ch 12)

## Governed delivery
**When to use**: Build, buy, replace, or change a system.
**How**: Establish need and feasibility, choose SDLC method and conversion mode, baseline scope/time/cost, create WBS and risk register, control changes, test, train, accept, and capture lessons.
**Trade-offs**: Waterfall suits stable requirements; Agile/DevOps suit volatility but require collaboration, product ownership, and operational maturity. (Ch 13)

## Ethical and sustainable design
**When to use**: A system changes rights, work, resource use, or environmental impact.
**How**: Assign responsibility, accountability, and liability; test profits/people/planet, quality of life, consent, accessibility, civil rights, full lifecycle footprint, and the four Rs.
**Trade-offs**: More safeguards can slow deployment or raise cost, but hidden social, legal, and environmental liabilities are not savings. (Ch 14)
