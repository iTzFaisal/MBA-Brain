# Chapter 14: IT Ethics and Local and Global Sustainability

## Core Idea
Technology changes who has power, whose rights are exposed, how people work, and what the planet absorbs. Use explicit ethical accountability and the sustainability lens of profits, people, and planet to design, deploy, monitor, and retire ICT responsibly. A people-first approach makes technology a partner rather than an imposed system.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three Basic Tenets of Business and IT Ethics**: Use before approving a data practice, system, or decision.
  - **Responsibility** means anticipating and accepting consequences. **Accountability** means determining who is responsible for actions. **Liability** means having the right to recover damages for actions committed by people, organizations, or systems.
- **Ethics and compliance program**: Use to turn values into enforceable behavior.
  - Build comprehensive reporting, clear individual roles and accountability, and full and effective oversight by management and the board. Add a code of ethics, internal controls, training, monitoring, and a protected whistleblower channel.
- **Triple Bottom Line**: Use to evaluate financial, social, and environmental risks, obligations, and opportunities together.
  - Manage **profits, people, and planet** and their interactions: **fair trade** connects people and profits, **eco-efficiency** connects profits and planet, and **environmental justice** connects people and planet.
- **Four "Rs" of environmental sustainability**: Use when designing operations, products, and ICT assets: **reduce, reuse, recycle, recover**.
  - Measure the carbon, water, energy, waste, and biodiversity effects of production and use, then reduce impact at the source and recover useful materials or energy.
- **Six elements of quality of life**: Use to test human impact: communications, relationships, work-life balance, healthcare, education, and travel. Assess both convenience and unintended effects such as isolation, cognitive overload, job loss, or surveillance.
- **People-first approach to technology**: Use throughout systems development and service design.
  - Involve users at every stage, align business value with user values and needs, and design for a partnership among people, profit, and planet. Avoid **tech-clash**, the love/hate response when technology's value does not align with users' expectations and values.
- **Accenture Technology Vision 2020 trends**: Use as a post-COVID design prompt: **The I in Experience**, **AI and Me**, **The Dilemma of Smart Things**, **Robots in the Wild**, and **Innovation DNA** (including DARQ: distributed ledgers, AI, extended reality, and quantum computing).

## Key Concepts
- **Ethics**: Values and customs defining what a group considers right and wrong.
- **Ethical behavior**: Acting consistently with accepted values and moral principles.
- **Code of ethics**: Principles and rules that guide decisions and distinguish right from wrong.
- **Civil rights**: Political and social freedoms and equality protected by law.
- **Protected class**: A group that cannot lawfully be targeted for discrimination or harassment, such as by age, race, disability, gender, national origin, genetic information, or religion.
- **Social media discrimination**: Using social-network information or targeting to treat candidates less favorably based on protected characteristics.
- **Insider fraud**: Fraud enabled by an employee or trusted insider's access and position.
- **Sustainability**: Creating conditions in which humans and nature can exist in productive harmony for present and future generations.
- **Greenhouse gas (GHG)**: Carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, and other compounds that absorb infrared radiation and retain heat.
- **Food security**: Reliable access to enough safe, nutritious food for a healthy, active life.

## Mental Models
- **Every capability has a victim and an owner**: Before using tracking, AI, monitoring, or automation, name who bears the risk and who is accountable for correcting harm.
- **Triple bottom line, not green marketing**: Evaluate profit, people, and planet together; a cheaper system that shifts health, privacy, or environmental costs elsewhere is not sustainable.
- **ICT has a double effect**: The same network or device can improve access, safety, and resilience while increasing surveillance, distraction, emissions, and exclusion.
- **People first, then technology**: Start with user needs, values, accessibility, and agency; select technology that serves them and explain how it operates.

## Anti-patterns
- **Secret collection or surveillance**: La Liga's hidden microphone and location monitoring violated transparency and consent even though it pursued piracy.
- **Using protected information in recruitment**: Social-media microtargeting or informal screening can violate civil rights and create discriminatory outcomes.
- **Monitoring employees without transparency**: Keyloggers and activity tools can destroy trust and engagement if policy, purpose, access, and limits are unclear.
- **Counting only device energy**: Smartphones can have 85-95% of their emissions impact in production; sustainable ICT must include manufacturing, infrastructure, data centers, use, and disposal.
- **Deploying ICT without local capacity**: Developing-country projects fail when electricity and networks, STEM education, or usable policies and regulations are missing.

## Worked Example
**La Liga and the Marauder app.** La Liga wanted to identify bars illegally streaming matches and used its Android app to activate microphones and location data. It identified about 600 bars and detected illegal streams, but the spy function was not clearly described, users were not alerted each time recording began, and withdrawal of consent was not available. Spain's AEPD fined La Liga EUR 280,000 for violating GDPR Article 5.1, which requires lawful, fair, transparent processing, and Article 7.3, which requires that consent can be withdrawn. Applying the chapter's lens: La Liga had a commercial responsibility to protect intellectual property, but it failed accountability and exposed itself to liability because the data practice was covert and disproportionate.

## Key Takeaways
1. Make responsibility, accountability, and liability explicit before deployment.
2. Use codes, controls, training, monitoring, oversight, and whistleblower protection to make ethics operational.
3. Evaluate sustainability through profits, people, planet, fair trade, eco-efficiency, and environmental justice.
4. ICT can improve communication, remote work, healthcare, education, and travel while also causing distraction, isolation, job loss, and privacy harm.
5. Measure the full ICT carbon footprint, then use the four Rs, renewable energy, smart systems, and efficient networks to mitigate it.
6. Use ICT for clean water, sanitation, food security, and poverty reduction, but address infrastructure, STEM, and policy barriers first.
7. Design post-COVID technology around trusted human-AI partnerships, transparent smart devices, contactless robotics, and resilient digital services.
