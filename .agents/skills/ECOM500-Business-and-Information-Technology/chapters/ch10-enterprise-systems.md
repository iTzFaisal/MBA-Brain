# Chapter 10: Enterprise Systems

## Core Idea
Enterprise systems replace isolated applications with integrated processes and shared data. They can improve planning, productivity, customer and supplier relationships, and traceability, but success depends primarily on business-process fit, leadership, change management, training, and disciplined implementation.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Six types of enterprise systems**: Use the list to locate an integration problem and select the right enterprise capability: **enterprise resource planning (ERP)**, **supply chain management (SCM)**, **customer relationship management (CRM)**, **enterprise knowledge management (EKM)**, **enterprise content management (ECM)**, and **enterprise social platforms (ESPs)**.
  - How: Connect the systems through a central data repository so departments, suppliers, vendors, and customers work from current information.
- **ERP Selection Rules**: Use before committing to a package.
  - How: Select a solution targeted to the company's requirements; evaluate vendor strengths and weaknesses; meet each vendor for a hands-on demo; calculate total cost of ownership (TCO), including implementation, customization, training, hardware, bandwidth, and staff.
- **ERP Implementation Critical Success Factors**: Use to govern the implementation rather than blaming the software.
  - How: Focus on business processes and requirements, set realistic expectations and measures, assign strong program management, obtain executive support and buy-in, and invest in organizational change management and training. The factors interact; neglecting one raises failure risk.
- **Four Technology Stages of Digital Adoption**: Use to sequence supply-chain digitization.
  - How: Build **digital connectivity**, then **automation**, **advanced analytics**, and finally **artificial intelligence**. Each stage is a foundation for the next.
- **Three Supply Chain Flows**: Use to diagnose an SCM bottleneck.
  - How: Track material or product flow, information flow, and financial flow together. Add EDI to automate order, invoice, fulfillment, shipping, and payment exchanges.
- **Five key phases of the CRM process**: Use to manage acquisition and retention as one lifecycle.
  - How: Identify new prospects; track and monitor sales opportunities; organize and engage current customers; establish relationships through communication; manage the customer lifecycle to encourage repeat purchases and referrals.
- **Koenig's Three Stages of KM Development**: Use when an EKM investment is not producing knowledge reuse.
  - How: Establish information technology and communication networks; change HR practices and corporate culture so people share; then improve taxonomy and content management so relevant content can be retrieved.

## Key Concepts
- **Enterprise system**: Large-scale application software supporting business processes, information flows, reporting, and analytics.
- **Legacy system**: An older IS maintained because it still fulfills critical needs.
- **ERP**: Integrated business-process management software using a shared database across functions.
- **SCM**: Management of material, data, and payment flows among supply-chain companies.
- **CRM**: A strategy and system for making and maintaining efficient customer interactions.
- **Customer lifetime value (CLV)**: The value a customer contributes over the entire relationship.
- **Always-on supply chain**: A connected, high-velocity supply network using continuous information and analytics.
- **Electronic data interchange (EDI)**: Electronic exchange of structured business documents between authorized partners.
- **EKM**: A system that captures, organizes, and creates knowledge from explicit and tacit organizational assets.
- **ECM**: Processes, strategies, and tools for securely capturing, organizing, storing, delivering, and routing structured and unstructured content.

## Mental Models
- **Shared repository, shared reality**: Integration is not just connecting screens; it is establishing authoritative data that multiple functions can use simultaneously.
- **One-up, one-back visibility**: An SCM system manages the links a company touches, so improve collaboration with the immediate supplier and customer before attempting to control the whole chain.
- **CRM is socio-technical**: Technology and strategy are necessary, but people and processes determine adoption. Sales users must see the system as helping them close and retain profitable customers.
- **CLV before loyalty**: Retain customers when lifetime revenue exceeds service, return, promotion, and acquisition costs. The source formula is `(Average Value of a Sale) x (Number of Repeat Transactions) x (Average Retention Time)`.

## Anti-patterns
- **Replacing legacy systems without process redesign**: A new platform can automate bad workflows and preserve the original problem.
- **Selecting by license price or vendor demo alone**: TCO, fit, scale, integration, training, and vendor stability matter.
- **Putting IT in charge of CRM without business users**: Users may reject an inflexible system, limiting adoption and value.
- **Treating SCM as linear and internal**: Delayed information, supplier silos, and weak reverse logistics damage the entire chain.
- **Capturing content without retrieval or culture**: An EKM repository that employees cannot find or are not rewarded for using becomes another silo.

## Worked Example
**Walmart Food Traceability Initiative.** Walmart's old food traceability process moved only one step up and one step back and could take days or weeks to identify a product's source. Walmart, IBM, and suppliers implemented a permissioned blockchain using a decentralized, encrypted, permanent shared record. After pilot testing, tracing a food item from store to source took seconds. The value depended on supplier participation: Walmart required leafy-green suppliers to capture digital traceability events. The result was faster recalls, less unnecessary product destruction, real-time visibility, better shelf-life management, and restored customer trust.

## Key Takeaways
1. Enterprise systems create value by integrating processes and data, not by accumulating applications.
2. Treat ERP implementation as organizational redesign with executive sponsorship, realistic measures, and training.
3. Manage product, information, and financial flows together in SCM.
4. Build CRM around the five customer phases and profitable lifetime relationships.
5. Sequence supply-chain digitization from connectivity to AI; do not skip the data foundation.
6. Use EKM, ECM, and ESPs to preserve knowledge, control content, and coordinate distributed work.
