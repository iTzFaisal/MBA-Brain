# Open-Economy Judgment Aid

## Start With The Question

| If the question asks... | Start with... | Do not confuse it with... |
|---|---|---|
| Why goods or tasks cross borders | Opportunity cost, factor endowments, scale, demand | Who wins from a policy |
| Whether a restriction is worthwhile | Welfare decomposition and incidence | A protected industry's gain |
| Why a currency moved | Quote convention plus time horizon | PPP as a daily forecast |
| Whether an external deficit is dangerous | Current account, saving-investment, and financing quality | The merchandise balance alone |

## Trade Theory Rules

- If `OC_A(good) < OC_B(good)`, A has comparative advantage and should tend to export it.
- If the proposed terms of trade are outside the two domestic opportunity costs, the exchange is not mutually beneficial.
- If production costs rise with specialization, expect partial specialization and converging relative prices.
- If countries are similar, add economies of scale, differentiated products, overlapping demands, and product-cycle effects before forcing a Heckscher-Ohlin explanation.
- If using Heckscher-Ohlin, compare factor ratios, not absolute stocks; then use Stolper-Samuelson to identify distributional conflict.

## Trade Policy Decision Tree

1. Is the claim about a price wedge or a quantity cap?
   - Price wedge: analyze a tariff and its revenue, transfers, and deadweight losses.
   - Quantity cap: calculate quota rent and identify its owner.
2. Are imported inputs used?
   - Yes: compute `e = (n - a*b)/(1-a)`; the nominal rate is insufficient.
3. Is the country large enough to lower the foreign export price?
   - Yes: test a possible terms-of-trade gain, then subtract efficiency losses and retaliation.
   - No: treat producer and government gains as domestic transfers; protective and consumption losses are national costs.
4. Is the problem an import surge, subsidy, dumping claim, or foreign barrier?
   - Match safeguard, countervailing duty, antidumping duty, or Section 301 respectively; follow downstream users before endorsing relief.

## Integration And Development

- Customs union: `net static gain = trade creation - trade diversion`; intra-bloc trade growth alone proves nothing.
- If a common currency removes exchange costs, ask what absorbs asymmetric shocks: labor mobility, flexible prices, fiscal transfers, or similar cycles.
- Infant-industry protection is credible only with a learning/scale mechanism, measurable milestones, and an exit.
- Commodity price boom plus inelastic supply and demand signals volatility risk; diversify, process, and build institutions.

## External Accounts

- Record a foreign receipt as credit and a payment as debit, with the matching asset/liability entry.
- `CA deficit = (G-T) + (I-S)`: durable correction requires lower government dissaving, higher private saving, lower investment, or a combination.
- A current-account deficit is a flow; net debtor status is a stock. Ask what the inflow finances.
- High domestic interest rates can attract capital, appreciate the currency, and create a current-account deficit; capital flows may lead trade flows.

## Currency And Adjustment

- With `E = domestic currency / foreign currency`, rising `E` means domestic depreciation. Check this before saying "stronger" or "weaker."
- Short run: use real returns and expectations. Long run: use relative inflation/productivity and PPP. If the rate moves too far initially, suspect overshooting.
- For depreciation, pass the three gates: `|eta_M| + |eta_X| > 1`; allow for the J-curve; require `Y` to rise relative to `A` in `B = Y-A`.
- Payable in foreign currency -> buy foreign currency forward. Receivable -> sell it forward. Option = premium-paid protection with upside; forward = binding certainty.

## Regime And Policy Defaults

| Constraint or target | Default judgment |
|---|---|
| Fixed rate + free capital | Monetary independence is surrendered; use fiscal policy or change the regime. |
| Floating rate + high capital mobility | Monetary policy is strengthened; fiscal stimulus is partly offset by appreciation. |
| Temporary external shock | Use reserves or managed intervention if credible; do not defend a structural imbalance forever. |
| Internal and external imbalance | Use target-instrument matching; one AD tool rarely solves two or three targets. |
| International loan | Stack credit, country, and currency risk; monitor interest relative to export growth. |

## Fast Diagnostic Tells

- Protected output rises while national welfare falls: likely a transfer plus deadweight loss.
- Imports are fixed but price rises as demand grows: likely a binding quota, not a tariff.
- Reserves fall while a peg looks increasingly fragile: reserve-loss/speculative-attack loop.
- A higher nominal interest rate accompanies depreciation: inflation or expected depreciation may be dominating the nominal yield.
- Debt relief is followed by no productive investment: liquidity was released, but repayment capacity was not created.
