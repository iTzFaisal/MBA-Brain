# Glossary

**Acceptance** — Actual understanding, support, and use of an approved change; formal approval alone is insufficient. (Ch 5)
**Adaptive capacity** — The ability to anticipate, absorb, and respond to environmental shifts while preserving useful organizational purpose and relationships. (Ch 1)
**ADKAR** — An individual adoption sequence: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement. (Ch 7)
**Ambivalence** — Mixed positive and negative reactions to a change; a learning window before a firm decision to support or oppose. (Ch 7)
**Balanced Scorecard** — A linked set of financial, customer or stakeholder, internal-process, and learning-and-growth measures. (Ch 10)
**Change Path Model** — Deszca and Ingols's four-phase guide: Awakening, Mobilization, Acceleration, and Institutionalization. (Ch 2, Ch 11)
**Change vision** — A specific, tangible picture of the near-term future produced by a defined change. (Ch 2, Ch 4)
**Congruence Model** — Nadler and Tushman's map of fit among work, people, formal organization, informal organization, strategy, environment, and outputs. (Ch 3)
**Continuous change** — Ongoing, cumulative adaptation that builds capability through repeated small adjustments. (Ch 1, Ch 8, Ch 11)
**Creeping commitment** — Gradually increasing support through small decisions, evidence, participation, and coalition building. (Ch 5)
**DICE Model** — A project-risk estimate combining Duration, Integrity of performance, senior and local Commitment, and Effort. (Ch 10)
**Discontinuous change** — Broad, disruptive, intentional change that breaks substantially with the status quo. (Ch 1, Ch 8, Ch 9)
**Environmental scanning** — Deliberate monitoring of external developments to detect threats, opportunities, and early-warning signals. (Ch 1, Ch 4)
**Force field analysis** — Assessment of driving and restraining forces, their strength and timing, and the leverage points that can shift their balance. (Ch 6, Ch 9)
**Formal organization** — Designed roles, reporting relationships, structures, systems, processes, and controls that prescribe work. (Ch 3, Ch 5)
**Gap analysis** — Comparison of present reality with a desired future to clarify the case, destination, and bridge. (Ch 2, Ch 4)
**Homeostasis** — The relatively stable balance produced by interdependent forces that resist disturbance and restore familiar patterns. (Ch 2, Ch 6)
**Individual change equation** — Support rises when dissatisfaction, perceived benefits, and probability of success together exceed perceived personal cost. (Ch 6)
**Informal organization** — Relationships, norms, culture, influence, political patterns, and spontaneous processes that shape how work actually occurs. (Ch 3, Ch 6)
**Institutionalization** — Embedding a change in processes, culture, measures, and everyday behavior so it survives a campaign. (Ch 2, Ch 10, Ch 11)
**Lewin's Three-Stage Model** — Unfreeze the old equilibrium, enact new patterns, and refreeze or re-gel a workable new equilibrium. (Ch 2)
**Mechanistic and organic designs** — A contingency continuum from specialized, rule-bound, centralized coordination to flexible, decentralized, lateral coordination. (Ch 5)
**Open Systems Approach** — Diagnosis that treats the organization as exchanging resources and information with an external environment. (Ch 3)
**Organizational readiness** — The perceived need, acceptability, confidence, support, and capacity required for an organization to undertake change. (Ch 4, Ch 7)
**Paradox management** — Holding competing requirements together, such as central direction and local agility, instead of prematurely choosing one. (Ch 1, Ch 11)
**Person x Vision x Situation** — Change-agent effectiveness as the fit among personal capability, a meaningful future, and enabling conditions. (Ch 8)
**PESTEL** — Political, economic, social, technological, ecological, and legal scanning of the external context. (Ch 1, Ch 4)
**Psychological contract** — Explicit and implicit expectations about obligations, security, status, rewards, autonomy, and fair treatment. (Ch 7)
**Readiness beliefs** — Beliefs that the gap is real, the proposal is appropriate, people can enact it, trusted people support it, and benefits matter to me/us. (Ch 4)
**Resistance** — Behavior intended to slow, block, or undermine change after opposition has become a considered judgment, not merely a question. (Ch 6, Ch 7)
**Responsibility charting** — Assignment of who is Responsible, has Approval or veto, Supports, and is Informed or consulted. (Ch 9)
**Risk Exposure Calculator** — A nine-driver, 1-to-5 diagnostic of pressure, change culture, and information-management exposure. (Ch 10)
**Schein's culture model** — Cultural diagnosis through artifacts, espoused beliefs and values, and basic underlying assumptions. (Ch 6)
**Sigmoid curve** — A performance life cycle that grows, matures, flattens, and declines; renewal should begin before crisis. (Ch 2)
**Stakeholder mapping** — A relationship-based view of affected parties, influence, motives, support or resistance, and leverage roles. (Ch 6, Ch 9)
**Sterman's Systems Dynamics Model** — A diagnostic lens for feedback loops, side effects, delays, buffers, competing goals, and nonlinear outcomes. (Ch 3)
**Strategy map** — A cause-and-effect display linking capabilities to internal processes, customer or stakeholder value, and final results. (Ch 10)
**Transition management** — Coordinating the human, structural, and operational move between old and desired states while work continues. (Ch 2, Ch 9)
**Triple bottom line** — Performance judged across economic, social, and ecological dimensions. (Ch 1)
**What/how distinction** — Separating diagnosis of the content that must change from the process that will move people and systems. (Ch 2, Ch 3)
