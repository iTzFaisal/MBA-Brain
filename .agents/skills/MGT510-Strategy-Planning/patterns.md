# Strategy Patterns

## Strategy Diagnosis Loop
**When to use**: A firm has an unclear problem or disappointing performance.
**How**: Identify current strategy; appraise performance; diagnose causes; analyze industry and capabilities; formulate; implement; learn and adjust.
**Trade-offs**: A rigorous loop improves judgment but cannot remove uncertainty or managerial choice.

## Five Forces Scan
**When to use**: Assess an industry's profit potential or forecast structural change.
**How**: Define boundaries; rate rivalry, entry, substitutes, buyers, and suppliers; document evidence; translate trends into force changes; identify lower-pressure positions.
**Trade-offs**: Strong baseline for structure, but incomplete for ecosystems, platforms, and rapid innovation.

## Profitability Diagnosis
**When to use**: Financial performance is below expectations.
**How**: Decompose ROA/ROCE into margin and turnover; trace each to costs, assets, working capital, customers, and processes; compare with rivals and time.
**Trade-offs**: Makes problems actionable, but narrow targets can create dysfunctional behavior.

## Resource-Capability Test
**When to use**: Decide whether an internal strength can support advantage.
**How**: Test relevance and scarcity; then durability, transferability, and replicability; finally identify who appropriates the returns.
**Trade-offs**: Protects against resource hype, but embedded capabilities are harder to measure.

## Value-Chain Advantage Analysis
**When to use**: Explain a cost gap or design differentiation.
**How**: Disaggregate activities; identify cost or uniqueness drivers; map linkages; include the customer's downstream value chain; redesign or outsource weak activities.
**Trade-offs**: Reveals causal mechanisms, but excessive disaggregation can hide system-level fit.

## Life-Cycle and Ambidexterity Review
**When to use**: Technology, demand, or industry structure is changing.
**How**: Diagnose life-cycle stage; match current capabilities to the stage; protect exploitation while creating exploration; define scaling or exit milestones.
**Trade-offs**: Separation protects radical exploration but can isolate it from adoption.

## Innovation Commercialization Ladder
**When to use**: An invention exists but commercialization capabilities are incomplete.
**How**: Map appropriability and complementary resources; choose licensing, outsourcing, alliance, joint venture, or internal commercialization; stage commitments and build adoption.
**Trade-offs**: More control captures more value but increases capital, integration, and execution risk.

## Make-or-Buy Decision
**When to use**: Set the boundary of the firm.
**How**: Compare market transaction costs with internal administration, incentives, scale, capability, and risk costs; consider hybrids before ownership.
**Trade-offs**: Integration reduces hold-up but sacrifices specialization, flexibility, and high-powered incentives.

## Global Configuration
**When to use**: Expand across countries or relocate activities.
**How**: Apply the National Diamond and CAGE; locate each activity; select entry mode by advantage source, tradability, capability, appropriability, and transaction costs; choose aggregation, adaptation, or arbitrage.
**Trade-offs**: Fragmentation lowers some costs but increases coordination and resilience risk.

## Diversification Screen
**When to use**: Evaluate a new business or acquisition.
**How**: Apply attractiveness, cost-of-entry, and better-off tests; name the synergy mechanism; compare ownership with contracting; test parenting advantage and complexity costs.
**Trade-offs**: Relatedness can create synergy but also costly coordination and managerial distraction.

## Corporate Parenting Review
**When to use**: Allocate capital or decide whether to retain a business.
**How**: Plot parenting upside against destruction risk; classify heartland, edge, ballast, value trap, or alien territory; choose invest, test, maintain, repair, or exit.
**Trade-offs**: Corporate intervention can add value or destroy business-level autonomy.

## External Growth Governance
**When to use**: Acquire, partner, license, or develop a capability.
**How**: Start with strategic need; choose the lowest-control mode that can transfer value; quantify synergies; design integration, learning, IP, governance, and exit.
**Trade-offs**: Acquisitions enable reciprocal integration; alliances preserve flexibility but require relational capability.
