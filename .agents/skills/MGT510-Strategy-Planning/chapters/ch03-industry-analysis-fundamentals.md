# Chapter 3: Industry Analysis: The Fundamentals

## Core Idea
Industry structure determines the potential for profit by shaping customer value, producer rivalry, and the bargaining power of buyers and suppliers. Industry analysis supports corporate choices about where to compete and business choices about how to build advantage.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Porter's Five Forces framework**: Use to explain current profitability, forecast structural change, and identify positions where competitive pressure is lower.
  - **Substitutes**: Test buyer willingness to switch and relative price-performance; close substitutes cap prices.
  - **New entrants**: Assess capital, scale, cost advantages, differentiation, distribution, legal barriers, and retaliation; returns above the cost of capital attract entry.
  - **Rivalry**: Assess concentration, competitor diversity, differentiation, excess capacity, exit barriers, fixed costs, and scale economies.
  - **Buyer power**: Assess concentration, switching costs, information, backward integration, differentiation, and the credibility of refusing a transaction.
  - **Supplier power**: Assess concentration, input uniqueness, switching alternatives, switching costs, and forward-integration threats.
- **Industry profitability forecasting**: Explain current structure, identify trends, translate each trend into changes in the forces, and judge the net effect on future returns.
- **Industry boundary analysis**: Define competition through demand-side substitution, supply-side substitution, and geographic mobility. Use narrower boundaries for positioning and wider boundaries for major investments.
- **Key success factors (KSFs)**: Ask what customers want and how firms survive competition; the intersection identifies external requirements for success.

## Key Concepts
- **Industry attractiveness**: Potential return relative to the cost of capital.
- **Horizontal competition**: Rivalry, substitutes, and entry among producers.
- **Vertical competition**: Value captured by buyers and suppliers.
- **Contestability**: Vulnerability to entry when sunk costs are low, even if concentration is high.
- **Exit barriers**: Conditions that keep weak competitors in an industry and sustain rivalry.
- **Bargaining power**: The credible ability to refuse, delay, or redirect a transaction.
- **Key success factors**: Industry-level requirements for attracting customers and surviving competition.
- **Value appropriation**: The portion of created value retained by producers after other participants claim value.

## Mental Models
- **Structure-to-profit chain**: Structure shapes competitive behavior, which shapes profitability.
- **Trend-to-force translation**: A macro trend matters strategically only after showing which force it changes.
- **Two-question KSF test**: Customer choice explains demand-side success; competitive survival explains supply-side success.
- **Pressure map**: Choose segments, relationships, or activities that reduce exposure to the strongest forces.

## Anti-patterns
- Listing PEST factors without translating them into industry effects.
- Defining an industry by a code or broad sector instead of substitution and competitive relationships.
- Extrapolating current profitability without forecasting structural change.
- Treating concentration or competitor count as a complete rivalry analysis.
- Confusing industry attractiveness with a firm's competitive advantage.
- Assuming shared KSFs require identical strategies.

## Worked Example
Airlines face strong rivalry because high fixed costs, excess capacity, and low marginal costs for filling empty seats encourage price competition. Buyers are informed and price-sensitive, substitutes constrain demand, and aircraft, fuel, airports, and labor can hold bargaining power. A viable airline must therefore improve load factors, unit costs, route economics, and price realization, while seeking positions such as loyalty, hub dominance, alliances, or underserved routes that reduce direct pressure.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start with industry structure, not an undifferentiated environmental list.
2. Use all five forces and explain the causal evidence behind each rating.
3. Forecast profitability by linking structural trends to force changes.
4. Define boundaries according to the decision being made.
5. Derive KSFs from customer choice and competitive survival.
