# Chapter 8: Industry Evolution and Strategic Change

## Core Idea
Demand growth and knowledge diffusion reshape industries, while organizational inertia prevents many incumbents from adapting. Strategy must exploit today's business while deliberately building tomorrow's capabilities.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Industry life cycle**: Diagnose introduction, growth, maturity, and decline through demand, technology, knowledge, capacity, entry/exit, and price pressure. Match capabilities and investment to the stage.
  - **Introduction**: Experiment, build legitimacy, and learn; do not optimize prematurely for scale.
  - **Growth**: Scale capacity, distribution, and process capabilities as a dominant design emerges.
  - **Maturity**: Emphasize efficiency, selective differentiation, capacity rationalization, and profitable niches.
  - **Decline**: Choose explicitly among harvesting, niche specialization, rejuvenation, and exit.
- **Dominant design**: When an industry converges on a product architecture, radical product innovation usually gives way to incremental product and process innovation.
- **Organizational inertia and adaptation**: Explain resistance through routines, social/political structures, conformity, limited search, and interdependent fit. Adapt through variation, selection, and retention.
- **Dual strategies and organizational ambidexterity**: Exploit existing competencies while exploring new opportunities. Separate exploration structurally when architectures or cultures conflict; integrate it contextually when distributed innovation is more valuable.
- **Dynamic capabilities**: Sense and shape opportunities, seize them through commitments and business models, and reconfigure assets and capabilities to maintain competitiveness.
- **Knowledge management**: Manage explicit knowledge through systems and tacit know-how through practice, mentoring, communities, and relationships. Convert and routinize knowledge without making it unnecessarily imitable.

## Key Concepts
- **Competence-enhancing change**: Builds on incumbent resources and routines.
- **Competence-destroying change**: Makes incumbent resources or routines less valuable.
- **Path dependence**: Current choices and learning constrain future options.
- **Punctuated equilibrium**: Long stability interrupted by comprehensive transformation.
- **Exploration**: Search for new opportunities, technologies, and capabilities.
- **Exploitation**: Improve and scale existing activities.
- **Dynamic capability**: Higher-order ability to build, integrate, and reconfigure operating capabilities.
- **Tacit knowledge**: Know-how expressed through performance and difficult to codify.

## Mental Models
- **Stage-to-capability test**: The capability that wins in introduction is rarely the one that wins in maturity.
- **Fit trap**: Tightly linked strategy, structure, systems, and culture make incremental adaptation difficult.
- **Two-time-zone test**: Every decision should protect current performance and create future options.
- **Variation-selection-retention loop**: Search broadly, test cheaply, retain what works, and stop what no longer fits.

## Anti-patterns
- Waiting for declining performance before acting.
- Applying mature-industry efficiency logic during an emerging technology transition.
- Forcing disruptive exploration into incumbent metrics and routines.
- Creating an innovation unit with no path to adoption or scale.
- Making isolated changes in a tightly coupled system.
- Assuming documented knowledge transfers tacit expertise automatically.

## Worked Example
An automobile incumbent facing electric vehicles should treat the shift as potentially architectural and competence-destroying. A protected EV unit can explore with different metrics while the core retains scale and reliability. Scenarios, pilots, partnerships, and staged platform commitments support sensing and seizing; plant, supplier, and skill changes are reconfigured progressively. The new unit needs explicit milestones for scaling or integration, not permanent isolation.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diagnose life-cycle stage from evidence, not industry age.
2. Match investment and KSFs to the stage and technology regime.
3. Build future capabilities before crisis makes change unavoidable.
4. Use ambidexterity to protect exploitation while enabling exploration.
5. Manage explicit knowledge with systems and tacit knowledge socially.
