# Strategy Cheatsheet

## Start Here
| If you need to... | Ask first | Then use |
|---|---|---|
| Explain poor performance | What changed in margin, turnover, cash flow, or value? | ROA/ROCE diagnosis (Ch 2) |
| Choose where to compete | Which industry has attractive structure and KSFs? | Five Forces + boundaries (Ch 3) |
| Decide how to win | What do customers value and what can we do distinctively? | KSFs + resources/capabilities (Ch 3, 5, 7) |
| Change the firm's boundary | Are market transaction costs higher than internal governance costs? | Make-or-buy / scope analysis (Ch 10) |
| Enter another country | Is the advantage country-specific or firm-specific, and how transferable is it? | CAGE + entry ladder (Ch 11) |
| Add a business | What exact synergy survives entry cost and complexity? | Three essential tests + parenting advantage (Ch 12, 13) |

## Decision Rules
- When **industry pressure is strong**, reposition toward segments, activities, or relationships where the strongest force is weaker.
- When a trend appears, do not act on the trend alone. Translate it into changes in Five Forces, KSFs, capabilities, and cash flows.
- When a resource is valuable, test **relevance -> scarcity -> durability -> transferability -> replicability -> appropriability** before calling it an advantage.
- When differentiating, start with customer willingness to pay and downstream cost/value, not feature count.
- When uncertainty and irreversibility are high, prefer staged investment, pilots, alliances, and options.
- When exploration conflicts with existing metrics and culture, separate it structurally but define an adoption path.
- When transaction-specific investment, uncertainty, and dependence are high, prefer ownership or a protected relational contract.
- When a business is attractive but the parent cannot add distinctive value, do not acquire it.
- When using BCG or GE/McKinsey, treat the matrix as a screen; test synergies, future structure, and parenting fit before allocating capital.
- When a global strategy faces distance, standardize scale-sensitive activities and adapt customer-, regulation-, and culture-sensitive activities.
- When choosing M&A versus alliance, use the minimum integration needed to transfer the resource; higher integration also raises fragility risk.

## Fast Smells
| Tell | Likely problem |
|---|---|
| Goals are ambitious but no how-to logic exists | Goal mistaken for strategy |
| SWOT list has no causal evidence | Labels replacing analysis |
| Profit is rising while ROCE falls | Capital productivity problem |
| A capability is praised but no routine or process is named | Asset/capability confusion |
| Platform is monetized before critical mass | Adoption failure |
| Acquisition case says "synergy" without mechanism or price | Premium capture risk |
| HQ equalizes investment across businesses | Political allocation |
| Innovation unit has no scale or integration milestones | Isolated exploration |
| Outsourcing removes architectural knowledge | Coordination and renewal risk |
| Global cost savings ignore coordination and resilience | Globalization penalty |
