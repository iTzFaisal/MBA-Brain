# Chapter 8: Strategy Formulation and Implementation

## Core Idea

Strategic management determines an organization's mission and long-term objectives and then implements a plan to attain them. In an MNC, the plan must connect external opportunity and threat analysis with internal capabilities, while balancing global integration against national responsiveness. Use strategy as a coordinated set of choices, not as a domestic plan copied into foreign markets. Middle managers matter because they translate broad intent into operating decisions.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Four strategic imperatives:** Use the **economic imperative** when the product is relatively homogeneous and upstream activities create most value; compete through worldwide cost leadership, differentiation, and segmentation. Use the **political imperative** when downstream marketing, sales, and service determine success; protect local niches through country-responsive or multi-domestic strategies. Use the **quality imperative** when continuous improvement and customer expectations are central; apply total quality management through cross-training, process re-engineering, empowerment, and quality-linked rewards. Use **administrative coordination** when the situation does not fit a predetermined economic or political formula; make the decision from the merits of the individual market.
- **Global Integration vs. National Responsiveness Matrix:** Map the need for worldwide scale and coordination against the need to adapt to local tastes, standards, and regulations. High integration and low responsiveness call for a **global strategy** based on standardization and price competition. Low integration and high responsiveness call for a **multi-domestic strategy**. Low pressure on both dimensions supports an **international strategy**, which transfers valuable core competencies with limited adaptation. High pressure on both calls for a **transnational strategy**, integrating production while differentiating marketing; it is strategically attractive but difficult to execute.
- **Basic steps in formulating strategy:** Use **environmental scanning** to forecast industry/market, technology, regulatory, economic, social, and political changes. Conduct **internal resource analysis** of managerial, technical, material, and financial resources, asking whether the firm can build the market's **key success factors (KSFs)**. Formulate goals in profitability, marketing, operations, finance, and human resources, then convert them into subsidiary parameters and controls.
- **CSA-FSA Matrix:** Compare **country-specific advantages (CSAs)**, such as resources, labor, institutions, and skills, with **firm-specific advantages (FSAs)**, such as technology, brands, distribution, and managerial know-how. Use the matrix to decide whether a location or the firm's proprietary capability is the primary source of advantage and whether cost leadership, differentiation, or a combination is feasible.
- **Specialized emerging-market strategies:** Use a **first-mover strategy** during privatization or liberalization when early entry can capture learning, scale, scarce partners, and a protected beachhead, but avoid premature entry before legal and political institutions work. Use a **base of the pyramid strategy (BOP)** to redesign the business model for low-income consumers through affordable, accessible products and local relationships. Use **international entrepreneurship** and **born-global firms** when innovative, proactive, risk-seeking ventures can cross borders soon after founding.

## Key Concepts (5-10)

- **Strategy implementation** provides goods and services according to the plan and requires location, entry/ownership, marketing, production, and finance decisions.
- Global integration centralizes activities to gain economies of scale, experience-curve benefits, and coordination.
- National responsiveness adapts offerings and management to segmented tastes, regulations, and local workforces.
- A KSF is necessary to compete effectively in a market niche; internal analysis asks whether the MNC owns or can acquire it.
- Location choice weighs country-level incentives and restrictions against local access to markets, labor, infrastructure, transport, power, and risk.
- A frontier market is a pre-emerging market with potentially high rewards, high risk, and relatively low correlation with the global economy.
- Functional implementation uses the four Ps in marketing, globally coordinated production networks, and finance tools such as currency options or countertrade.

## Mental Models

- **Fit, do not transplant:** Match the strategy to external pressures and internal strengths; a home-country success formula can become a local liability.
- **Outside-in, then inside-out:** Scan the environment, assess resources and KSFs, set goals, implement, and keep rescanning as conditions change.
- **The transnational tension:** Scale lowers cost, while adaptation raises relevance and often cost. A transnational strategy succeeds only when shared global functions create enough synergy to fund local variation.
- **Early entry is an option, not a virtue:** Move first when learning and partner access outweigh institutional uncertainty; otherwise preserve flexibility.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating strategic planning intensity as universally beneficial; a few affiliates may perform best with medium intensity, while highly international firms may need high intensity.
- Copying home-country pay, products, systems, or management practices without testing local responsiveness.
- Treating environmental scanning as a one-time market-entry exercise.
- Assuming incremental adaptation is enough for BOP markets; poverty often requires a fundamentally different model, distribution system, and partnership base.
- Entering before the legal, political, and institutional framework can support contracts, ownership, and repatriation.

## Worked Example

Starbucks illustrates a transnational challenge. China offered scale and a growing consumer base, but success required local legitimacy: higher employee benefits, close government relationships, a Chinese CEO, extensive local suppliers, and an Alibaba partnership that connected the Starbucks app to Taobao, Tmall, and Alipay. Italy required a different response because local espresso culture created high national-responsiveness pressure. A manager should preserve the Starbucks experience and brand FSA while adapting store format, menu, and service to local expectations. The decision is not simply “global or local”; it is which activities should be integrated and which should be localized.

## Key Takeaways

1. Formulation joins external scanning, internal resource analysis, and goal setting; implementation turns the choices into locations, entry modes, and functions.
2. The four imperatives are lenses, not mutually exclusive silos; MNCs often combine economic, political, quality, and administrative responses.
3. The global integration-national responsiveness matrix yields international, multi-domestic, global, and transnational strategies.
4. CSAs explain where advantage exists; FSAs explain what the firm can transfer or leverage.
5. Emerging markets, BOP customers, and born-global ventures require specialized strategies and disciplined learning.
