# Decision Patterns

## Global-Regional-Local Market Screen
**When to use**: Before choosing a country, region, supply chain, or expansion pace.
**How**: Map global trade, capital, migration, communication, and technology flows; then map bloc rules and local institutions, demographics, infrastructure, and economic system. Separate growth potential from investability and test both exchange-rate and PPP views.
**Trade-offs**: Global scale and regional access can lower cost, but local rules, political backlash, currency, and execution conditions can erase the apparent opportunity.

## Political-Legal-Technology Due Diligence
**When to use**: Before entry, a digital launch, a major contract, or a cross-border dispute.
**How**: Classify the political system, identify the legal foundation and actual enforcement, apply person/place/security jurisdiction questions, check FCPA exposure, national treatment, most-favored-nation treatment, regulation, connectivity, censorship, and cyber resilience.
**Trade-offs**: Speed and a simple home-country compliance model save effort; layered diligence costs more but prevents jurisdictional, regulatory, and technology surprises.

## Ethical Operating Screen
**When to use**: When local custom, cost pressure, labor sourcing, offshoring, or withdrawal creates a moral conflict.
**How**: Establish legality as a floor, test the decision through Kantian, virtue, utilitarian, and Eastern lenses, apply ISCT hyper norms, and score people, planet, and profits. Include suppliers, agents, and JV partners.
**Trade-offs**: Local legitimacy matters, but cultural relativism cannot excuse abuse. Embedded controls and measurable operations are stronger than disconnected publicity or philanthropy.

## Culture-to-Practice Translation
**When to use**: When a management practice, merger, team, or reward system is failing across borders.
**How**: Move from artifacts to norms/values to basic assumptions; use Hofstede, Trompenaars, and GLOBE to form questions about authority, uncertainty, status, time, and relationships. Separate values from practices, then validate with individuals and subcultures.
**Trade-offs**: Profiles accelerate preparation; treating them as predictions creates stereotypes, hides variation, and turns a diagnostic tool into a constraint.

## Global Core, Local Execution
**When to use**: When deciding what to standardize in products, processes, authority, HR, or communications.
**How**: Test scale, industry homogeneity, local tastes, regulation, insider knowledge, coordination burden, and subsidiary capability. Select an ethnocentric, polycentric, regiocentric, or geocentric posture, then centralize the core and adapt execution.
**Trade-offs**: Standardization improves scale and consistency; adaptation improves relevance and legitimacy but raises complexity and coordination cost.

## Context-Aware Communication
**When to use**: When a message is understood literally but not acted on, or when trust and feedback are weak.
**How**: Diagnose high- versus low-context expectations, directness, detail, affect, status, language, time, space, gaze, touch, and silence. Use both upward and downward channels; paraphrase and confirm intended meaning.
**Trade-offs**: Explicit written detail improves auditability; relationship-rich communication improves trust but takes time and may be misread by outsiders.

## Principled Cross-Cultural Negotiation
**When to use**: For a partnership, license, government relationship, or high-value commercial agreement.
**How**: Plan, build the relationship, exchange task information, persuade, and agree. Separate people from problems, uncover interests, generate options, use objective criteria, trade concessions reciprocally, and protect the BATNA.
**Trade-offs**: Distributive bargaining may capture immediate value; integrative bargaining creates options and durable relationships but requires disclosure, patience, and protection against opportunism.

## Strategy-Fit Cascade
**When to use**: When converting a market opportunity into an international strategy.
**How**: Scan the environment, assess resources and KSFs, compare CSAs with FSAs, choose among the four strategic imperatives, place the business on the Global Integration vs. National Responsiveness Matrix, set goals, implement, and rescan.
**Trade-offs**: A global strategy captures scale; a multi-domestic strategy captures local fit; an international strategy transfers competencies; a transnational strategy seeks both but demands the most coordination.

## Entry-Mode Choice
**When to use**: When selecting how much to invest, own, control, and learn in a host market.
**How**: Use export or licensing for a low-commitment test; acquisition for speed with an integration plan; wholly owned investment for control; alliance or JV for local knowledge, risk sharing, and legitimacy. Define ownership, authority, IP, targets, and exit.
**Trade-offs**: Control and investment rise together; cooperative modes add partner dependence but reduce local ignorance and exposure.

## Structure-Follows-Strategy Network
**When to use**: When international scale, geographic diversity, product complexity, or subsidiary capability makes the current structure slow.
**How**: Choose product, area, functional, mixed, or transnational network logic from the strategy. Set formalization, specialization, centralization, reporting, and information rights. Let dispersed units contribute expertise while nodes coordinate.
**Trade-offs**: A matrix can integrate competing demands but blurs accountability; a network enables learning but needs clear mandates and trust.

## Political-Risk Portfolio
**When to use**: Before committing capital or when a government, sanctioning country, or regulator can change project value.
**How**: Separate macro and micro risk; score transfer, operational, and ownership-control exposure; classify investment type; estimate relative bargaining power; stage investment and preserve alternatives. Use local sourcing, managers, JVs, and relationships for legitimacy, while protecting portable knowledge and assets.
**Trade-offs**: Integration reduces foreignness but can make assets captive; protection preserves mobility but can invite distrust and political intervention.

## Decision-Control and Quality Loop
**When to use**: To govern dispersed units without either headquarters micromanagement or local drift.
**How**: Choose, act, measure, compare, and correct. Centralize scale, proprietary technology, and interdependence; decentralize local adaptation to capable managers. Combine monthly indirect reports with periodic direct contact, and build quality through empowerment, QCCs, supplier learning, training, statistical control, and kaizen.
**Trade-offs**: Direct control is rich but expensive; indirect control scales but loses context. ROI is useful only after adjusting for currency, tax, transfer pricing, and imposed costs.

## Culturally Fit Motivation Package
**When to use**: When engagement, retention, participation, or rewards do not transfer across countries.
**How**: Diagnose needs -> drives -> goals; fix hygiene factors before adding motivators. Compare equity with the relevant other, use goal specificity and feedback, test expectancy links, and match individual/group rewards, job design, work centrality, family support, and autonomy to the workforce.
**Trade-offs**: Individual rewards emphasize equity; group rewards emphasize equality and solidarity. More autonomy can motivate capable employees but frustrate people who expect structure.

## Global Leadership and HR Lifecycle
**When to use**: For staffing, developing, rotating, or retaining international leaders and assignees.
**How**: Select talent from home, host, third-country, or inpatriate pools; assess adaptability, language, health, leadership, and family readiness. Prepare, support in-country adjustment, compensate through a fair method, teach with validated cultural assimilators, reinforce locally, and plan repatriation and knowledge transfer. Keep a universal core of integrity and purpose with local expression.
**Trade-offs**: Expatriates transfer control and expertise but cost more and can fail through poor adjustment; local and third-country talent improve legitimacy and learning but require deliberate global integration.
