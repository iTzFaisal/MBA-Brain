# Chapter 2: Financial Statements and the Accounting System

## Core Idea
An accounting system converts objective evidence about transactions into usable reports. Source documents initiate analysis; accounts classify increases and decreases; journals preserve chronology; ledgers accumulate balances; and trial balances test the arithmetic before financial statements are prepared.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Steps in Processing Transactions**: Use the full workflow: (1) identify each transaction or event from source documents, (2) analyze it with the accounting equation, (3) record it in a journal, (4) post journal information to ledger accounts, and (5) prepare and analyze the trial balance and financial statements.
- **Ledger and Chart of Accounts**: Use the ledger as the complete collection of accounts and balances. Use the chart of accounts as its indexed list: commonly `101-199` assets, `201-299` liabilities, `301-399` equity, `401-499` revenues, and `501-699` expenses. Account numbers make posting and report ordering systematic.
- **T-Account and Normal Balance Rules**: A T-account has a title, debit (left) side, and credit (right) side. Debit and credit mean directions, not good and bad. Normal balances are debit for assets, dividends, and expenses; credit for liabilities, Common Stock, and revenues.

| Account group | Increase | Decrease |
|---|---|---|
| Assets | Debit | Credit |
| Liabilities | Credit | Debit |
| Common Stock | Credit | Debit |
| Dividends | Debit | Credit |
| Revenues | Credit | Debit |
| Expenses | Debit | Credit |

- **Double-Entry Accounting**: Use for every transaction. At least two accounts are affected, at least one debit and one credit are recorded, and total debits equal total credits. The rule follows the left/right structure of the accounting equation.
- **Journalizing and Posting**: Journalizing records the date, affected account titles, debit/credit amounts, and a brief explanation in a general journal. Posting transfers each debit and credit to its ledger account and records cross-references in the posting reference (PR) column.
- **Trial Balance and Error Search**: Prepare by listing ledger balances, totaling debit and credit columns, and proving equality. If unequal, check the totals, copied balances, debit/credit placement, ledger computations, posting, and finally the original journal entry, in reverse order of processing. A transposition difference is divisible by 9, but equality does not prove that the accounts are correct.
- **Building Blocks of Analysis**: Use ratios to examine liquidity and efficiency, solvency, profitability, and market prospects. Examples are current ratio, debt ratio, profit margin, and price-to-earnings ratio.

## Key Concepts
- **Source document**: Hard-copy or electronic evidence such as a sales ticket, check, purchase order, supplier bill, payroll record, or bank statement.
- **Account**: A detailed record of increases and decreases in one asset, liability, equity, revenue, or expense item.
- **General ledger**: The complete record of a company's accounts; the accounting books.
- **Balance column account**: Ledger format that adds dates, explanations, debits, credits, and a running balance to the T-account idea.
- **Compound journal entry**: One entry affecting three or more accounts, such as a credit billing for consulting and rental services.
- **Unearned revenue**: A liability for cash received before products or services are delivered.
- **Current ratio**: `Current assets / Current liabilities`, a short-term liquidity measure.
- **Debt ratio**: `Total liabilities / Total assets`, the share of assets financed by creditors.
- **Unadjusted trial balance**: Ledger balances listed before the Chapter 3 adjustments.

## Mental Models
- **Use source-to-statement traceability**: Every reported amount should be traceable backward from statement to trial balance, ledger, journal entry, and source document.
- **Think of debit and credit as coordinates**: First identify the account type and its normal balance; then choose the direction that increases or decreases it.
- **Use the trial balance as an arithmetic gate, not an accuracy certificate**: Balanced totals permit the next step, but wrong accounts, equal wrong amounts, or omitted equal debits and credits can remain.
- **Use ratio blocks before individual ratios**: Ask whether the decision concerns short-term payment ability, long-term creditor risk, earnings generation, or market expectations before selecting a formula.

## Anti-patterns
- **Posting directly to the ledger without journalizing**: It loses the chronological, complete record needed to explain and audit entries.
- **Assuming a debit is unfavorable**: A debit increases cash and equipment but also expenses and dividends; its meaning depends on the account.
- **Treating a balanced trial balance as proof of correct statements**: A credit posted to the wrong revenue account can leave totals balanced while overstating income.
- **Recording an advance receipt as revenue**: The company owes performance; credit an unearned-revenue liability until the work is earned.
- **Preparing statements from stale ledger balances**: All journal entries must be posted before statement preparation.

## Worked Example
FastForward's five-step analysis of a $1,900 credit billing demonstrates the system:

1. **Identify**: A client is billed for $1,600 consulting and $300 rental services.
2. **Analyze**: Accounts Receivable increases $1,900; equity increases through two revenues; no cash changes.
3. **Journalize**:

```text
Dr Accounts Receivable              1,900
    Cr Consulting Revenue                         1,600
    Cr Rental Revenue                               300
```

4. **Post**: Debit Accounts Receivable and credit each revenue ledger account; enter account numbers in the journal's PR column. When the client later pays, `Dr Cash 1,900 / Cr Accounts Receivable 1,900`; the collection is not additional revenue.
5. **Summarize**: After FastForward's 16 transactions, the ledger reports assets $42,395, liabilities $9,200, and equity $33,195. Its unadjusted trial balance totals $45,300 on both sides; the income statement reports net income $3,395 and the retained earnings statement reports $3,195 after the $200 dividend. These are still unadjusted until Chapter 3.

## Key Takeaways
1. Preserve source-document evidence and analyze the equation before choosing accounts.
2. Record debits first, indent credits, explain the entry briefly, and post both sides.
3. Memorize normal balances by account type, not by the words debit and credit.
4. Use a trial balance to expose arithmetic and posting errors, then investigate its limits.
5. Build financial statements only from current, posted ledger balances.
