# Decision Cheatsheet

## Start With The Question

| Question | First tool | Check next |
|---|---|---|
| Can we pay soon? | Current ratio, acid-test | Receivable days, inventory quality, cash controls |
| Are operations profitable? | Gross margin, profit margin, ROA | Margin vs. total-asset-turnover driver |
| Can we carry debt? | Debt-to-equity, times interest earned | Maturity, collateral, earnings volatility |
| Where did cash go? | Statement of cash flows | Operating cash, capital spending, financing dependence |
| Is earnings quality durable? | Sustainable-income review | Unusual items, discontinued operations, accounting changes |

## Journalize By Timing

| Situation | Entry pattern |
|---|---|
| Cash before expense | Dr Expense / Cr Prepaid Asset |
| Cash before revenue | Dr Unearned Revenue / Cr Revenue |
| Expense before cash | Dr Expense / Cr Payable |
| Revenue before cash | Dr Receivable / Cr Revenue |

If an adjusting entry uses Cash, re-check it. If a proposed entry does not keep debits equal to credits, stop before posting.

## Inventory Rules

- If goods are owned at the reporting date, include them; check FOB terms and consignment status first.
- Under rising costs, FIFO usually gives higher ending inventory and income; LIFO gives higher COGS and lower income; weighted average smooths the result.
- Use specific identification only when unit traceability is reliable.
- Reconcile `beginning inventory + net purchases = COGS + ending inventory`.
- Use turnover and days' sales together: very low turnover may mean excess stock; very high turnover may mean stockouts.

## Cash And Receivables

- Bank side: deposits in transit are added; outstanding checks are deducted; no entry.
- Book side: fees, interest, collections, NSF items, and book errors require entries.
- Use percent-of-sales for bad-debt expense; use percent-of-receivables or aging for an ending allowance target.
- A write-off under the allowance method debits the allowance, not Bad Debts Expense.
- Factoring transfers receivable risk for a fee; pledging creates a loan and retains ownership.

## Debt And Equity

- Contract rate determines cash interest; market rate determines bond price and effective-interest expense.
- Discount: carrying value and interest expense rise toward par. Premium: both fall toward par.
- Cash dividend: record declaration and payment, not the record date.
- Stock split: change share count/par description; no journal entry and no total-equity change.
- Treasury stock is contra-equity; never report a gain or loss on the corporation's own shares.

## Cash-Flow Classification

| Purpose | Section |
|---|---|
| Runs the ordinary business | Operating |
| Buys/sells long-term assets, nontrading investments, or loan principal | Investing |
| Changes debt principal or owners' equity | Financing |

Indirect signs: add noncash expenses and losses; subtract gains; subtract increases in operating assets; add increases in operating liabilities.

## Investments And Time Value

- Trading unrealized changes go to income; AFS unrealized changes go to OCI/equity; HTM stays at amortized cost.
- Equity-method dividends reduce the investment account; they are not dividend revenue.
- Before PV/FV: draw a timeline, set a common date, and match rate frequency to period count.
