# Patterns and Procedures

## Transaction-to-Statement Trace
**When to use**: Recording or auditing any business event.
**How**: Identify the source document, analyze the accounting equation, journalize, post to the ledger, prove the trial balance, adjust, prepare statements, and close temporary accounts.
**Trade-offs**: More documentation takes time, but preserves auditability and prevents unsupported statement balances.

## Current-to-Should Adjusting Entry
**When to use**: At each reporting date for revenues or expenses crossing periods.
**How**: Determine the recorded balance, calculate the required ending balance, and record the difference. Use an income-statement account plus an asset or liability; never use Cash.
**Trade-offs**: Accrual accuracy requires estimates and counts, but avoids cash-timing distortions.

## Two-Sided Perpetual Sale
**When to use**: Recording a merchandise sale under a perpetual inventory system.
**How**: Record the customer asset and sales revenue, then record COGS and reduce Merchandise Inventory.
**Trade-offs**: Provides current inventory information; requires more transaction-level records.

## Inventory Cost Allocation
**When to use**: Assigning goods available for sale between COGS and ending inventory.
**How**: Establish ownership and inventoriable costs, select specific identification, FIFO, LIFO, or weighted average, and reconcile beginning inventory plus purchases to COGS plus ending inventory.
**Trade-offs**: Methods change timing of income, taxes, and reported assets even when total goods cost is unchanged.

## Bank Reconciliation
**When to use**: Explaining a difference between bank and book Cash.
**How**: Adjust the bank side for deposits in transit and outstanding checks; adjust the book side for fees, collections, interest, NSF items, and book errors; journalize only unrecorded book items.
**Trade-offs**: Detects errors after the fact; it cannot replace segregation, authorization, custody controls, or independent review.

## Allowance and Aging
**When to use**: Estimating material credit losses.
**How**: Choose percent of sales for current-period expense, or percent of receivables/aging for the desired ending allowance. Write off specific accounts against the allowance.
**Trade-offs**: Better matching and net realizable value require judgment and estimates.

## Note-Interest Timeline
**When to use**: Recording an interest-bearing note across reporting dates.
**How**: Mark principal, annual rate, elapsed time, maturity, and reporting cutoffs; use principal x rate x time and accrue each period's earned or incurred interest.
**Trade-offs**: Day-count conventions improve consistency but can differ from calendar-day intuition.

## Long-Lived Asset Allocation and Disposal
**When to use**: Acquiring, depreciating, improving, or disposing of plant assets.
**How**: Capitalize readiness costs, estimate salvage and useful life, select a consumption pattern, update depreciation, remove cost and accumulated depreciation, and compare proceeds with book value.
**Trade-offs**: Historical-cost allocation is consistent and verifiable but does not equal market value.

## Bond Pricing and Amortization
**When to use**: Issuing or carrying bonds at a discount or premium.
**How**: Price principal and interest at the market rate, record par plus discount/premium, then use effective interest: beginning carrying value x market rate. Bridge carrying value to par.
**Trade-offs**: Effective interest reflects economics; straight-line is simpler when immaterially different.

## Contingency Decision
**When to use**: Evaluating lawsuits, warranties, or other possible losses.
**How**: Confirm a past event, assess probability, and assess estimateability. Record when probable and estimable; disclose when reasonably possible or probable but not estimable; otherwise take no action.
**Trade-offs**: Avoids unsupported liabilities but depends on honest probability and measurement judgments.

## Cash-Flow Reconstruction
**When to use**: Preparing or checking a statement of cash flows.
**How**: Classify by operating, investing, or financing purpose; use the indirect method to reverse accrual effects; reconstruct noncash changes in long-term accounts; prove beginning cash plus net change equals ending cash.
**Trade-offs**: Indirect reporting is efficient and reconciles net income; direct reporting is more intuitive but requires more conversion work.

## Financial-Analysis Dashboard
**When to use**: Interpreting statements for a specific decision maker.
**How**: Select liquidity/efficiency, solvency, profitability, or market-prospect measures; compare dollars and percentages across time, peers, industry, and carefully chosen benchmarks; explain numerator and denominator movements.
**Trade-offs**: Ratios compress information and enable comparison, but can mislead without business-model, accounting-policy, and scale context.

## Investment Classification
**When to use**: Selecting the accounting method for a debt or equity holding.
**How**: Determine security type, holding intent, and ownership influence. Route unrealized changes to income, OCI/equity, or nowhere according to the classification; use the equity method for significant influence and consolidation for control.
**Trade-offs**: Classification reflects economics but gives management judgment a large reporting effect.

## Present-Value Timeline
**When to use**: Comparing lump sums, annuities, bonds, notes, or investment alternatives.
**How**: Set a common date, align periodic rate and number of periods, choose single-amount or annuity factors, and multiply by the actual amount or payment.
**Trade-offs**: Tables are fast but rounded; calculators or spreadsheets improve precision.

## Partnership Allocation and Liquidation
**When to use**: Allocating partnership income or winding up the partnership.
**How**: Follow the agreement, allocate income/loss before distributions, pay creditors, then distribute remaining cash by updated capital balances. Handle capital deficiencies explicitly.
**Trade-offs**: Contract-specific rules preserve fairness but make assumptions based on equal sharing unsafe.
