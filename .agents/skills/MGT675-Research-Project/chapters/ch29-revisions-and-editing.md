# Chapter 29: Revisions and Editing

## Core Idea
After peer review, substantive revision, introduction, and conclusion are complete, editing provides the final technical-quality pass. Check clarity, correctness, directness, grammar, punctuation, lists, citations, quotations, references, and style; then personalize the checklist for recurring errors.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Editing checklist**: Use the chapter's questions in order: sentence clarity; possessives and plurals; names and titles; grammar; spelling; simple and direct wording; active voice; absence of colloquial terms and extra prepositional phrases; punctuation; list formatting; citations for paraphrases; quotation marks and citations for quotations; and correctly formed references.
- **Personalized editing list**: Use after each project to add words, phrases, citation problems, or grammar errors you repeatedly make. Run the expanded list on every future review.
- **Spelling and grammar check as first pass**: Use Microsoft Word's automated check to identify obvious issues, but treat it as a starting point rather than a complete editor.
- **Reader's-eye review**: Use a final reread to judge sentence rhythm, technical wording, and fit with the type of review being written.

## Key Concepts
- **Editing**: Technical examination of a substantially complete draft for correctness and directness.
- **Revision**: Substantive improvement of logic, evidence, organization, or content before editing.
- **Active voice**: Wording that makes the actor and action direct where appropriate.
- **Colloquial term**: Informal wording that weakens professional or academic tone.
- **Prepositional phrase excess**: Unnecessary strings of phrases that make sentences indirect or difficult to follow.
- **Paraphrase citation**: Source credit required even when the wording is the reviewer's own.
- **Reference accuracy**: Correct presence and formatting of the sources cited in the review.
- **Reader's eye**: A perspective that tests whether the finished text is clear to someone who did not write it.

## Mental Models
- **Edit in layers.** Check meaning and sentence clarity before surface spelling, then verify citations and references against the final text.
- **Treat the checklist as a memory prosthetic.** Recurring errors are predictable; write them down instead of relying on attention alone.
- **Use tools for detection, judgment for correction.** Automated grammar checks find patterns, but the writer must decide whether the sentence is accurate, direct, and appropriate.

## Anti-patterns
- Editing grammar before resolving unsupported claims or broken logic.
- Trusting a spelling and grammar checker to detect all errors, citation omissions, or misleading wording.
- Leaving a citation off a paraphrase because no exact words were copied.
- Mixing lists into dense prose when separate list formatting would make the logic clearer.
- Preserving long, passive, colloquial, or prepositional wording because it sounds familiar.

## Worked Example
A final paragraph says, "It was determined by the researchers in the study that there were a number of factors which were impacting on the outcomes of the program in a way that was not entirely positive." The editing pass asks whether the sentence is clear, direct, active, and precise. A tighter version identifies the actor and result: "The study identified several factors that reduced program outcomes." The editor then checks whether the sentence is a paraphrase requiring citation, whether "reduced" accurately reflects the evidence, whether the source appears in the reference list, and whether the surrounding paragraph uses the same technical register.

## Key Takeaways
1. Perform editing after substantive peer review and revision, not instead of them.
2. Check clarity, directness, voice, grammar, spelling, punctuation, lists, citations, quotations, and references.
3. Use an automated checker for a first pass, then apply human judgment.
4. Maintain a personalized list of recurring problems and use it on future work.
5. Read the final review as a reader and verify that style matches the type of review and its audience.
