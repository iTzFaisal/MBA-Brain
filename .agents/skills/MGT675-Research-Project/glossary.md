# Glossary

**Abstract screening** - Using an article abstract to triage population, factors, perspective, method, and results before full reading (Ch 6)

**Academic literature review** - A review for coursework or study that normally requires peer-reviewed sources, thoroughness, organization, analysis, and depth (Ch 2)

**Analysis** - Examination of an article's logic, design, sample, evidence, interpretation, conclusions, and limitations (Ch 9, Ch 10)

**Analysis paragraph** - A concise unit that reports only relevant source information and judges its trustworthiness and use (Ch 11)

**Applied literature review** - A review supporting business, government, nonprofit, professional, or program planning through research and context sources (Ch 2)

**Article Analysis Checklist** - Questions testing theory, question, design, sample, sources, interpretation, conclusions, credentials, and limits (Ch 10)

**Article Analysis Form** - A working record for article method, evidence, interpretation, strengths, weaknesses, and use (Ch 6, Ch 31)

**Article Assessment Form** - A structured source record used to preserve article details and judgments during the review process (Ch 30, Ch 31)

**Attribution** - Language and citation identifying whose idea, finding, wording, or conclusion is being used (Ch 5, Ch 22)

**Bias** - An interest, omission, perspective, or imbalance that distorts evidence selection or interpretation (Ch 3)

**Boundary condition** - A population, setting, method, or other difference that limits prediction or transfer (Ch 4, Ch 19)

**Clustering** - A visual map from a central question to spokes and layers of possible factors (Ch 7)

**COLR** - Critique of Literature Reviews, a structured review of source appropriateness, content, concision, integration, and effectiveness (Ch 23)

**Comparison** - Identification of meaningful similarities in structural or substantive dimensions across sources or situations (Ch 12)

**Comparison and Contrast Chart** - A sorting tool that separates direct similarities, direct differences, weak inferred links, and synthesis possibilities (Ch 14)

**Contrast** - Identification of meaningful differences that may reveal conditional outcomes (Ch 13)

**Critical Thinking Chart** - A living table linking article analysis to comparisons, contrasts, evaluations, syntheses, and integrations (Ch 6, Ch 20)

**Evaluation** - Assessment of literature's importance and applicability to a specific outside topic or situation (Ch 15)

**Evaluation point** - A factor, subgroup, program, role, or characteristic used to test contextual relevance (Ch 15)

**Free writing** - Timed, unrestricted idea generation before judgment or organization, typically about 3-20 minutes (Ch 7)

**Generalizability** - An evidence-based prediction from a study population to an appropriately similar larger population (Ch 4)

**Integration** - A conditional explanation of how apparently conflicting findings can fit together without erasing their differences (Ch 19)

**Living outline** - A research plan that changes as evidence, relationships, gaps, and syntheses become clearer (Ch 8, Ch 21)

**Local source** - Situation-specific news, records, histories, broadcasts, opinions, or participant material used mainly for context (Ch 2, Ch 6)

**Mining the data** - Using related research to generate bounded questions or illuminate a target situation when direct evidence is scarce (Ch 4, Ch 6)

**Narrow-Strand Rule** - Focusing comparison or contrast on the smallest meaningful relationship that can be interpreted cleanly (Ch 14)

**Objective research** - A structured search for relevant parameters, competing findings, and evidence limits rather than support for a preferred answer (Ch 3)

**Paraphrase** - A faithful restatement in the reviewer's language that preserves source meaning and still requires citation (Ch 5)

**Peer-reviewed source** - Research evaluated by field reviewers for method, evidence, analysis, and conclusions before publication (Ch 2, Ch 6)

**Research gap** - A relevant point for which adequate research has not been found or conducted (Ch 2, Ch 8)

**Research question** - The defined question that sets the review's scope and determines what evidence it must explain (Ch 2)

**Selective minimalism** - Omitting detail that does not change the reader's judgment about integrity, relevance, or applicability (Ch 11)

**Synthesis** - Combining sound, compatible, and complementary findings into a more complete but bounded framework (Ch 17, Ch 18)

**Transferability** - A reader's reasoned decision to consider information from one environment in another, after comparing conditions (Ch 4)

**Wild card** - A relevant characteristic whose effect is unknown or insufficiently studied (Ch 15)
