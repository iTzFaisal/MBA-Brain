# Cheatsheet

## If/Then Gates

| If... | Then... | Smell to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| The question has too many groups, factors, or periods | Narrow it and add subquestions until thorough coverage is possible | Broad topic, shallow coverage |
| Sources agree too quickly | Search for denials, pros/cons, modifications, and alternate perspectives | Confirmation-only search |
| A source shares a keyword but not the population, method, outcome, or context | Park it or label the relationship weak; do not call it direct evidence | Theme matching treated as comparison |
| A study is relevant but methodologically weak | Use it as a bounded lead or perspective, not proof | All-or-nothing source verdict |
| Findings differ | Compare context, population, procedure, measure, timing, and limitations before choosing a winner | Forced agreement |
| Evidence is compatible | Synthesize the strands, retaining each group's boundary | Article-by-article summary |
| Literature must inform a local setting | Evaluate against the specific situation using subquestions and an evaluation point | Jump from research to recommendation |
| A claim crosses from author evidence to your inference | Put the citation at the boundary, then use a transition and cautious verb | One citation at paragraph end |

## Defaults And Thresholds

- Use free writing for about **3-20 minutes**, then cluster and converge; stop when further layers no longer improve scope.
- Prefer **paraphrase**. Quote only when exact wording is unusually precise or valuable; preserve qualifiers, population, and limitations either way.
- For self-critique, create distance: **two weeks** is preferred when possible; read no more than about **20 pages per sitting** for a long draft. A narrative critique activity is capped at **250 words**.
- For a substantial review, complete the review/revise cycle **at least twice** before final technical editing.
- Use **past tense** for completed studies and **present tense** only for a defined ongoing/generalizable conclusion.
- Peer review is a strong floor for academic sources, not a guarantee of relevance, integrity, or transfer. Timeliness has no universal cutoff; retain foundational sources when their role is clear.

## Six-Lens Decision Order

1. **Analysis**: Can the logic, question, design, sample, evidence, interpretation, and conclusion be trusted?
2. **Comparison**: What meaningful structural or substantive similarities recur?
3. **Contrast**: What meaningful differences may make the result conditional?
4. **Evaluation**: What does this mean for the exact target situation?
5. **Synthesis**: Which sound strands fit into one bounded framework?
6. **Integration**: Under what conditions can apparent contradictions coexist?

An article that fails the analysis gate should not carry equal weight downstream. Strong internal integrity does not guarantee external fit.

## Tells And Smells

- **"Everyone," "always," or "proves"** after a local or correlational study: check population and design limits.
- **A neat framework with no citations or unstudied groups**: the writer has filled gaps with prose.
- **A long article summary with no use judgment**: analysis is missing.
- **Evaluation saved only for the conclusion**: the logic bridge is delayed; place it after the relevant comparison.
- **A single-chain causal diagram for many possible factors**: replace it with a cluster-style if-then map and show intermediate links.
- **A quotation-heavy paragraph**: replace noise with controlled paraphrase and reviewer synthesis.
- **A polished conclusion with a new claim**: return it to the body or remove it.
- **Grammar edits before evidence and organization are sound**: diagnose and revise first.
