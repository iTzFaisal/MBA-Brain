# Chapter 6: Strategic Management: How Exceptional Managers Realize a Grand Design

## Core Idea
Strategic management aligns an organization's distinctive position, choices, resources, and execution. Strategy is not a list of ambitions: it creates a unique and valuable position, accepts trade-offs, and makes activities fit and reinforce one another.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Porter's strategic positioning principles**: Strategy creates a unique and valuable position, requires trade-offs in competing, and creates a fit among activities.
  - When to use: To test whether a proposed initiative is distinctive, coherent, and difficult to copy as a bundle.
- **Three levels of strategy**: Corporate asks what businesses to be in; business asks how to compete in an industry; functional asks how each function supports the business-level strategy.
- **Five-step strategic-management process plus feedback**:
  1. Establish mission, vision, and values.
  2. Assess current reality.
  3. Formulate corporate, business, and functional strategies.
  4. Execute the strategy.
  5. Exercise strategic control and correct course.
- **SWOT**: Strengths and weaknesses are internal; opportunities and threats are external.
- **VRIO**: Test a resource or capability for value, rarity, imitability, and organization.
- **Porter's five forces**: New entrants, supplier power, buyer power, substitutes, and rivalry.
- **Porter's four competitive strategies**: Cost leadership, differentiation, cost focus, and focused differentiation.
- **BCG matrix**: Evaluate business units by market growth and relative market share: stars, question marks, cash cows, and dogs.
- **Welch's strategy questions**: What does the playing field look like now? What has competition done? What have we done? What is around the corner? What is our winning move?

## Key Concepts
- **Strategic fit**: Reinforcement among activities that makes the whole system stronger.
- **Growth strategy**: Expand revenue or market share, often through innovation.
- **Stability strategy**: Make little or no significant change.
- **Defensive strategy**: Reduce organizational effort or scope.
- **Diversification**: Enter other businesses through related or unrelated expansion.
- **Strategic implementation**: Putting strategic plans into effect.
- **Execution**: Questioning, analysis, and follow-through that mesh strategy with reality and align people, strategy, and operations.

## Mental Models
- Ask "what will we not do?" before celebrating a strategic choice; trade-offs protect distinctiveness.
- Use SWOT to scan broadly, then use VRIO to test whether an internal strength can produce durable advantage.
- Treat execution as strategy, not an afterthought; people, strategy, and operations must move together.

## Anti-patterns
- **Activity accumulation**: Adding initiatives without a distinctive position or trade-off.
- **SWOT laundry list**: Listing factors without ranking, causal reasoning, or strategic implication.
- **Execution denial**: Assuming a good plan will survive weak ownership, incentives, capabilities, or controls.

## Worked Example
LaCroix had a distinctive brand and many flavors but was slow to change product formats, distribution, and growth initiatives while PepsiCo and Coca-Cola entered the sparkling-water market. It continued selling mostly multipacks, lacked single-serve cans for convenience stores, and relied more on warehouse distribution while competitors used direct-store-delivery systems and established cooler access. A strategic review would map the five forces, assess the brand and distribution through VRIO, identify threats in SWOT, select a clear competitive position, and align functional actions rather than defend the existing formula.

## Key Takeaways
1. Define the position and the trade-offs before choosing initiatives.
2. Use the five-step process as a feedback loop, not a once-a-year retreat.
3. Distinguish internal capability from external opportunity; both are needed for advantage.
4. Translate strategy into functional plans with owners, resources, measures, and controls.
