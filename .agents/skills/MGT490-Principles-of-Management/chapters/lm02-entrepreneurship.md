# Learning Module 2: Entrepreneurship

## Core Idea
Entrepreneurship is recognizing and exploiting opportunities to create a new enterprise; it is different from merely being self-employed. Entrepreneurs combine opportunity recognition, risk, creativity, resources, and disciplined execution, while social entrepreneurs apply those capabilities to improve society.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Sources of business ideas**: Personal passions and skills; a problem or frustration; an opportunity or unmet need; and customer complaints.
  - When to use: When screening ideas before investing substantial time or money.
- **Entrepreneur characteristics**: Risk propensity, generalized self-efficacy, need to achieve, need for autonomy, Big Five traits, tolerance for ambiguity, proactivity/work ethic, internal locus of control, innovative/creative ability, and positive intentions/attitudes.
- **Business-plan questions**: What business are we in? What is our vision and where are we going? What will we do to achieve the goals?
- **Legal structures**: Sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, and limited liability company (LLC).
- **Funding options**: Personal funds, family/friends, bank loans, venture capital, angel investors, and crowd investing.

## Key Concepts
- **Entrepreneur**: A person who recognizes an opportunity and accepts risk to create an enterprise.
- **Intrapreneur**: An employee who applies entrepreneurial behavior inside an existing organization.
- **Social entrepreneurship**: Improvising systems, devising approaches, seeing overlooked opportunities, and creating solutions that improve society.
- **Franchising**: A franchisee pays for the right to use a company's brand and package of materials/services, usually sharing profits.
- **Business model**: The logic for how a venture creates, delivers, and captures value.
- **Organizational design**: The structure and coordination mechanisms that make the venture's strategy executable.

## Mental Models
- Validate demand before quitting a job or scaling; a good idea without customers is not a business.
- Pair founder ambition with humility, complementary people, and basic accounting.
- Choose legal structure and financing for the venture's risk, control, liability, and growth needs.

## Anti-patterns
- **Founder certainty**: Treating passion as proof of customer demand.
- **Friends-and-family hiring**: Selecting loyalty over the skills, work ethic, and experience the venture needs.
- **Financial blindness**: Ignoring budgets, cash flow, balance sheets, and income statements until the venture is in distress.

## Worked Example
The text's startup advice can be applied as a five-step launch screen: clarify whether the motive is escape or a specific goal; build clients while still employed and test a free or low-cost offer; seek advice with humility; recruit complementary A-players rather than only friends; and learn enough accounting to manage a budget and read financial statements. Each step reduces a different failure risk.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start with an opportunity and evidence of demand, not only a personal identity.
2. Distinguish entrepreneurship, intrapreneurship, self-employment, and social entrepreneurship.
3. Match structure, financing, culture, and design to the strategy.
4. Common failure causes are weak planning, insufficient capital, poor management, and lack of customer interest.
