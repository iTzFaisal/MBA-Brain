# Chapter 16: Control Systems and Quality Management: Techniques for Enhancing Organizational Effectiveness

## Core Idea
Controlling means monitoring performance, comparing it with goals, and taking corrective action. Effective control combines standards, timely measurement, feedback, balanced metrics, employee involvement, quality improvement, and respect for privacy.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four-step control process**: Set standards; measure performance; compare performance with standards; take corrective action.
- **Three control types**: Feedforward controls act before work; concurrent controls operate during work; feedback controls evaluate completed work.
- **Management by exception**: Escalate significant deviations rather than forcing managers to inspect every normal result.
- **Balanced Scorecard**: Financial, customer, internal-business-process, and innovation-and-learning perspectives.
- **Strategy map**: Visualizes the causal path from learning and capability through internal processes and customer value to financial results.
- **Deming management and PDCA**: Aim quality at customer needs, improve the system rather than blame workers, use data, and Plan-Do-Check-Act.
- **Total Quality Management**: Top-management-led, organization-wide commitment to continuous improvement, training, and customer satisfaction; its core principles are people orientation and improvement orientation.
- **Quality tools**: Outsourcing, reduced cycle time, statistical process control, Six Sigma, ISO 9000, and ISO 14000.

## Key Concepts
- **Control standard**: Desired performance level for a goal.
- **Benchmarking**: Comparing performance with high-performing organizations or standards.
- **Quality control**: Managing production stages to minimize errors.
- **Quality assurance**: Systems and practices urging workers toward zero defects.
- **Statistical process control**: Periodic random sampling to check whether quality remains within an acceptable range.
- **Kaizen**: Small, continuous improvement involving everyone.
- **Six Sigma/DMAIC**: Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control to reduce defects and variation.
- **Functional versus dysfunctional turnover**: Underperformers leaving versus high performers leaving.

## Mental Models
- Control should produce learning and correction, not only surveillance or punishment.
- Financial outcomes are lagging indicators; customer, process, and learning measures often reveal problems earlier.
- Improve the system before blaming the worker; individual correction is appropriate only after system causes are considered.
- Use feedforward, concurrent, and feedback controls together when delay or failure is costly.

## Anti-patterns
- **Single-metric control**: Managing only sales, cost, or another measure that can be gamed.
- **Feedback-only control**: Waiting until damage occurs when earlier controls were possible.
- **Blame-the-worker quality**: Ignoring process design, training, equipment, and incentives.
- **TQM rhetoric**: Claiming continuous improvement without employee involvement, measurement, evidence, and a quality culture.
- **Opaque surveillance**: Tracking employees off the clock or without legitimate purpose, consent, and data safeguards.
- **Turnover blindness**: Treating all turnover as equally harmful.

## Worked Example
The U.S. shale industry expanded when oil prices were high and financing was cheap, but fracking costs near $60 per barrel made firms vulnerable when prices fell to about $26. The chapter proposes using AI and analytics to improve drilling locations, steer drill bits, optimize fractures, maintain equipment, and coordinate logistics. A balanced scorecard would combine financial viability, customer/stakeholder trust, internal operational efficiency and safety, and innovation/learning capability.

## Key Takeaways
1. Define a standard before measuring performance.
2. Use leading and lagging indicators across all four Balanced Scorecard perspectives.
3. Apply PDCA and evidence to improve systems continuously.
4. Empower employees and cross-functional teams to find quality problems.
5. Make AI and employee monitoring transparent, proportionate, secure, and human-supervised.
