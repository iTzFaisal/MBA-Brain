# Chapter 12: Motivating Employees: Achieving Superior Performance in the Workplace

## Core Idea
Motivation is the psychological process that arouses and directs goal-directed behavior. Managers should diagnose needs, perceptions of fairness, expectancy, job design, reinforcement, and context rather than assuming money or one universal theory will motivate everyone.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Simple motivation model**: Unfulfilled need -> motivation -> behavior -> reward -> feedback that satisfies or reshapes the need.
- **Four motivation perspectives**: Content, process, job design, and reinforcement.
- **Content theories**: Maslow's hierarchy; McClelland's acquired needs (achievement, affiliation, power); Deci and Ryan's self-determination (competence, autonomy, relatedness); Herzberg's two-factor theory (hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction, motivators create satisfaction).
- **Process theories**: Equity/justice compares inputs, outputs, and comparison targets; expectancy asks whether effort can produce performance and rewards; goal-setting uses specific, challenging, achievable goals linked to action plans.
- **Job Characteristics Model**: Skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy, and feedback affect meaningfulness, responsibility, knowledge of results, motivation, performance, and satisfaction.
- **Behavior modification**: Positive reinforcement, negative reinforcement, extinction, and punishment alter behavior through consequences.
- **Six-step self-management**: Identify a wildly important goal; break it into short-term goals; make a to-do list; prioritize; schedule; work, reward, and adjust.

## Key Concepts
- **Intrinsic reward**: Internal satisfaction from performing a task.
- **Extrinsic reward**: Payoff received from another person or entity.
- **Organizational justice**: Distributive, procedural, and interactional fairness.
- **Expectancy**: Belief that effort leads to performance.
- **Instrumentality**: Belief that performance leads to a reward.
- **Valence**: Value placed on a reward.
- **Job enlargement**: Horizontal loading through more tasks and variety.
- **Job enrichment**: Vertical loading through more responsibility.

## Mental Models
- When motivation is low, inspect ability, resources, goals, fairness, rewards, meaning, and context before blaming attitude.
- If expectancy, instrumentality, or valence is low, motivation will be low even when the other two are high.
- Use punishment sparingly and pair it with clear expectations, prompt feedback, and positive reinforcement.

## Anti-patterns
- **Pay-only motivation**: Assuming compensation alone creates satisfaction, commitment, or meaning.
- **Equity blindness**: Ignoring input/output comparisons, employee voice, procedures, or interpersonal treatment.
- **Vague goals**: Saying "do your best" without a specific, challenging, achievable target.
- **Negative-reinforcement confusion**: Treating removal of an unpleasant condition as punishment.
- **Reward mismatch**: Offering rewards employees do not value, delivering them late, or using unclear criteria.
- **Burnout optimization**: Pursuing output while ignoring recovery and health.

## Worked Example
During the COVID-19 pandemic, health-care workers faced extreme workload, infection risk, inadequate protective equipment, and burnout. Hospitals offered large bonuses, but workers also persisted because of task significance, patient relationships, professional identity, autonomy, and a sense of duty. Managers used open-ended check-ins to learn what people needed. The case shows why extrinsic rewards, safety, equity, self-determination, meaningful work, and stress management must be considered together.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diagnose the motivational mechanism before choosing an intervention.
2. Make performance-reward links credible and rewards personally meaningful.
3. Set challenging goals with resources, participation, action plans, and feedback.
4. Design jobs for variety, identity, significance, autonomy, and feedback.
5. Combine compensation with growth, flexibility, positive climate, meaning, and recovery.
