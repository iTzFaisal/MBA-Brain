# Chapter 9: Human Resource Management: Getting the Right People for Managerial Success

## Core Idea
Strategic HRM designs systems that align human and social capital with strategic objectives. Recruitment, selection, compensation, onboarding, development, performance management, employee relations, and legal compliance must work as an integrated system.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Strategic HRM fit**: Internal fit means HR practices reinforce one another; external fit means HR aligns with culture, structure, and strategy.
- **Talent management and high-performance work systems**: Approaches for attracting, developing, motivating, and retaining high performers.
- **Recruitment options**: Internal, external, and hybrid recruiting, including employee referrals and boomerang hires.
- **Selection tools**: Background information, interviews, and employment tests. Interviews may be unstructured, structured situational, or structured behavioral-description.
  - When to use: Use standardized, job-related, reliable, and valid tools when comparing candidates.
- **Learning and development process**: Assessment, objectives, selection, implementation, evaluation.
- **Performance management**: Define performance, monitor/evaluate, review, provide consequences.
- **Compensation**: Base wages/salaries, incentives, and benefits.
- **Performance feedback**: Identify personal tendencies, listen, use self-compassion instead of defensiveness, ask for feedback, and practice mindfulness.

## Key Concepts
- **Human capital**: Productive potential of employee knowledge, experience, and competencies.
- **Social capital**: Productive potential of trusting, cooperative relationships.
- **Onboarding**: Integrating newcomers into policies, culture, politics, expectations, and responsibilities.
- **Performance appraisal**: Assessing performance and providing feedback; appraisals may be objective or subjective.
- **360-degree feedback**: Feedback from managers, peers, subordinates, customers, and sometimes the employee.
- **Labor relations**: Collective bargaining and other relationships between employees/unions and management.
- **Legal defensibility**: Selection and employment practices are job-related, reliable, valid, documented, and nondiscriminatory.

## Mental Models
- Treat HR as a system: changing selection without changing onboarding, development, rewards, and performance measures creates misfit.
- Define performance as results plus behaviors; results without ethical or collaborative behavior are incomplete.
- Give feedback as a recurring developmental conversation, not an annual verdict.

## Anti-patterns
- **Administrative HR**: Treating people practices as paperwork disconnected from strategy.
- **Unstructured hiring**: Letting charisma, credentials, or similarity substitute for job-related evidence.
- **Training theater**: Providing courses without needs assessment, objectives, implementation support, or evaluation.
- **Annual-review dependence**: Saving useful feedback for a single yearly meeting.
- **Favoritism**: Making promotions, discipline, or dismissal decisions without consistent evidence and process.

## Worked Example
T-Mobile reorganized HR around its customer-service strategy by creating Employee Success Partners, Talent Scouts, and Career Agents, expanding training and coaching, and placing customer-service representatives in empowered teams with complementary skills. The case links external fit to the customer strategy and internal fit among selection, development, empowerment, and performance practices. The reported improvements included lower turnover and absenteeism, fewer service calls per account, and better customer outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
1. Align HR practices with both the organization strategy and with one another.
2. Validate selection tools and standardize comparisons.
3. Use onboarding, learning, and development to build capabilities, not just compliance.
4. Make performance expectations, evidence, feedback, and consequences explicit.
5. Consult current HR/legal specialists for jurisdiction-specific employment decisions.
