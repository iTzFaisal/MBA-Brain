# Chapter 15: Interpersonal and Organizational Communication: Mastering the Exchange of Information

## Core Idea
Communication is the transfer of information and understanding, not merely transmission. Managers must choose an appropriate medium, reduce noise, create feedback, adapt to culture, manage formal and informal channels, and communicate truthfully through social media.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Communication process**: Sender encodes a message, selects a medium, the receiver decodes it, and feedback checks understanding; physical, psychological, semantic, or physiological noise may disrupt any stage.
- **Media richness**: Rich media, such as face-to-face interaction and video, suit ambiguous, emotional, and nonroutine work; lean written media suit routine, clear information.
- **Formal channels**: Vertical communication flows up and down; horizontal communication coordinates units; external communication crosses the organizational boundary.
- **Informal channels**: Grapevine and face-to-face communication develop outside the formal chain of command.
- **Three empathy types**: Cognitive, emotional, and compassionate empathy.
- **Active listening**: Focus on the other person, ask open-ended questions, be curious, avoid judgment, and remain fully present.
- **Nondefensive communication**: Replace evaluation with description, control with problem solving, strategy with straightforwardness, neutrality with empathy, superiority with equality, and certainty with openness.
- **Networking**: Positive mindset, career goals, purposeful networking, personal connections, mindfulness, and follow-up.

## Key Concepts
- **Encoding/decoding**: Translating a message into symbols and interpreting it.
- **Feedback**: The receiver's reaction to the sender's message.
- **Paraphrasing**: Restating a message to verify understanding.
- **Grapevine**: Unofficial system of gossip and rumor.
- **Jargon**: Specialized terminology that may block understanding.
- **Misinformation**: False information not intended to harm.
- **Disinformation**: Deliberately misleading false information.
- **Fake news**: False information intentionally published as authentic news.
- **Cyberloafing/phubbing/FOMO**: Personal Internet use, phone snubbing, and fear of missing out.

## Mental Models
- Choose the medium for ambiguity and feedback needs, not personal convenience.
- An information vacuum will be filled by the grapevine; transparency is a control mechanism.
- Verify understanding as a two-way process through questions and paraphrase.
- Use social media for real business purposes while treating privacy, security, and credibility as constraints.

## Anti-patterns
- **Lean-media mismatch**: Sending complex or emotionally charged change through a short, context-poor message.
- **No feedback**: Assuming receipt means understanding.
- **Jargon performance**: Using impressive terms instead of clear language.
- **Phone presence**: Multitasking, phubbing, or mindlessness during important conversations.
- **Unverified repetition**: Treating a repeated claim as true.
- **Marketing before operations**: Promising a customer experience before people, processes, vendors, and resources exist.

## Worked Example
Fyre Festival used influencers and polished social media to sell a luxury event before securing a realistic venue, budget, vendors, transportation, security, food, or performers. Warnings from production professionals and vendors were ignored, stakeholders received inconsistent answers, and attendees lacked a reliable communication channel. When the event failed, organizers initially blamed weather and infrastructure online. The case demonstrates communication, planning, ethical, credibility, and feedback failures.

## Key Takeaways
1. Match medium richness to ambiguity, emotion, and need for immediate feedback.
2. Use active listening and paraphrase before evaluating or responding.
3. Keep formal channels clear and address grapevine information quickly.
4. Write simply, directly, and for the audience; present with "tell them, say it, tell them."
5. Make social media policies fair, transparent, professional, and privacy-aware.
