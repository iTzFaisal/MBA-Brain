## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. Run the POLC loop
Management pursues organizational goals efficiently and effectively by integrating people and resources through **planning, organizing, leading, and controlling**. Plan the result and method, organize tasks and resources, lead through motivation and influence, then control by comparing evidence with goals and correcting the plan or execution. The four functions operate simultaneously. Efficiency is the means; effectiveness is the end.

Use Mintzberg's roles to inspect the work behind a title: figurehead, leader, liaison; monitor, disseminator, spokesperson; entrepreneur, disturbance handler, resource allocator, negotiator. Match the intervention to the level: top direction, middle implementation, first-line execution, or team facilitation.

### 2. Choose the management lens that fits the problem
Classical management improves methods and structure; behavioral management explains needs, relationships, and motivation; quantitative management uses operations and evidence; systems management traces inputs, transformations, outputs, and feedback; contingency management asks what fits this person, task, and environment; contemporary management builds learning organizations, high-performance work practices, and shared value. Do not optimize one part while ignoring the system or assume one best way.

### 3. Align responsibility, strategy, and execution
The triple bottom line tests people, planet, and profit. Map internal, task-environment, and general-environment stakeholders, then test difficult choices through utilitarian, individual, moral-rights, and justice lenses. Strategic positioning creates a unique and valuable position, accepts trade-offs, and makes activities fit. Use SWOT for internal/external diagnosis, VRIO for resource advantage, Porter's five forces for industry pressure, and the five-step strategy process: mission/vision/values, current reality, formulation, execution, and strategic control.

Creating shared value improves CSR by connecting societal needs to products, value chains, and local clusters. Look for new markets, value-chain transformation, and supportive ecosystems that create both social and economic value.

### 4. Convert intent into coordinated work
Planning copes with uncertainty. Mission states why, vision states what the organization wants to become, and values state what it stands for. Cascade strategic goals into tactical and operational goals; make them SMART: specific, measurable, attainable, results-oriented, and time-bound. MBO jointly sets objectives, creates action plans, reviews performance, and rewards results. Use flowcharts for sequence, Gantt charts for time, and break-even analysis for cost/volume scenarios.

### 5. Lead people as individuals, teams, and stakeholders
Strategic HRM aligns human and social capital with strategy through recruitment, validated selection, onboarding, learning, compensation, performance management, and employee relations. Diagnose motivation through needs, equity, expectancy/instrumentality/valence, job characteristics, and reinforcement. Build teams through purpose, trust, goals, feedback, accountability, composition, roles, norms, and processes; use Tuckman's forming, storming, norming, performing, and adjourning model, while expecting punctuated equilibrium after disruption.

Leadership uses legitimate, reward, coercive, expert, referent, and informational power. Prefer rational persuasion, consultation, collaboration, and inspiration. Match task/relationship behavior to the situation; combine transactional clarity with transformational purpose, humility, empowerment, servant leadership, and ethical modeling.

### 6. Use evidence, feedback, and learning
For consequential decisions, identify the problem/opportunity, generate alternatives, evaluate cost/quality/ethics/feasibility/effectiveness, implement, and evaluate. Counter availability, representativeness, confirmation, sunk-cost, anchoring, overconfidence, hindsight, framing, escalation, and categorical-thinking biases. Use evidence-based management, data analytics, AI with human oversight, minority dissent, devil's advocacy, dialectic debate, and post-mortems.

Communication transfers information and understanding. Match rich media to ambiguity and emotion, reduce noise, listen actively, paraphrase, and address the grapevine quickly. Control uses standards, measurement, comparison, and corrective action; combine feedforward, concurrent, and feedback controls with the Balanced Scorecard and PDCA/TQM. Improve systems before blaming workers, and treat privacy and ethics as control constraints.
